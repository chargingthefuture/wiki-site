A job title, and the status that comes with it, says nothing about the person holding it ([A badge is a job title](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/a-badge-is-a-job-title)).

So the list is by skill, never by title or status. The Skills Economy records what people can do, not the job title they last held ([Counted by skills, not job titles](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/counted-by-skills)). Nobody needs a current job, or any job, to be on it.

Alicia Macchione's listing is the work of a shop, written down one skill at a time.

## Alicia Macchione

Alicia Macchione (https://www.quora.com/profile/Alicia-Macchione-4), in Florida. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/a1604cf2-46b6-4dcf-95d0-a7e9c38fa637.

The listing carries five specializations:

- Customer service, and client service skills: looking after the person in front of you, and giving them a reason to come back.
- Point-of-sale operation: running the till, from ringing up a sale to balancing it at the end of a shift.
- Inventory control: knowing what is in stock, what is running low, and what has gone missing.
- Stock replenishment: getting the shelves filled before they run empty.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on October 14, the Directory's 321 people held 260 of them.

All five of Alicia's skills are filed in Retail and Services: customer service, point-of-sale operation and stock replenishment under shop staff and cashiers, client service under personal services, and inventory control under supply managers. Retail and Services holds 14 of 15, the nearest any sector on the list comes to full.

A title would put Alicia in one of those boxes. The skills put them under three job titles at once, and none of the three depends on whether they hold any of those jobs today. With 14 of 15 held, somebody on the list can do nearly every part of running a shop. A second person who can is a second person to ask.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what Alicia said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it carries my handle as the person who nominated it: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs: what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work is looking after customers needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The listing that makes them findable makes those people findable to them.
