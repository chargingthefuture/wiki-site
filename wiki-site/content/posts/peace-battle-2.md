A protest starts on Friday, September 18, 2026 at 7:00 PM Eastern.

It is called Peace Battle 2. The page with the countdown and everything you need to take part is here: [chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/peace-battle-2](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/peace-battle-2). The short form is [/pb2](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/pb2), which goes to the same place.

There is no address to travel to. That is the design, not a compromise.

## Why it has a name

In 1903 W. E. B. Du Bois looked back at what the people who founded the colleges had done after the Civil War. Two thousand were trained. Those two thousand trained fifty thousand. Those fifty thousand taught nine millions. He called it the most wonderful peace-battle of the 19th century.

A battle fought with teaching and with work. And it was won.

Movements get names. The Civil Rights Movement had one. What is happening to Targeted Individuals has been going on without one, which makes it hard to point at and easy to wave off. So it gets a name, and the name says which lineage it belongs to.

The Skills Economy has been this since it started. It just did not have the label.

I am organizing it. I am not leading it. Survivors are a distributed group and nobody speaks for them, including me. Somebody has to call a date, so I am calling one.

## A thing I said that was wrong

I used to say their methods were ingenious. I said it in conversation more than once.

That was callous, and it was also just inaccurate.

There is nothing clever about it. Look at the schemes one at a time and most of them are obvious to anyone standing nearby. People circle a person. Drama follows them from place to place. A stranger repeats something only a person's own household would know.

Somebody watching once can tell themselves there is another explanation. Somebody watching the same person for a year cannot. What they learn instead is that the plausible deniability is not really for them — it is for a courtroom. The behavior is arranged to sit in the gap where the law has nothing to charge.

That is not genius. It is criminals adapting faster than the legal framework, which is the same thing that happens everywhere else. Laws arrived late for social media. They arrived late for data privacy. They are arriving late here too, and human trafficking is harder than either, because the people running it keep changing shape while the statutes stay still.

Calling that ingenious hands them a compliment they did not earn. I am not saying it again.

And some people genuinely believe what they are told about a survivor. That is real and I am not pretending otherwise.

## Why it is a protest, and why it is virtual

A protest is normally bodies in one place. Targeted Individuals have tried that. People have announced a protest and stood there by themselves.

It is not apathy. A protest in a place assumes three things: neighbors who are also affected, money to travel, and a day you can afford to lose. Most survivors have none of the three, because being made destitute is part of what is done to them.

And the spread is deliberate. Targeting does not concentrate in one neighborhood, one race, one class, or one religion. It is scattered across all of them, everywhere. That keeps the people it happens to from finding each other, and it lets anyone describe them as unrelated individuals with unrelated problems.

You can see it in what gets said about any one person. In my case the story changes with whoever is being recruited: terrorist for one audience, racist for another, prostitute for another, violent, impatient, hates Black men, hates Mexicans, hates dogs. The lie is chosen for the listener. It is not meant to be consistent. It is meant to divide.

So this protest is distributed too, because what it answers is distributed. It runs for years, which a protest in a physical place cannot do. It costs nothing but the minutes you give it. And it is the first form of this that a person who is broke, isolated, or too tired to leave the house can actually join.

## Three ways to take part

Three, not ten. More than three and a protest turns into homework.

### One: say something under a post

Read any post on this blog and leave a comment or a reaction under it. Reading the conversation needs no account at all. Writing takes a free one.

That is Fireside: [app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/fireside](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/fireside).

This is the one that answers Quora directly. TrustTransport is the Uber for Targeted Individuals. Fireside is the Quora for Targeted Individuals.

More than twenty times an account of this project has been erased on Quora, and every comment under it went with it — mine and other people's. If you replied to me there, your words are gone and you did nothing wrong. The dated record is on [old links, new links](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/old-links-new-links).

You are already writing for free on a platform that sells what you write and deletes it whenever it likes. Writing here costs you the same nothing and the words stay findable.

### Two: take a slot on TI Radio

A published week of live audio rooms, at [app.chargingthefuture.com/ti-radio](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/ti-radio). Take an open time, write a line about what it is about, and turn up.

Low pressure on purpose. Pick a time every week, sit in the room, and talk to whoever arrives. It does not have to be about being targeted. It can be about anything.

The aim is a schedule with somebody in it as often as possible. The rooms themselves run through [Chyme](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/chyme). Listening needs no account; speaking does. A slot is taken, not granted, so a name on the schedule is not an endorsement from this project.

### Three: post your 1%

Open [What's your 1%?](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/workforce?view=one-percent). It takes one percent of five million survivors — fifty thousand people — and runs the arithmetic backwards to you, weighted to your own trade.

Adjust the figures to what you would actually charge. Screenshot it. Put it where people will see it.

It costs a minute and it argues with nothing but arithmetic.

If you would rather act than post, do the other version: use the app to offer or ask for something real. Then the figure stops being a projection.

## If you think none of this will work

Take part anyway, and here is the honest reason.

Nothing in it harms anybody. The Skills Economy does not ask you to fight, to confront, or to give anything up. It says: you were going to take that ride anyway, so check first whether another survivor can drive it.

Five million people leaving the global economy would not dent it. Not a little — not at all. And the people doing this already work to keep survivors from earning, which tells you they do not want that participation either. What they do not want is being named.

So the cost of being wrong about this is a minute of your time. The cost of being right and doing nothing is another generation.

## What winning looks like

Peace Battle 2 is finished when a survivor can get 99% of what they need — housing, transportation, goods, services — from other survivors.

Not 100%. A phone and a vehicle need supply chains no community this size can run, and saying otherwise would be a lie. Nearly everything else can be grown, made, taught, driven, repaired, or arranged by somebody already on the list.

That is what exiting the psyop means in practice. Not leaving the global economy. Being able to come and go from it by choice, because what you need is available from people who are not part of what is being done to you.

The nearer marker is 384 approved members working with each other at any given time.

You can see the far end of it as a game: [Peace-Battle 2 in the arcade](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/offline-os/apps/peace-battle-2/) starts from the 147 people on the Directory today and asks you to fill a catalog of 657 skills inside two generations. It is free, needs no account, and runs offline once it opens.

One generation is what I would like. Two is what I expect, given where we are starting.
