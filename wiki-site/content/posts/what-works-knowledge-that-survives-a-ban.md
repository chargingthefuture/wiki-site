Someone in the Skills Economy space wrote the single most useful kind of post a survivor can write. Not a theory about who is doing this. A report on what they bought, what they tried, and what happened.

## What they wrote

Gn0b0dy Pneuma (https://www.quora.com/profile/Gn0b0dy-Pneuma) posted about shielding fabric and faraday cages, from having bought and used them.

![The first part of a post by Gn0b0dy Pneuma in the Skills Economy space on Quora, reading: I've bought and have alot of experience with shielding fabric and faraday cages. I just want to add some info and advice for people trying to look into this to help protect themselves from various rf or microwave technologies. There are a few very important things many people neglect or do not know about: Grounding can be very important, a simple and cheap grounding wire you plug into an outlet has helped me out a lot in situations. It is very important to know whether or not your skin should be touching the shielding fabric/metal, it depends if you are trying to absorb/reflect the signals or just change your own resonant frequency, so it is important to try to determine the frequencies being used.](images/gn0b0dy-pneuma-shielding-2026-08-23-1.png)

![The rest of the same post by Gn0b0dy Pneuma, reading: Your faraday cage can have small gaps if it is a somewhat lower frequency (similar to a microwave oven window), but if the faraday cage doesn't comepletely surround you it will greatly reduce its effectiveness. Different materials have very different effective reflecting/absorbing frequency ranges. Lookup an be aware of electromagnetic skin effect and skin depth. I've had faraday materials be very effective at first and then quickly become ineffective for some reason, almost like they are somehow calibrating the signal to counter my recent success or something. I've even spent hundreds of dollars on a full body emf body suit with tons of silver in it, it ended up not working very well and it also didn't last long because the silver didn't stay in the fabric, although I did buy it from aliexpress.](images/gn0b0dy-pneuma-shielding-2026-08-23-2.png)

Read what is actually in that. Grounding helped, and the wire is cheap. Whether your skin should touch the material depends on what you are trying to do, which means you need to know the frequency first. A gap in a cage matters differently at different frequencies. Materials differ in what range they work across. Two terms to go read about: electromagnetic skin effect, and skin depth.

And then the part most people would leave out: several hundred dollars on a full-body suit with silver in it that did not work well and did not last, because the silver came out of the fabric.

That is years of somebody's money and attention, written down for strangers.

## Why I asked for links

I replied asking for links to the specific items, so people can buy them or research them.

That is not a detail. "Shielding fabric" is a category with an enormous range of quality inside it, and the difference between a product that does something and one that does nothing is not visible in a description. A person reading that post and going shopping is starting the same expensive trial and error over again. A link ends it.

The other half of the reason is the platform. Quora has erased seven of my accounts. When an account goes, everything on it goes — every answer, every comment, every link anyone saved. Content from Targeted Individuals (TIs) gets removed constantly, and the effect is that we cannot hand each other anything durable. Work like that post gets done, and then it gets deleted, and then somebody else does it again from the start.

## The comments go with the post

This is the part that took me a while to understand, and it changes where it makes sense to write things.

When one of my posts is deleted, the comments underneath it are deleted too. Not only my words — everyone's. Somebody who took twenty minutes to write out what they know, in a reply, loses it because of an account decision that had nothing to do with them.

So a comment on my post is the least durable place a person can put something useful. It is attached to an account that has already been erased seven times, and it goes down with that account without warning.

Which is why the discussion belongs in Commons, the group chat in the app. Nothing there is removed by a platform decision, so an answer written in it is still there next month.

That is a claim about durability and nothing else. I am not telling you Commons is private, and I would not tell anyone that about any channel. What I am telling you is that it does not disappear.

## What WhatWorks is

One shared list, organized by the problem rather than by the person or the date.

You pick a problem you are facing. Under it is a list of specific tools somebody here actually bought, used, and said helped, each with a direct link to get it. Anyone can add a problem, and anyone can add a tool under one.

Right now it holds three problems and two tools. Both tools sit under Sleep Deprivation, which the list describes as loud noises meant to disrupt your sleep. One is a pair of AirPods Pro 2, listed for having the best active noise cancellation, with a note that they work with Android but sound best with Apple hardware. The other is generic in-ear headphones, listed because they are worth the price and cheap to replace when they get stolen.

The other two problems on it — shaking and vibrating, and voice to skull — have nothing under them yet. The list says so plainly on the screen rather than hiding the empty ones.

## What survivor-verified means, and what it does not

The list marks its entries survivor-verified, and that phrase is narrower than it sounds. It means a specific person says they bought this, used it, and it helped them.

It does not mean the tool works. It does not mean it will work for you. It is one person's account with their name attached to it, which is a different and smaller claim than a review site makes, and it is the only claim any of us can honestly make.

The reason it is worth anything is that the alternative is worse. The alternative is a search engine, a comments section, and a product page written by whoever wants your money.

## The failures belong on it too

The most valuable line in that post is the one about the silver suit that did not work.

Somebody reading it has just saved several hundred dollars. That is a larger and more certain benefit than most positive recommendations deliver, and it is the part that almost never gets written down, because saying you wasted money is harder than saying you found something good.

A list of what works needs the other column. I would rather it held both than only ever collected wins.
