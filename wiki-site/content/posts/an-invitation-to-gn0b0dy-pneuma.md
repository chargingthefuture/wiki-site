Most of the people I have invited so far were strangers to this blog until their post went up. This one is different. Gn0b0dy Pneuma's writing is already here three times, credited each time, and I have quoted it because it was better than mine.

## Gn0b0dy Pneuma

Gn0b0dy Pneuma (https://www.quora.com/profile/Gn0b0dy-Pneuma), in Lexington, Kentucky. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/93fcd868-10df-4d3f-ae3d-6bdf8141b83b and it carries one label, Advocacy, which is the placeholder the list uses when somebody's public writing shows they speak up for Targeted Individuals and no trade is stated anywhere. It stands in for a skill that is not known yet. It does not say they are an advocate by trade, and neither does this post.

What the record does show is more specific than a label.

They [posted about shielding fabric and faraday cages](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/what-works-knowledge-that-survives-a-ban) from having bought and used them: grounding, whether skin should touch the fabric, working out which frequencies are in play, and the several hundred dollars of silver body suit that did not work. I wrote at the time that it was the most useful thing one survivor can hand another — specific, first-hand, and including the failures — and that it held [more practical knowledge on that subject than everything I have ever written about it put together](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/knowledge-library-one-persons-writing). I could not have produced it.

They also [wrote about the claim that operators can see through your eyes and read your mind](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/pizza-is-not-my-favorite-food): survivors repeat it, the people saying it to them are the ones lying, and it works the way a stage trick works — say what the person expects and let them do the rest. They closed on Sun Tzu. That is somebody reading their situation rather than reciting it.

## You do not have to agree with me

I want to be plain about this, because it is the reason for writing to them in particular.

My account of what this is runs one way. [Coexistence over conspiracy](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/how-are-you-doing): I think it is a great many ordinary people in a distributed network of organized crime, and I have stopped spending my life on which organization it is because there is no single door to knock on. The Dictionary carries the [vocabulary](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/Dictionary) I use for it.

Their writing puts its weight elsewhere: on the equipment, the frequencies, what fabric and grounding do and do not stop. A reader could hold that the two accounts sit at different ends of a room.

The list does not need us to agree. It never has. A Directory entry says what a person can do and what they need. It does not ask what they believe is being done to them, or by whom, and nothing about claiming one asks them to sign my version. The people on the list are doing rather than saying, and that counts for more than a stated position does — mine included.

Somebody who bought the fabric, built the cages, measured what happened and wrote down what failed is doing. That is the entry.

## How they came to be listed

Gn0b0dy Pneuma did not put themselves in the Directory. I did, after reading what they wrote in public, and the listing is marked community-generated and names me as the person who made it, so that nobody has to guess.

That is how most of the list was built, one person at a time. The Directory is the thing survivors ask for more than anything else: a findable list of people and what they can do. Building it by waiting for people to sign up for another app would have meant building nothing.

## The invitation

The listing is under your name, and the one label on it is a placeholder. What goes in its place is yours to write — the shielding work belongs there in your own words, and so does anything else you can do that the record has not shown me.

Claiming it is the step that makes it yours. It takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that the entry is under your control rather than mine: you set what it says, and you can take it down.

Nothing else is required. You do not have to use the rest of the app, you do not have to post anything, and you do not have to agree with a word I have written about why any of this is happening. That last part is not a courtesy. It is how the list works.

If the answer is no, that is a complete answer and nothing about the listing changes. It can come down at any time, with no account involved anywhere in that. Nobody who has been asked has wanted theirs removed, and the offer has been real since the day the list went up.

## This does not run one way

An invitation that only asks is not worth much to somebody already carrying a full week.

Somebody who has worked out shielding the hard way needs things too: a ride that does not go through somebody who should not be driving, a room, a repair, an hour of a trade they do not have. Those are on the list under other people's names, and the same listing that makes them findable is what makes those findable to them.

And the writing itself has somewhere to live that a ban cannot reach. WhatWorks is one shared list organized by the problem a person is facing, and shielding is on it. The Knowledge Library answers questions from the writing of people who have lived this, and I asked once already whether they would lend theirs. The ask stands, and it does not depend on the answer to anything above.
