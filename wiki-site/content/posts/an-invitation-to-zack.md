A working economy needs people who know where things go. Waste leaves a building and ends up somewhere. Water comes in and has to go back out clean. A place people live in for years has land around it, and that land changes. None of it is anybody's first thought about building something together, and all of it decides whether the thing lasts.

## Zack

Zack Tom (https://www.quora.com/profile/Zack-Tom-4), in Denmark. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/068816c1-d096-4020-bd79-fca07e9db706.

Five of the specializations are environmental work, and they fit together:

- Biodiversity monitoring and habitat assessment is finding out what is on a piece of land and what state it is in.
- Pollution control system design is building the thing that keeps a site from fouling what is around it.
- Waste-flow modeling and policy evaluation is working out where waste actually goes, and whether a rule about it does what it says.
- Enforcement actions and corrective plans is what happens when something has gone wrong: what has to change, and in what order.
- Community outreach and stewardship programs is getting the people who live somewhere to look after it themselves.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on September 28, the Directory's 163 people held 226 of them.

Two sectors are close to empty. Environmental and Waste Management: 6 of its 25 skills are held by anybody on the list. Water and Sanitation: 3 of 19.

Most plans people make here assume those two are covered. A room, a shared house, a community somebody could live in for years: each one produces waste and needs clean water from the first day, and nobody notices the work until it stops being done.

## What this is not

This is not a request to test anybody's home, soil or water, and nothing here says Zack will. Being on a list of what a person can do is not an offer of anything, and the Directory has never worked that way.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what Zack said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs — what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work is looking after a place needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The same listing that makes them findable is what makes those people findable to them.
