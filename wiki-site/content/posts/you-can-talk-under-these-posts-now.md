There is now a conversation under every post on this blog. It is called Fireside, and it sits at the bottom of every one of them.

## Reading it takes nothing

No account. No sign-in. No box asking you to create something before you are allowed to see what people said.

That is deliberate, and it is backwards from where most of you read me. Quora will not show you a comment thread without a login. Fireside does the reverse: anybody who can read the post can read the conversation under it, whether they have ever heard of this app or not.

## Writing takes an account, and here is exactly what happens

You sign in, you write, and your comment saves. It is not publicly visible yet. It becomes visible when you are approved, and the screen tells you that at the moment you press post — not afterwards, and not by leaving you to work out why nothing appeared.

Approval is per person, not per comment. When somebody is approved, everything they have written here becomes visible at once, including what they wrote before. So the waiting is not wasted and you are not writing into a hole.

What approval involves is one thing: a Quora profile link. A person reads it. That is the same look this app does before anyone is approved, and writing a comment is a reasonable place to start it, because judging what somebody wrote is the same judgment.

Three reactions rather than an open set of emoji — I recognize this, This helped, Same here — so a count means the same thing on every comment. Replies go one level deep and stop there, because anything deeper is unreadable on a phone, and a phone is the only width this app has.

## Why this exists

Quora has erased more than twenty of my accounts. Each time, everything posted from that account went with it, and so did every comment under it — mine and everybody else's. Conversations I had with people over two years are gone from where they happened. If you replied to me there, your words are gone too, and you did nothing wrong.

Two other things are true about comments on that platform, and they are quieter problems. You cannot search them. You cannot bookmark one. A good answer somebody wrote three months ago is functionally lost even while the account is alive.

A comment under this post has an address. It is stored on a system this project runs, it survives any platform deciding anything, and it can be found again.

## What you control, and what nobody else does

Take your own comment down at any time, with no admin involved and no reason required. The words go. The row stays only so a reply underneath it does not lose its place, and it is shown to nobody.

Fireside carries the same deletion rights as the group chat, from the same account screen. If you delete your account, your comments and reactions go with it.

There is one exception and it is worth reading twice. A comment can be copied into this blog's own published pages — searchable, and captured by web archives. That takes two separate yeses: you ask for it, and an admin agrees. It is off unless you turn it on, nobody else can turn it on for you, and you can turn it back off right up until the copy is made. The reason it needs both is that a web capture cannot be recalled afterwards by anybody, this project included, and a page that is permanent should not be one person's mistake or one account's bad faith.

## Nothing is under any post yet

This went up today, so every thread on the blog is empty. Somebody has to be first, and it may as well be about whichever post you actually have something to say about.

There are few rules here on purpose. The group chat's guidelines apply, and beyond them not much, because a conversation about what was done to people and what to do next does not survive being tidied into a format.
