Quora deleted my account for the third time. Two years of connections and content went with it — not just my posts, but every thread, every comment, every person who found me through one of them. Nothing I did wrong triggered it, and no warning preceded it. The account can go at any time, and everything reachable through it goes with it.

This page did not go anywhere, and that is the point of it.

## What changed on my end

My content strategy is now simple: everything gets published on the blog first. Platforms get an excerpt and a link back. Nothing of mine is authored on a platform anymore, so a deletion costs me that account's reach and nothing else — no posts lost, no history erased, no starting over.

If you want to find me at any point, one page always carries my current handles: [old links, new links](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/old-links-new-links). It is kept current, and every change to it is on the public record. Bookmark that page rather than any handle, because handles are the part that keeps dying.

## The part that hurts most is not the posts

I can rewrite a post. What I cannot rewrite is two years of people knowing who I am.

The proof that other survivors vouched for this work is gone — [ten people who publicly backed the Skills Economy](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/honoring-the-earliest-supporters) had their comments deleted along with the accounts those comments lived on. Someone arriving today cannot see any of it. That is the real damage of being deplatformed: not the writing, but the standing you built and the people who could confirm it.

Many of you are dealing with exactly this. The answer is not a better platform. It is not keeping your head down so the next account survives. The answer is having somewhere your record does not depend on a company's decision.

## What I suggest

Join the Skills Economy and create a Directory profile.

It takes a couple of minutes, and what it gives you is a place where your skills are stated in your own words, where the work you do for other survivors is recorded, and where people can find you by what you can do — none of which disappears when an account does. Your contributions stay yours. Your standing in the community stays yours. If a platform deletes you tomorrow, the people here still know who you are and still know how to reach you.

That is the whole point of building this where a single account cannot take it down. Exit their economy, exit the psyop.
