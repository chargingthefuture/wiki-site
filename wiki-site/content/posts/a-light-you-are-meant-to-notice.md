A question I saved on Quora in October 2024 and never answered, as its title was recovered from the address it was saved at:

> Why do gang stalkers use bright lights from cars to harass me

The address it was saved at is https://www.quora.com/Why-do-gang-stalkers-use-bright-lights-from-cars-to-harass-me. The [Dictionary](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/Dictionary) carries the words I use for this; the question keeps the words it was asked in.

## Meant to be seen

The lights are a message, and the message is that you are being watched. When somebody asked whether the cars match the car the target drives, I answered on Quora on November 13, 2025: "Yes. In the same way, they will wear the same clothes as you and repeat parts of your private conversations or online searches." In the same answer: "They want you to know that they are stalking you 24/7." Bright lights in your mirror belong with those. They are not hidden, and they are not meant to be.

The list survivors keep of named schemes has one for it. Road Sensitization: high beams flashed in your mirror, brake checks, cars pacing you or boxing you in, repeated until every drive is something you have to read and second-guess. [Same car, same color](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/same-car-same-color) answers the question about matching cars and sets that scheme beside Color Sensitization. This post takes the lights on their own.

## Repetition, and a reaction

Sensitization works by repetition: take a thing you have been seen with often, attach it to a bad moment, and repeat until the thing itself carries the moment with it ([Pizza is not my favorite food](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/pizza-is-not-my-favorite-food)). Applied to the road, the repetition is meant to make every drive something you have to read.

It is also waiting for a reaction. You are provoked until you react like anyone would, and the reaction is what gets written down ([The reaction is what gets written down](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/the-reaction-is-what-gets-written-down)). Of the matching cars, I wrote in that November 2025 answer that it "is intended to drive you crazy." The standing verdict that you are mentally ill or dangerous is built out of reactions.

## Not all that happens on the road

Some of what happens on the road goes further than lights: staged crashes, among other attempts ([Arranged to look like something else](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/arranged-to-look-like-something-else)). Cars have followed mine for weeks, and one ran me over when I was on foot ([They cannot be ignored](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/they-cannot-be-ignored)).

## Recognize it and let it go

The lifelong targeting cannot be ignored. The small disruptions in a day are different: we are outnumbered, it runs around the clock, and a person cannot answer every one of them and still have a life. Those you recognize and let go. A flash of high beams is one of them, and in November 2025 I said the same of the matching cars: "Please do your best to ignore it."

Writing it down lets it go without losing it. [ClickLog](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/click-log) has an entry for headlights and flashlights shined at you, and Road Sensitization is one of its named schemes. An entry takes about fifteen seconds, and your notes stay yours.

What members choose to share is counted across everybody. The first report already paired headlights and flashlights shined at you with Color Sensitization three times ([The Record Has Started](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/clicklog-the-record-has-started)). A pattern no single driver can see shows when enough people write down what they saw, with the date.
