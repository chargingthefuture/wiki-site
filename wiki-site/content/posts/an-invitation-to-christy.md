A community that can stand up on its own needs two things to be true at the same time.

Somebody has to keep people well. And somebody has to build the things people stand inside.

Most lists have those on opposite sides of a room. There is one person on the Directory who is listed for both.

## Christy

Christy (https://www.quora.com/profile/None-Ya-970), in the United States. Her Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/173cb388-7df9-4079-a048-4de2d64b2bf4 and it carries eight skills across two trades.

On one side: patient assessment and monitoring, medication administration, wound care and procedures, patient education and triage.

On the other: structural design, geotechnical assessment, construction supervision, and site surveys and CAD.

Her own description is shorter than the list: caregiver right now, many different jobs over the years, a Jane of all trades and master of none. Her headline is two words — just about anything, and the profile files her under Housing & Construction, which is one of the two trades and not the other.

## Why the range is the thing, not the caveat

Master of none is how people are taught to describe a working life that went in several directions. It assumes a room already full of specialists, where the only open position is the narrow one nobody has taken.

That is not the shape of this list. The app carries a catalog of 657 skills — the set a working economy of about five million people needs — and most of them have nobody in them at all. The list is empty rather than competitive.

An empty list is filled by people who can do more than one thing. Somebody who has held several jobs over several years has been paid to learn things that a person with one straight line has not had to. Being a caregiver right now and having done structural work before is two entire sectors covered by one person who already knows what both actually involve when the day goes wrong.

Medicine is the shortage this community feels hardest — SkillsHunt runs a standing mission to find a doctor, because a group this size needs one and does not have one. Nobody is claiming that patient assessment is being a doctor, and this post is not going to pretend otherwise. But triage, wound care and knowing when something needs somebody more qualified are what stand between a problem and a worse one, and they are on the list under her name.

## How she came to be listed

Christy did not put herself in the Directory. I did, after reading what she said about herself in public, and the listing is marked community-generated so that nobody has to guess.

That is how most of the list was built, one person at a time. The Directory is the thing survivors ask for more than anything else: a findable list of people and what they can do. Building it by waiting for people to sign up for another app would have meant building nothing.

## The invitation

Christy, both of those trades are on the list under your name, and neither has many people in it.

Claiming the listing is the step that makes it yours. It takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that the entry is under your control rather than mine — you set what it says, and you can take it down.

Nothing else is required. You do not have to use the rest of the app, you do not have to post anything, and you do not have to agree with anything I have written about why any of this is happening.

If the answer is no, that is a complete answer and nothing about your listing changes. You can have it taken down at any time, with no account involved anywhere in that. Nobody who has been asked has wanted theirs removed, but the offer has been real since the day the list went up.

## This does not run one way

An invitation that only asks is not worth much to somebody already carrying a full week.

The list works in both directions. A caregiver needs a ride that does not run through somebody who should not be driving her. A person doing site work needs a room, a repair, a meal, an hour of a trade she does not have. Those are on the list too, under other people's names, and the same listing that makes her findable is what makes them findable to her.

That is the exchange, and it is deliberately the first thing here. Asking somebody for something real is also what filters out the people who will never hand you anything.
