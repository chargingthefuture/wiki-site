Two things have to be true for the targeting to work.

The first is that the people around you can be reached. A neighbor, a landlord, a clerk, the front desk at a clinic. What you are told depends on who you ask, and whoever you ask can be reached first.

The second follows from the first: given enough time, there is nobody left to turn to.

Health care is one of the places where the first of those is felt most. The intake desk, the person deciding how urgent you are, the record that follows you from one visit to the next. It is worth having people nearby who know how all of that works from the inside.

The listing describes them as a former LPN, a licensed practical nurse, who also worked in admissions and utilization review, which is checking whether a treatment or a hospital stay meets the criteria to be covered. It carries seven specializations:

- Medication administration: giving the right medicine, in the right dose, at the right time.
- Patient assessment and monitoring: checking how somebody is doing and noticing when that changes.
- Patient education and triage: deciding how urgent a problem is, and explaining to a patient what to do next.
- Wound care and procedures: cleaning and dressing wounds, and the routine procedures a nurse carries out.
- Patient communication: talking with patients so they understand their own care.
- Patient intake and registration: getting a person admitted and their details recorded correctly.
- Records management: keeping those records complete and in order.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on October 14, the Directory's 312 people held 260 of them.

Where those skills sit in the catalog:

- Six are filed under Health, under nurses and medical assistants. Health is one of the better covered sectors on the list: 53 of its 96 skills are held by anybody.
- Records management is filed under administrative and office support in Professional and Business Services, where 21 of 52 are held.

A well covered sector still needs more than one person in it. Care happens where people are, and one nurse in one state is not much use to somebody three states away.

## What this is not

This is not a request to treat anybody, give medical advice or review anybody's records, and nothing here says Tired Of Being A TI will. Being on a list of what a person can do is not an offer of anything, and the Directory has never worked that way.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what Tired Of Being A TI said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs: what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work was looking after other people needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The listing that makes them findable makes those people findable to them, which is the second of the two things turned around: not nobody left, but somebody.
