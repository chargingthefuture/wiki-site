A gated community for Targeted Individuals is one of the most common things people ask for on Quora.

This July, KC asked it outright in a survivor space (https://uspsyopstargetedindividuals.quora.com/Are-there-actual-physical-communities-of-Targeted-Individuals-living-together-protecting-one-another-2): "Are there actual physical communities of Targeted Individuals living together protecting one another?" Their answer under it was one line — "If this does exist, I want to know!"

![KC's question in the US-PSYOPS-TARGETED-INDIVIDUALS space on Quora: "Are there actual physical communities of Targeted Individuals living together protecting one another?" Answered by KC, Jul 8: "If this does exist, I want to know!" 309 views, 7 upvotes, 23 comments, 2 shares, 1 of 4 answers.](images/kc-gated-community-question-2026-07-08.jpg)

People lined up in the comments. One wrote that Targeted Individuals are the only people they want to be around, that they could not trust anyone who has not been targeted, and that such a community would grow as more people are subjected to this. Others volunteered by name: willing to meet in a neutral place, a safe house in every community, land within reach of family, "I can surely benefit from a TI Community."

![The full comment thread under the question: another answer reading "We could all form together and fund a safe haven. Yes, people talk a lot about it and wish for it, but we have to form together and put it into action"; a commenter recommending Farah Brunache as someone "who has started to set up such a system for people needing support — this is what we need to do, band together and get this stopped"; and several people volunteering interest by name.](images/kc-question-full-thread-2026-08.jpg)

One disclosure about that screenshot: it shows the thread as it stood, including a law-office flyer posted into it. I am showing the thread as evidence that people want this, and that is all — I do not know the people in it and I do not support or vouch for anything advertised there.

My answer to the question is that a gated community is possible. Not by me alone, and not as one place somewhere — but possible. And the app now shows who could plan one, which is the part of the answer I can demonstrate rather than assert.

## The most common answer, and why it is not grounded

The most common answer survivors give each other is no — that the moment a community of targets formed, it would be found, infiltrated within a day, and destroyed. I have read that answer many times, from people who genuinely mean it and who have every reason to expect the worst.

The opinion is common. It is not grounded, and one detail shows how ungrounded it is: the destruction is always described as if it already happened. Under one version of that answer this July, someone asked the only question that matters — where did this happen? There was no answer, because it has not happened. It is a prediction dressed as a memory, and a prediction is exactly the kind of claim you weigh against the record.

The record says the opposite.

Marginalized groups live together today. Jewish communities exist. Chinatowns exist. The list goes on, and every group on it has enemies. Hate-group violence against them never ceases — and the communities continue anyway, decade after decade, because a neighborhood is not an organization. An organization can be beheaded. A neighborhood of people who know each other, trade with each other, and watch each other's streets does not fall apart because one bad actor walks in. Estonia is 1.3 million people with a hostile neighbor on its border, and it continues.

The history runs the other way too. Black Americans were sold integration, and it failed — to the point where it is now difficult to keep their neighborhoods from being ruined. Gentrification is the deliberate breaking up of communities that concentrated, and the effort spent on it tells you concentration works. The generation W.E.B. Du Bois wrote about built trade schools and businesses by training with their own community, and [the manifesto](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/chargingthefuture/The-Answer:-EXIT-THEIR-ECONOMY,-EXIT-THE-PSYOP) covers what they did and how. What diluted their unity and prosperity was dispersal, not concentration.

So: yes, a physical place can be attacked, and infiltration is a real problem — a later section is about exactly that. But the people running this want survivors diluted and isolated. That is not a reason to stay scattered. It is the reason to go the opposite direction.

I am not writing this to argue with anyone who has settled on staying apart. That distance is common, and it is not mine to judge. This section is for the reader on the fence — the one who wants this and has been told, as if it were history, that it cannot exist. Nobody can point to the event that settled that, because there is no such event. What can be pointed to is every community on the list above, still standing.

## What the question is actually asking for

People asking usually picture the finished thing: a gate, a road, houses, neighbors who believe them.

What comes before any of that is a document. Who does what, in what order, on what money, on what land, under what rules for who gets in. Every community that ever got built had one, whether or not anyone called it that.

That document is not one person's job. It needs someone who can form a legal entity and write membership criteria. Someone who can read a title and a zoning map. Someone who can cost out water, power, and waste. Someone who can plan a care model and a quiet room. Someone who can plan a gate.

And it needs someone to write all of it down in a form a different group can pick up and use somewhere else entirely. That last part matters most. One runbook, reusable anywhere in the world, is worth more than one compound in one state.

## The Community tab

The Workforce screen in the app has a tab called Community. It sorts the people already on the skills map into the ten teams that document needs.

Nobody signs up for it. It is not a second roster and it is not a second application. Everyone in Community is already in the Directory — it is the Directory, filtered on the categories a gated community would need.

The screen says so itself: "Each team draws from the Workforce sectors it maps to; the roster is every member who already matches those sectors. This recomputes live from the Directory — it updates itself as members and skills change."

To reach it, scroll the tab strip at the top sideways. This is the Workforce screen as it opens:

## The question everyone asks second

Who gets in.

It is not theoretical and I have already had to answer it. In November 2025 a person posing as a Targeted Individual came to me wanting a spot in exactly this kind of community — one organized entirely through Quora. He spent weeks in the messages first. I wrote it up at the time: [Damon Mayle, posing as a TI](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/chargingthefuture/discourse-migrate/damon-mayle-posing-as-a-ti).

My answer then is my answer now, from that post:

> "Vetting" TIs is what I signed up for in the TI economy. And by vetting I mean meeting in person. That is the only way I can tell if someone is a TI or not. Most Quora posts, DMs, calls, video chats (which I will never do) are mostly meaningless. Easy to fake being a TI. Anyone can copy and paste real TI posts and mannerisms and use it as their own. You cannot fake authenticity in person.

That is the work behind the Safety & Security team's line: gate and perimeter model, emergency plans, drills, and an incident process that respects member privacy. The 2025 attempt is also why I do not think a community like this can be organized on a platform. A platform is where the people trying to get inside it already are.

And notice what that attempt actually proves. One infiltrator tried, in person, and was identified in an afternoon — by exactly the vetting the doubters say is impossible. The community was not destroyed. There was nothing to destroy yet, and the method for keeping it that way worked on its first test.
