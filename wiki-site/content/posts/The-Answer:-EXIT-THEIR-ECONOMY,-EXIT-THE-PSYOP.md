# Live, Work, and Prevail — a manifesto for survivors

## Who we are

We are survivors. Targeted Individuals (TIs) — people subjected to organized, long-running harassment carried out by what I call the Specterati: a coordinated network that profits from surveilling, isolating, and exploiting us. This is not a mystery and it is not spiritual warfare. It is organized crime — human trafficking — and it is old: I have found writing describing this same crime from the 1690s. I estimate at least five million of us worldwide. I am not suggesting we form a nation-state. What I am saying is that five million people are more than enough to meet each other's needs — it is possible to live a decent life with less harassment, fraud, and abuse if we work together. Each of us discovered the same pattern separately, in our own towns, jobs, and homes — and each of us was told, separately, that we were imagining it.

I am done arguing about whether it is happening. I built what comes next. Filling it is up to us.

## What we have learned

The harassment is not the point. The economy behind it is.

- It runs on reward and extortion. Favors, jobs, housing, rides, and status are used to recruit ordinary people and bind them to harming us.
- Our own participation feeds it. The reason they can torture us successfully is that we depend on their infrastructure — their rides, their rentals, their platforms, their jobs. Their house, their rules. Every transaction we route through services run by people who profit from targeting extends their reach.
- Evidence alone has not ended it. Cameras, clips, and reports matter for our records, but no recording has ever shut down the machine. Cameras or not, it does not matter. What matters is their economy. That is why they can do what they do. We cannot record our way out. We have to stop paying into the market that funds our abuse.
- They mobbed us out of society. So we form our own community.

The Specterati thrive on division, shame, and isolation. The distractions — the cameras, the theatrics, the endless debates — are designed to keep us circling. The one thing that is not a distraction is where the money and labor flow.

Come as you are: it does not matter how much money you have; you can contribute. Nothing in it routes value to the people who profit from targeting us. That is the whole design.

The Specterati run an economy on torture. We can run an economy on abundance. The choice is ours.

## This has been done before

History has shown that oppressed people must take responsibility for their own liberation. There is no savior coming. We must come together — like it or not.

In 1903, a persecuted community in America published a book of essays called The Negro Problem — Booker T. Washington, W.E.B. Du Bois, Charles W. Chesnutt, Wilford H. Smith, and others, writing for people who were denied jobs, housing, fair courts, and physical safety by an organized system that most of the country refused to combat. The full text is free to read and download: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15041.

Their answer was not to wait for rescue.

Washington wrote that "all races that have got upon their feet have done so largely by laying an economic foundation," and that education should be brought to bear "upon something that is needed to be done, and something which they will be permitted to do in the community." He closed with how a community answers those who doubt it: "we must re-enforce argument with results. One farm bought, one house built, one home sweetly and intelligently kept … one factory running successfully … these will tell more in our favor than all the abstract eloquence that can be summoned to plead our cause."

Du Bois opened his essay with: "The Negro race, like all races, is going to be saved by its exceptional men" — leadership developed from within, not granted from outside.

Chesnutt and Smith documented, plainly and in public, exactly how the system worked — the vote juggled away, the law dodged — so no one could claim it did not exist.

Skills. An economic foundation. Leadership from within. A public record. Results in place of argument. And it keeps working: communities in our own time have built mutual-credit economies — Switzerland's WIR network, Sardinia's Sardex — proof that people who trade with each other can hold their own inside a hostile economy. We do not compare our pain to theirs — we take the method. When the surrounding economy is used against you, build one that is not.

## Our commitments

1. Refuse coercive rewards. No accepting "free" goods, services, or friendships tied to control or recruitment.
2. Use vetted services first. Route work, housing, transport, repairs, and care through the TI Skills Economy directory.
3. Document securely; do not monetize the abuse. Keep evidence for legal aid and trusted advocates, not for platforms that profit from our attention.
4. Share skills, not attention. Trade time and services inside the community instead of giving currency and views to the people harming us.
5. Stand together. Isolation is what they need to succeed. We do not isolate and we do not give up; we compare notes, witness for each other, and get what we need as a community.

The best revenge is living well, and living well together is exactly what they cannot stop. Stabilize at 384, then invite the next 1,000. Then 10,000. Then five million.
