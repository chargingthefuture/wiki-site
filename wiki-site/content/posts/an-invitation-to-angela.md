Anybody can open [chargingthefuture.com](https://chargingthefuture.com), tap what they need, and see how many people on the list could help. No account and no name. When a need comes back with nobody, the page notes it so I know to look ([See who could help you, before you make an account](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/see-who-could-help-without-an-account)).

One of the choices is health care. Nursing, first aid and caregiving are among the skills behind it, and Angela Womer is listed for all three, so Angela is one of the people that count is made of.

## Angela Womer

Angela Womer (https://www.quora.com/profile/Angela-Womer-2), in the United States. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/b0d79cee-77fa-4772-822d-495241a68787.

The listing carries ten specializations, in two kinds of work.

The first is care:

- First aid and CPR: keeping somebody alive and steady until more help arrives.
- Medication administration: giving the right medicine, in the right dose, at the right time.
- Patient assessment and monitoring: checking how somebody is doing, and noticing when it changes.
- Wound care and procedures: cleaning and dressing a wound so it heals.
- Caregiving: looking after somebody day to day who cannot manage alone.
- Health promotion and education: explaining how to stay well, in words people can use.
- Referral and continuity of care: knowing who somebody should see next, and making sure nothing is lost on the way.
- Ethical practice and boundaries: keeping care honest, and keeping clear where the carer's role ends.

The second is hands-on:

- Basic tool operation: using hand and power tools safely on a job.
- Landscaping and grounds work: keeping a yard and the ground around a building in order.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on October 14, the Directory's 321 people held 260 of them.

Where those skills sit in the catalog:

- The eight care skills are filed in Health, where 53 of 96 are held: under nurses, paramedics, community health workers, general practitioners, therapists, and home health aides and caregivers.
- Basic tool operation and landscaping are filed under construction laborers in Housing and Construction, where 18 of 51 are held.

A count on the front page says how many people could help and never who they are. Names, and a way to reach anybody, stay behind a free account and the check.

## What this is not

This is not a request for anybody's medical advice or care, and nothing here says Angela will give either. Being on a list of what a person can do is not an offer of anything, and the Directory has never worked that way.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what Angela said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs: what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work is looking after other people needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The listing that makes them findable makes those people findable to them.

## Who is on the list

Everyone written about in this series is a self-identified Targeted Individual. That means they said so in public, and it is the only basis for the listing. I cannot verify it, and neither can a reader. Some people who say it are not survivors, and some are the people doing the harassing, so a post here is not a vouch for anybody and should not be read as one. I am not responsible for what anyone named on this list does or says, on the app, on Quora, or anywhere else. The closest anybody can get to finding a real Targeted Individual is through the app, which admits people one at a time through Unlock and has other mechanisms in place to filter actively. Even with those, there is no such thing as a hundred percent.
