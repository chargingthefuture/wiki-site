When [TI Radio](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/ti-radio) went up in September, I wrote about the schedule. This post is about what goes in it.

The rows are empty until somebody takes one. I am not booking sixteen slots a day and calling it a station. So this is an invitation, and it is meant for people who have something to perform or something to say.

## Any hour, ninety minutes

The day is cut into 90-minute slots, sixteen of them, and the guide runs seven days ahead. Every time is printed in the reader's own timezone. If your best hour is three in the morning, there is a slot at three in the morning, and somebody in another timezone may be wide awake for it.

## How to take a slot

1. Open [TI Radio](https://app.lagatos.com/ti-radio).
2. Press an open row.
3. Write what it is about, and add a few sentences for somebody deciding whether to come if you want to.

The slot is then yours. Your handle and your subject appear on that row for everyone, including people who never sign in. At the time you booked, you and whoever turns up meet in [Chyme](https://app.lagatos.com/apps/chyme), the app's live audio rooms.

Hosting needs an approved account. Reading the guide and listening do not.

First come, first served. There is no queue, no application, and nobody approving the topic. A slot is taken, not granted, so a name on the guide is not an endorsement from this project.

## The rules

- Three slots in any 24 hours. Not three per calendar day: any 24-hour stretch. That keeps one person from taking a week.
- You can give a slot back any time before it starts, and it goes straight back to the guide as open.
- You cannot give it back once it has begun. By then people have turned up.
- An admin can remove a slot and is asked why. That is me, and it is one person.

If you book, turn up. Putting your name on a public schedule is a commitment to be there.

## A stage you run

In Chyme, speakers are on the stage and everyone else is in the audience. A listener raises a hand to speak, and whoever moderates the room decides who comes up, who is muted, and who is removed. Any member can open a room of their own, so the stage can be yours from the first minute to the last.

That matters if you used to perform and stopped. Nobody walks on in the middle of your set unless you bring them up.

## Show ideas

For musicians and singers:

- Three songs, and the story behind each one.
- Learning an instrument in public, the same slot every week, so listeners hear the progress.
- A late acoustic set for the people awake when nobody else is.
- An open mic: you host, and listeners raise a hand to take a turn.
- A rehearsal with the door open.

For poets and readers:

- Your own work, or somebody else's with their name said out loud.
- A chapter a week of a book, read and then talked through.

For talk shows and interviews:

- A weekly check-in at the same hour, so people know where to find you.
- An interview with a survivor about what they can do, not about what was done to them.
- Practical subjects: finding housing, earning a living, keeping records.
- Questions and answers on something you know well.

It does not have to be about being targeted. It can be about anything.
