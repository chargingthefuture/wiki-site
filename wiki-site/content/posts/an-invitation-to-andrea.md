I have been targeted for over thirty years, and in that time a trafficker's stance has never varied: a stranger already owes them something, before a word has been exchanged ([You don't owe me anything](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/you-dont-owe-me-anything)).

The list starts from the opposite stance. Being on it costs nothing and asks for nothing back. Nobody on it owes me anything, and I owe nobody on it anything either.

Andrea is on it, for ethics work and for settling disputes between people.

## Andrea

Andrea (https://www.quora.com/profile/Andrea-R-453), in Florida, United States. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/771da7b0-e012-47c6-8769-7b64f3dd811c.

The listing carries three specializations:

- Ethics investigations and reporting: finding out whether something was done wrong, and writing down what was found.
- Ethics policy development and implementation: setting out the rules a group holds itself to, and making them work in practice.
- Employee relations and conflict resolution: hearing out two sides of a dispute at work and finding a way through it.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on October 14, the Directory's 321 people held 260 of them.

All three of Andrea's skills are filed in Professional and Business Services, where 21 of 52 are held: the two ethics skills under ethics and compliance officers, and conflict resolution under human resources.

The Skills Economy has nobody in the middle. You find the person, you arrange it between you, and the app records that it happened. Sooner or later two people who arranged something between themselves will see it differently, and hearing both sides out is a skill somebody has to hold.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what Andrea said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs: what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either. Claiming it does not put Andrea in my debt, and leaving it alone does not either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work is sorting out what went wrong between other people needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The listing that makes them findable makes those people findable to them.
