It is not an unusual thing to wonder. I have seen it asked more than once, in different words: whether this is going to get worse, whether it becomes more dangerous around an election, where it all ends up.

The vocabulary I use for the people running this is in the [Dictionary](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/Dictionary); the question keeps the words it was asked in.

## The question looks forward. I think the answer is behind us

There is an assumption inside the question, and it is worth pulling out before answering, because it is the part I would have got wrong myself a few years ago.

The assumption is that the number of people being targeted is the number that changes a society.

I do not think it is. A targeted person is a person things are done to. They can be starved, moved, made unemployable, and none of that alters how anybody else lives. What alters how everybody else lives is the other count: how many people have agreed to do it.

## Three hundred, in a day, in a town I had just arrived in

I have written this before and I will put it here plainly. In a town I had just arrived in, I counted the people involved on foot within 24 hours. I stopped at 300.

Not 300 over years. Not 300 who knew me. Three hundred inside a single day, in a place I had never been, already there when I got there.

That is not a projection about a future society. It is a description of a present one. Whatever the question is imagining might happen if this keeps growing has, on that street, already happened.

## What that ratio actually does to a place

I want to be concrete rather than dramatic, because the concrete version is worse.

Work stops going to the person who can do it. It goes to whoever is willing to do the other thing as well. I have watched employment close off on cue, and the mechanism is not mysterious — [the operation runs on economics](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/economics-oppress-save), and an economy where a job is a favor somebody grants is an economy where competence has stopped being the thing that decides.

Reporting stops working. Not because one officer is corrupt, but because a report describes something that the people around you will all contradict, and they will do it without hesitation, because they were asked to.

And a very large number of ordinary people learn something that cannot be unlearned. They learn that a stranger's livelihood is a thing you can be paid in favors to take, that there are no consequences for taking it, and that other people will help. Once a person knows that, they know it about everybody, not only about the person they were pointed at. Nobody is issued that knowledge and then contains it.

That last part is the answer to the question, as best as I can give it. A society does not get damaged by the people it targets. It gets damaged by how many people it teaches.

## Where it ends for the people targeted

I said the count of targeted people does not reshape a society. That is not the same as saying it does not matter, so I will say what it does.

It ends in three places, and I have [written about them](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/something-to-lose): destitution, a mass shooting, suicide. Those are the endings I have watched people arrive at. More people targeted means more people arriving at them.

## What I have instead of a prediction

I am not going to forecast a society. I do not know how it goes, and the people who tell you they do are usually selling the conclusion that nothing can be done.

What I have is an economy. Not as a metaphor — a list of people, what they can do, and a way to trade it, so that the job, the room, and the ride do not have to run through somebody who can be reached. That is worth building whether the count rises or falls, because it is the part that does not depend on anybody else's behavior changing.

I have said before that I expect fewer people taking their own lives and fewer mass shootings on the other side of this. I cannot prove it and I am not going to pretend to. It is what I expect, and I would rather say so than keep it to myself.

The recruited outnumber us by a great deal, and the arithmetic of building does not care. Compounding is not a contest. Twenty-five times over is twenty-five times over whether one person opposes it or a thousand do.
