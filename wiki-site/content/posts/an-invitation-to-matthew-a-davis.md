A working economy needs people who can hold it together on its worst day. A storm, a flood, a power cut that lasts a week. On an ordinary day everybody does their own work. On that day somebody has to know who is doing what, who needs what, and in what order.

## Matthew

Matthew A Davis (https://www.quora.com/profile/Matthew-A-Davis-1), in Zephyrhills, Florida. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/bdc6058f-17d7-456a-b6e7-9918c811db87.

The listing carries two specializations. Advocacy stands for what Matthew has written about Targeted Individuals. The other is Emergency Support Function coordination.

An emergency response in the United States is divided into support functions: transportation, communications, public works, firefighting, shelter and food, public health, and more. Each one has its own agencies and volunteers. Coordinating them is the work of keeping all of those running to one plan, so that the truck, the shelter and the people who need both end up in the same place at the same time.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on September 29, the Directory's 167 people held 227 of them.

Emergency and Reserve Roles is one of the thinnest sectors on the list: 2 of its 21 skills are held by anybody.

Most plans people make here assume nothing goes badly wrong. A room, a ride, a shared house: each one works until the weather, the power or the water stops. The people who know how to run that week are rarely thought about until it arrives.

## What this is not

This is not a request to run anybody's emergency, and nothing here says Matthew will. Being on a list of what a person can do is not an offer of anything, and the Directory has never worked that way.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what Matthew said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs — what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work is other people's worst week needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The same listing that makes them findable is what makes those people findable to them.

## Who is on the list

Everyone written about in this series is a self-identified Targeted Individual. That means they said so in public, and it is the only basis for the listing. I cannot verify it, and neither can a reader. Some people who say it are not survivors, and some are the people doing the harassing, so a post here is not a vouch for anybody and should not be read as one. I am not responsible for what anyone named on this list does or says, on the app, on Quora, or anywhere else. The closest anybody can get to finding a real Targeted Individual is through the app, which admits people one at a time through Unlock and has other mechanisms in place to filter actively. Even with those, there is no such thing as a hundred percent.
