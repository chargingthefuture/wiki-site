Losing your housing does not only mean losing somewhere to sleep. It means the ordinary business of washing moves into facilities that belong to other people, on their terms, under whatever conditions they set.

Where I stay now is somewhere between a shelter and the street. To wash, I leave it and use a shared facility.

Those showers cannot be locked from the inside. That is deliberate and it is not, on its face, unreasonable: a lot of the people using these facilities are criminals and people using drugs, and staff need to be able to open a door. The policy exists for a real reason. That is exactly what makes it usable against someone.

## Yesterday

Someone keyed me into a shower. I undressed. Immediately after, the door was opened and I was exposed, completely, to a man who was there. They laughed.

The laughter is the part that settles it. An accidental door is silent and embarrassed. This was neither. It has happened before, in other facilities — a gym I stopped using, paid facilities where the showers had the same problem. The buildings change and the move does not.

## Then getting to the next one

After leaving one facility for another, a man circled me on the metro and again as I approached the next place. It ended in an altercation and he became physically violent.

That belongs with the rest of it. Getting somewhere is exposure. Being there is exposure. Neither is optional, because washing is not optional.

## What ClickLog gained this week

Two additions, both live in the app.

A problem tag: sexually assaulted, or deliberately exposed and humiliated. It covers the whole range survivors report — assault and rape at the worst end, and, short of any contact, deliberate exposure. It is deliberately coarse. The tag names the harm so it can be counted; what happened stays in the private note where it belongs.

A scheme: The Staged Exposure. That is the engineered form — the key, the timing, the door opened at the moment it does the most damage, and nothing required from the target except being present.

## Why I think this is the substitute

They have run honeypots at me for several decades. I have not kept count and the number is not small for the time involved. I have never engaged with one, and they have never managed to sexually assault or rape me. Other survivors have not been so fortunate.

So they go to the next best option. That is what yesterday was. The exposure is not a separate interest of theirs that happened to surface — it is what is left when the thing they were working toward does not happen.

## Whether the honeypot belongs in the same category

A honeypot is a manufactured romance: an approach that was arranged, run for as long as it is useful, and ended in whatever way does the most harm. It has been on the scheme list for a while. Two things come out of it. One is the emotional wreckage, worked deliberately. The other is a fabricated domestic violence claim, which the arranged closeness is what makes credible — a person has to be able to say "I know this person" before an accusation carries.

Underneath both, as far as I can tell, is a fixation on sexual humiliation. The mechanism differs. The door handle does it in a second; the honeypot does it over months, through manipulation instead of a key.

My own view is that honeypotting is a form of sexual assault. I do not know whether a lawyer or a clinician would put it in that category, and people who talk about honeypotting generally do not describe it that way. So I am not asserting a classification. I am saying that when I look at what it is for and what it does, I cannot find the line that separates it from the rest.

That is why the tag is written the way it is. It names the harm, not the legal theory.

## The answer is not a better lock

There is no version of this that gets solved by vigilance in someone else's shower. The fix is not needing to be there.

Housing is the first half. [LightHouse](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/lighthouse) lists places offered by hosts who are survivors themselves, which is a different proposition from a facility where you are tolerated. Somewhere with a door that locks and a bathroom that is yours removes the entire category.

Rides are the second half. [TrustTransport](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/trust-transport) arranges transport inside the community, and the journey to and from is where the rest of it happens. The more of your movement that runs through people who are not doing this to you, the fewer occasions there are for any of it.

That is the design, stated plainly: not protection in the moment, but fewer moments that require it.

## And the arranged relationships

The same logic answers the honeypot, which is harder, because it arrives looking like something good.

An approach that was arranged does not survive being asked for something real. A person who was sent to be close to you will not do your repair, take your shift, or drive you somewhere at a cost to themselves. Material help is the one thing that consistently does not happen. So an economy that puts the exchange first filters them out early, without anyone having to accuse anybody of anything.

What is left are people who showed up with something in their hands, more than once. Relationships that come out of that are not arranged, because nobody arranges a person who actually helps you.
