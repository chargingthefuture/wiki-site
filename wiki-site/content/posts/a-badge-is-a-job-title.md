## Who pays

Most of the people taking part are not paid in money. They are paid in kind: a job, an apartment, friends, a favor, sex, drugs. The money is further up, laundered through the ordinary economy. [Why nobody does anything](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/why-nobody-does-anything) and [An epidemic, not a secret society](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/an-epidemic-not-a-secret-society) go into both.

## A badge is a job title

A job title, and the status that comes with it, says nothing about the character of the person holding it. Police officer is a job title. So are landlord, manager and judge.

The people taking part are paid in exactly those things, among others: a job, a promotion, standing. So a title is not evidence that somebody is outside it. Sherri Jenkins (https://www.quora.com/profile/Sherri-Jenkins-12) wrote that targets readily report law enforcement ignoring their pleas for help and taking part, along with firefighters, utility workers and post office employees. Her description is in [The best description of it I have read](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/best-description-i-have-read). That is a list of job titles.

I have called the police on individual incidents, and gone to a station over one. While I was there, an officer mocked a call I had made to a national hotline back to me, in person ([The Assistant Is Not a Clinician](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/the-assistant-is-not-a-clinician)). A report goes to whoever holds the title, and the title promises nothing about who that is. [Who to report it to](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/who-to-report-it-to) goes into what a report can and cannot carry.

None of this is legal advice, and it does not tell anybody what to do when a report goes nowhere.
