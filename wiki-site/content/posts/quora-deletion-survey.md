When an account is closed, the evidence of it disappears with the account. There is no page left saying what was there, no notice anyone else can read, and nothing you can point at later. The loss is quiet by design, and quiet losses do not add up to anything.

There is now a survey for recording them. It is here, and reading it needs no account at all:

https://app.chargingthefuture.com/survey/quora-account-deletions

## What it asks

Two things about you: whether you consider yourself a Targeted Individual, and whether at least one of your accounts was removed. Then one card for each closed account.

Each card asks for the handle, what happened to it, and when. The options for what happened are the things Quora does: the whole account deleted, banned or suspended, posts removed while the account stayed, a Space you ran removed, or blocked from posting. There is no option for closing an account yourself, so nothing in this data is somebody walking away.

It asks what reason Quora gave, and "no reason was given" is one of the choices rather than a blank. That is the answer I expect most often, and it is worth recording as an answer in its own right. It asks whether you appealed and whether anything came back. It asks what the account mostly wrote about, and roughly how many posts and how long it ran, both optional.

## Why one card per account

The count of removals is the number of cards, never a number anyone types into a box. That means every removal in any total has a handle and a date behind it, and anyone reading a figure later can ask what it is made of.

It is a smaller number than a typed one would be, and it holds up.

## What happens to your handle and your words

Three separate permissions, each one off unless you turn it on:

Your handles may be published. Your words may be quoted. Your handle may be attached to your quote. They are separate on purpose — you can allow your words to be used while staying unnamed, which is the combination most people want.

A response with all three switched off still counts in every total. Nothing is lost by declining, and nothing about you appears anywhere because you filled it in.

## What this data can and cannot say

It is self-report. Nothing in it is checked against Quora, and it does not need to be — it is a record of what people say happened to them, which is exactly the record that does not otherwise exist.

It will always be a floor rather than a full picture, for a reason worth naming: only someone who found their way to another platform can answer at all, and only someone willing to make a free account can submit one. So it counts people who kept going and were willing to sign up, and misses everyone who did not. Whatever number it eventually holds, the real one is larger.

It also cannot say what share of Quora, or what share of Targeted Individuals, this represents. It can say how many removals were reported, by how many people, over what period. That is the whole claim, and I will not stretch it further.

## Why submitting asks for a sign-in

Reading takes nothing. Submitting asks you to sign in free, and that is only there to keep bulk junk out of a table meant to be citable. It is not a claim that anyone is verified, and it is not a check on whether you belong here.

## Get your writing back, then give it somewhere to work

Quora deleted the account. It did not delete your right to a copy of what you wrote, and they will send you one if you ask.

From [their own help page](https://help.quora.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000839503-Can-I-get-a-copy-of-my-data), quoted here in case that page moves:

> Yes. Quora will send an archive of your content and personal data to your account's primary email address on request. If you would like to request a copy of your data, you may do so by submitting a request via email to privacy@quora.com or by visiting quora.com/contact and selecting "I want a copy of my data"
>
> Please note that you will typically receive the archive within 72 hours of our team confirming that we have received your data request.

Once you have that archive, it does not have to sit in a folder. If what you wrote was about being targeted, you can lend it to the [Knowledge Library](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/knowledge) — the reference the app's assistant answers from. It means a newcomer asking a question gets an answer shaped by more than one person's experience, and your writing goes on doing work after the platform that hosted it decided otherwise.

Two things then come out of one deletion: a line in a record that can be counted, and writing that is still useful to somebody.

## Why any of this

I have lost four accounts to this now, and each time the only record was whatever I happened to write down afterwards. That is not good enough, and it does not have to be how it goes.
