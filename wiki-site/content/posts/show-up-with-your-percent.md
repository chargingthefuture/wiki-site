Most of a Targeted Individual's day is not theirs. It goes to the attacks and to what they force: moving again, watching the people around you, sleeping badly or not at all, and doing ordinary things that have been made slow on purpose. What is left at the end of it might be five percent. Some days it is nothing.

Five percent is not enough to reach a goal alone. A job in another state, a vehicle you can live in, a move that holds: each one is a chain of small steps, research and calls and follow-ups, done in order over weeks. The attacks land on the chain. A step gets missed, the order breaks, and it starts over. Anybody living this knows the shape of it, and knows that telling somebody in it to try harder is telling them to have a day they do not have.

The arithmetic changes when the five percent is not all one person's. Twenty members with five percent of a day each is a working day. Nobody in it had more time than they had. That is what the goal board runs on.

## What it is

PeerProgramming now opens on a Goals tab, and the tab is a board.

You post one goal with a finish line. You break it into cards: small tasks somebody else could do from a phone in under half an hour. Find three yards hiring near Dallas and add their numbers. Find three campers under six thousand dollars within two hundred miles of Tulsa. Ask one dealer whether they finance with no credit check.

The cards sit in three columns: Up for grabs, Doing, Done. You pick any one card, from anybody's goal. You do it, you post what you found, and it moves to Done with your name on it. The goal's owner reads the result and marks it "It helped", keeps it, or sends it back for somebody else. That is the entire loop.

There is no conversation on the board. A goal, its cards, and their results, and nothing else can be written there. The weekly sessions and the call are still in PeerProgramming, and they are separate from this.

## Why it is shaped this way

I have tried group chats for this three ways and none of them held. Who shows up on a day depends on how hard that day was, so the chat is a different room every time. It slides from doing into comparing notes, which is talk about the attacks, and the attacks already have most of the day. And on an open chat there is no filter: anybody put out can come back under a new name the same evening.

The board sits inside the app, behind Unlock, so taking part needs an approved account, and an approved account is not something you can rejoin under a new name. A card is one task with one result, so there is nothing to slide into. And a card asks for one small thing, once, so a bad day costs nothing: you take a card when you have the half hour, and you do not when you do not. Nobody is waiting on you to appear.

## What counts

When the goal's owner marks a card "It helped", it counts. It goes toward the day's 384, the count of members who delivered something to another member on one day, and toward the helper's Weavers of the Commons badge. Both are defined on the [Dictionary](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/chargingthefuture/Dictionary). A card kept without that mark, or sent back, does not count, and nobody but the goal's owner and the helper sees which it was. A finished card is a finished card to the rest of the cohort.

## Step three

[Peace Battle 2](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/peace-battle-2) used to ask, as its third step, that you post your one percent. It now asks that you show up with your percent. Whatever is left of today, that is one card.
