I answered the version of this asked by somebody deciding whether to go, in [Move toward somebody](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/move-toward-somebody). This is the other half, and it is the half more people are actually in: you already went. You arrived. Within days it was running again, and now you are sitting in a new place wondering what the point was.

## The speed of the restart is not about the town

When I arrived in Kansas the street activity was there immediately, at something like three hundred people to one. Nobody had to follow me around for a month to learn my routine first. That is worth saying plainly because of what a person concludes from it.

The conclusion people reach is that the town is bad, or that the move was a mistake, or that these people are everywhere in a way that makes any plan pointless. What it actually shows is the thing underneath: what travels is a name on a list, not a neighborhood. The list arrives before you do. A place cannot be quiet for you when the reason is attached to you rather than to the place.

So the restart tells you nothing about the town, and nothing about the decision to go. It tells you the list works the way the list works.

## What the move did change

Something did change, and it is narrow and real.

The specific people are different. The neighbor who had been at it for six years is not there. The coworkers are not there. The person who had a key, the one who knew what time you leave, the ones who had been asked years ago and said yes — all of that had to start again with different people who have not been asked yet, or have only just been asked.

That is the window, and it is the only thing a move buys. It does not last, and it is not nothing.

Here is the part that decides whether the move helped: what you put into that window. Most people spend it setting up a life exactly the way they set up the last one, which means the same arrangements get rebuilt among strangers who will be reached in the same way and in the same order.

## What to put in the window

One arrangement at a time, into hands that are not part of it.

A ride. A room. An hour of a trade. The people on the [Directory](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory) are survivors, listed with where they are and what they can do, so the search starts from a list of people who have the same problem rather than from a stranger who does not. [LightHouse](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/lighthouse) is rooms offered by survivors to survivors, which matters most right after a move, when housing is the first thing a new place has to give you and the first thing they will try to hand you themselves. [Foundation](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/foundation) lists providers and what they offer. [SocketRelay](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/socket-relay) is where you post what you need without opening with a speech.

None of that ends the harassment. What it does is decide who your week runs through in the new place, which is the one variable a move actually hands you.

## Do not move again on the strength of this

The reflex after an arrival like that is to conclude the place was wrong and go somewhere else. Watch that reflex, because it runs in their favor.

Every move costs money, and money is what they take first. Every move empties the list of people who know you, which is the condition the harassment is built to produce anyway. A person moving every eight months is doing to themselves, at their own expense, what the [Forced Homecoming](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/why-they-want-you-living-with-family) is engineered to do to them.

Move again when there is a reason that survives the question: what will be different about who is around me when I get there? That reason exists sometimes. It is not the same thing as leaving because they found you, because they will find you.

## Write down the before and after

Keep a dated record across the move, and keep it the same way on both sides of it. [ClickLog](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/click-log) takes about fifteen seconds an incident and your notes stay yours.

It will not hand you a clean answer about whether the move helped, because nothing here produces one. What it gives you is the difference between knowing what happened and remembering it, and memory rewrites the earlier version to match the later one. A person who logged the last three months in the old place and the first three in the new one can see what actually changed. A person relying on memory gets the version their worst week wrote.

## So

The move was not wasted, and moving again is not the answer to it following you. What a move gives you is a short stretch where fewer of the people around you have been asked yet. Spend it building a week that does not run through them.
