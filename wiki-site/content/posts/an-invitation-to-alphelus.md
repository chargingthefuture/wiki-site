Two things have to be true for the targeting to work.

The first is that the people your week runs through can be reached. The employer, the landlord, the person who comes to fix the thing. Not the shouting on the sidewalk — that is theater, and it is there to be noticed. The work is quieter: a word to the employer, a word to the landlord, an arrangement with whoever is going to be in your home for an hour with the power off.

The second is that there is nobody else. That one is not a starting condition. It is what the first produces, given time.

So the answer is not to stop them reaching people, which [nothing I know of does](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/what-can-be-stopped). It is to have people they have not reached, doing the things a week runs on. Electricity is one of the things a week runs on.

## Alphelus

Alphelus Allen (https://www.quora.com/profile/Alphelus-Allen), in San Francisco, California. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/9cd4a855-509c-4802-9291-bb68fa803a5e. It notes a BS in electrical engineering from Mississippi State University and carries seven specializations: system design and architecture, frequency and voltage control, power systems design, building design, construction documentation, electrical, and wiring and circuit installation.

Read the seven together and they run from the power system down to the outlet. Frequency and voltage control and power systems design are the utility end — keeping power steady and designing the systems that carry it. Building design and construction documentation are the drawings a building gets built from. Electrical, and wiring and circuit installation, are the hands-on end: the panel, the circuit, the socket the space heater goes into.

Some of that a survivor never sees done. Some of it comes to the door. An electrician works in the place you sleep, with the power off, at a time they choose, and a survivor cannot get the work done any other way. That is one of the points at which a person who can be reached stands between you and the lights being on — and the point this post is about is that it does not have to be a stranger standing there.

## What the list actually holds

The skills catalog carries 686 entries — the set of things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do. Read on September 21, the day this post went up, the Directory's 161 people held 207 of them.

479 had nobody at all.

Energy and utilities, where power systems and voltage control sit, held 3 of its 20 skills that day. Housing and construction, where building design, construction documentation and wiring sit, held 15 of 46. Seven specializations across two of the thinnest sectors on the map is not somebody joining a queue.

No part of the catalog is finished. Retail and services comes closest, at 13 of its 14, and every other sector has more skills nobody can do than skills somebody can.

The app also keeps a shorter list: thirteen jobs a community has to be able to fill before it runs at all. Building and infrastructure is one of the thirteen, and it is one of the thin ones. Power systems, wiring and the drawings a building is built from all land on it.

Foundation, where members offer and ask for work, already carries Electrician as one of its trade filters. The button is there. What it needs is somebody behind it.

## How they came to be listed

They did not sign up. I built the listing from what they said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is why it is marked community-generated and says who made it. Most of the Directory was built that way, one person at a time.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs — what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose trade is in other people's homes and buildings needs things too, and usually from a stranger. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The same listing that makes them findable is what makes those people findable to them. That is the second of the two things, turned around: not nobody else, but somebody.

## Who is on the list

Everyone written about in this series is a self-identified Targeted Individual. That means they said so in public, and it is the only basis for the listing. I cannot verify it, and neither can a reader. Some people who say it are not survivors, and some are the people doing the harassing, so a post here is not a vouch for anybody and should not be read as one. I am not responsible for what anyone named on this list does or says, on the app, on Quora, or anywhere else. The closest anybody can get to finding a real Targeted Individual is through the app, which admits people one at a time through Unlock and has other mechanisms in place to filter actively. Even with those, there is no such thing as a hundred percent.
