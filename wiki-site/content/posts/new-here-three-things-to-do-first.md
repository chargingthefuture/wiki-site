The app has twenty-eight parts. You sign in and land on the Commons, a shared chat, with a grid of everything else one tap away. It is a fair place to not know where to start, and nobody should have to read a guide to find out. So, three things, in the order to do them.

## 1. Send your Quora profile address

Almost everything in the app waits on one step. On the Unlock screen you paste the web address of your own Quora profile and send it. A person reads it; no machine decides. The screen shows what the address should look like, why it is asked for, and where your submission has got to.

If you cannot find the address, the same screen has a button, "Ask for help in the Commons", and you ask there. Nobody is removed for being slow with this, for not finding it, or for asking for help instead. You stay where you are, for as long as it takes.

## 2. While you wait, write

Before approval you can look but not take part, with two doors left open on purpose.

One is under every post on this blog. Fireside is the conversation underneath, and writing there happens in the app: press the button under a post and it carries you across to that exact conversation. What you write is held until you are approved, and the screen says so.

The other is the Knowledge Library, where you lend your own public Quora writing to the app's assistant, so it can answer from more than one person's experience.

Both are open before approval for the same reason. Judging what somebody wrote means looking at a real person writing real things, which is the same look the check asks for, so your writing goes into the same queue as your profile address. Writing is a way in, not something to do while waiting for one.

## 3. Once approved, put your skills on the Directory

The Directory is a list of members and what they can do, and it is the number one thing survivors have asked for. Add your name, a line about yourself if you want one, where you are, and at least one skill. It takes five minutes, once.

Most of the help that happens between members never touches the app and is never recorded in it. The listing is what makes it possible, because it is how another member finds you.

## After that

With whatever is left of a day, there is the goal board: one small task toward somebody else's goal, from a phone, in under half an hour. [Show up with your percent](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/show-up-with-your-percent) says how it works.

And if you forget all of this, the Commons has a chip above the box you type in that reads "I'm new. What do I do first?" Tap it and these three come back on the spot.
