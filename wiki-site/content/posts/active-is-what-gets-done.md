A question I saved on Quora, as its title was recovered from the address it was saved at:

> How and where do I join an active community for targeted individuals

The address it was saved at is https://www.quora.com/How-and-where-do-I-join-an-active-community-for-targeted-individuals. The [Dictionary](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/Dictionary) carries the words I use for this.

The word in it that matters is active.

## Talk is not the measure

A group can post all day and nobody in it has been driven anywhere, had a thing repaired, or been taught a skill. That group is busy. Whether it is active depends on what is being counted.

The measure this project uses is written in the Dictionary. The goal is 384 members exchanging with each other on the same day, and it "counts exchanges that delivered something: a ride given, a repair done, a skill taught, credits sent for work that happened, a task done toward somebody's goal that they said helped. Talk does not count, and neither does signing up."

That is the definition of active I would give the asker. Not how many people are in a group, and not how often they write in it. How much got done between them today.

## What I found when I looked

On February 8, 2026, under a question asking whether there were support groups in Austin, Texas, I wrote: "From what I can tell, there is none. But I started a global network in an attempt to connect the 5 million estimated TIs." And: "With 5 million of us we can easily run our own economy: work, housing, transportation, etc."

On December 22, 2025, answering somebody who had started a space of their own, I described what that network does: "We essentially trade our skills among each other so we can opt out of the psyop."

Trading skills is the activity. A conversation can be part of it, and most of them are, but the conversation is how two people arrange the thing, not the thing.

## Where the doing happens

Talk has a place in the app. The Commons is a group chat of other Targeted Individuals, and it is where questions get answered and people get to know each other.

The doing has its own places. PeerProgramming opens on a goal board: a member posts one goal broken into small tasks, other members take a task and do it, and the goal's owner says whether it helped. It was built for the small part of a day the attacks leave over, so that a goal can be reached by several people's few minutes rather than one person's lost hours ([Show up with your percent](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/show-up-with-your-percent)). Foundation is where members find talent, tools, repairs and support from each other.

## How to join

You join an active community by doing one thing in it. Take a task on somebody's goal. Post a goal of your own. Offer the repair you know how to do.

None of that asks you to talk about the targeting first, or at all. The task is the introduction.

And if you would rather be found than go looking, the Directory lists survivors by what they can do. Being on it costs nobody an account, and it is how the next person who needs your skill finds you.
