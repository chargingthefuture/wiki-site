In the list of problems the app answers, Workforce sits next to three of them: being denied jobs for no good reason, job applications that loop and never submit, and coworkers who turn cold and push you out. Read quickly, that looks like a promise of work. It is not one. Nothing on Workforce hands you a job.

Workforce is a live simulation of individual and community potential. It shows what each of us could do, and what all of us could do together, and where the gaps are between that and today.

## What it shows

The model starts from a population of five million survivors and the share of them who work. I cataloged the jobs, titles and skills a working economy that size needs, 713 skills today, and spread the working population across sectors by how much of an economy each sector makes up. That is the demand side.

The supply side is the Directory. Every active listing is read live and matched by sector, job title, and skill. Nothing is entered twice; when someone adds a skill to their listing, Workforce counts it.

A gap is the goal minus the people listed. The Overview tab puts them under Sector Opportunities, each row reading how many are recruited against the goal and how many are left to fill. The Occupations tab goes one level down, to job titles, largest gap first.

## Why Finland

Right now the model runs against Finland. It is the only place with open data good enough to build it from: a country the right size, with its economy published in enough detail to say what a working population of that size actually does.

When the Directory is full, Workforce stops being a model and becomes a real-time view of how skills are distributed across a population at scale. Finland is the first place that view exists. The Skills Economy will be the second, ever.

## How a gap becomes a living

1. Open Workforce and read the gaps. Sector Opportunities shows where the community is short; Occupations names the job titles.
2. Pick an outcome. It can be the smallest thing that closes part of one gap, or something ambitious. Both count.
3. Fill it yourself. A gap is a market nobody in the community is serving yet. Be the entrepreneur who serves it.
4. Or team up. The people already counted against a sector are listed by name, and each links to their Directory profile. Find somebody who has the skill and join their operation, or bring them into yours.
5. Post the goal on the PeerProgramming goal board. Break it into tasks a member of your cohort can do from a phone in under half an hour, and they take a task and post the result.

## Why that answers those three problems

Traffickers do not hand people material help, so a place organized around giving each other something real filters them out. Being denied jobs, watching applications vanish, being pushed out of a workplace: each is a door somebody else controls. A gap you chose and filled, alone or with a member you found on the Directory, is a living earned without asking that person to open the door.
