The objection to all of this is that a small number of people with no money cannot build anything.

Estonia in 1991 is the answer I keep going back to. When the occupation ended, the country had no money and infrastructure that did not work. What it had was 1.3 million people who already knew how to do things, and it built a functioning modern state out of that over about two generations. [How the math works](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/how-the-math-works) has more on why it is the model.

Having no money is exactly when somebody has to know where the little there is goes.

## Holly

Holly (https://www.quora.com/profile/Holly-D-192), in Atlanta, Georgia. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/cf681670-b288-4b1d-b45d-c2a37b1984bf.

The heading reads Holly, below it "Community-generated profile" and "Nominated by @farah", and under that the location Atlanta, Georgia, United States. A button reads View Quora profile. Under the heading SPECIALIZATIONS are two entries: Financial planning and budgeting; Financial modeling and cashflow management.](images/holly-directory-profile-2026-10-01.jpg)

The two specializations fit together:

- Financial planning and budgeting is deciding, ahead of time, what money is for and how much goes to each thing.
- Financial modeling and cash flow management is knowing when money arrives and when it leaves, so a plan that adds up over a year does not run dry in March.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on October 1, the Directory's 168 people held 227 of them.

Finance and Public Administration is one of the thin sectors: 6 of its 34 skills are held by anybody.

Every plan people make here, a shared house, a business, a community that lasts, has a budget under it whether anybody writes it down or not. The ones that last are the ones where somebody does.

## What this is not

This is not a request to manage anybody's money, and nothing here says Holly will. Being on a list of what a person can do is not an offer of anything, and the Directory has never worked that way.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what Holly said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

Holly was also Member of the Day on December 12, 2025: [Looking for a Financial Advisor? Meet Holly D](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/chargingthefuture/member%20of%20the%20day/looking-for-a-financial-advisor-meet-holly-d).

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs — what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written, including the paragraph about Estonia. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work is other people's budgets needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The same listing that makes them findable is what makes those people findable to them.
