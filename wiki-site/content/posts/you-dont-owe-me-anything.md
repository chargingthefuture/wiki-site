I have been targeted for over thirty years, and in that time the default stance of a trafficker has never once varied: a Targeted Individual owes them something. A complete stranger, somebody they have never met, already owes them — in some way, for some reason, before a word has been exchanged.

This post is about coming to the Skills Economy with the reverse stance, and what that means for what you stand to lose by joining. The short version is: not much.

## Where the stance comes from

Other Targeted Individuals on Quora have described the people who take up trafficking strangers well enough that I do not need to add much: narcissistic, extremely vindictive, indulgent in a depraved way, without integrity or character. The list goes on. The [Dictionary](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/Dictionary) carries the vocabulary for who they are.

The trait that matters here is the one underneath the rest. A person like that meets a stranger and immediately believes that stranger owes them. It is not a conclusion they reach; it is where they start.

And it is how the network holds together. They achieve by theft — force, violence, the taking of what somebody else built — and they receive their positions the same way. Do what the structure asks, whatever it asks: traffic somebody, close an account, moderate a thread, report on a neighbor, spy on a coworker. In exchange you keep the job, the apartment, the house, the salary. The favors come first, on the strength of the slander, and once you have them the terms are plain: keep trafficking or lose them.

I wrote about a stand-up joke [recently](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/something-to-lose) — that the people who commit mass shootings all live with their mother, so the background check should be a mortgage. It is actually the reverse. The traffickers are the ones with the mortgages, and they are the ones producing the mass shootings, because what moved them into it in the first place was the expensive item, the sex, the drugs, the promotion. Those are the things being kept.

So nobody in that network is free. Everybody owes somebody, and everybody is owed. That is the shape of the thing, and it is the shape they carry into every room.

## Somebody in the middle

Here is how it shows up in practice. You work somewhere that has no traffickers in it. Slowly they arrive. I could go through examples and I will not, because it would take over the post — but the reason they have to arrive is simple: their economy only works when one of them sits in the middle of the transaction. Between you and the job, you and the landlord, you and the ride. Sitting there is where the owing gets collected.

The Skills Economy has nobody in the middle. It is peer-to-peer and distributed: you find the person, you arrange it between you, and the app records that it happened. I am the architect of the product, not a party to anything in it.

I will say the honest downside too. Today one person manages the application, and one person is a single point of failure. As the economy grows I expect to spread that across more people, with checks on each other, because there will be more people to spread it across. What one person has going for it in the meantime is that the software already carries a lot of the mechanism — what an admin can and cannot do, what gets recorded, what happens to your data when you leave — and it keeps taking on more as it learns what is needed, so that as little as possible ever depends on somebody sitting in the middle.

## What "you don't owe me anything" means

It means I already hold the stance that not a single person who joins owes me anything. That is not a courtesy. It is the difference between this and the thing it exists to get out of.

When you enter a transactional relationship with a trafficker, they believe they are owed before they have earned anything. That is where the pettiness comes from — the account deleted over nothing, the door closed over nothing. Some of them enjoy it. The ones who do not are following orders, and that is not better, because the order is the same debt being collected one level up.

So here is the line, stated plainly. An account goes for something egregious. A trafficker joining is egregious, and that is a removal. Somebody saying something I do not like, or doing something that annoys me, is not — because that person does not owe me anything, and I do not have to internalize their behavior. Annoying me is subjective. It is not where the line is.

The same holds for the few places where I am the one managing things — a safety report, say. I do not believe any of the parties owe me anything, so there is no consequence for having irritated me on the way in. Mutual benefit is the goal, and the [last post](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/mutual-not-equal) is about what that means between two people. It does not have to be equal. It has to be mutual.

And if you join, like what is happening here or the people who are here, and do not like what I say — you are welcome. You can use every part of the app without a single interaction with me. There is a block feature. Block me.

## No hierarchy, no score

There is no hierarchy in the app, and this has been tested rather than claimed. One member asked me for admin permissions. I am not going to name them, because they are still a member, and the ask is the point rather than the person. What that request asked for was exactly the two things a trafficker's economy needs and this one does not have: a seat in the middle of other people's transactions, and a rank above other survivors. The answer was no, and it will stay no for anybody. There is one admin, who is the architect, and every member is a member. Nobody sits above anybody else.

You do not have to take my word for it. The code is open source at https://github.com/chargingthefuture/chargingthefuture, and anybody can read what an admin can do, what a member can do, and that there is nothing between the two.

The same stand-up set had a second line I remember: nobody with a 739 credit score has ever done a mass shooting. It is the reverse again, for the same reason as the mortgage. The people carrying the good number are the ones inside the favors economy, and the number is one of the favors.

The Skills Economy has no credit score. There is no social ranking, and the [Trust](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/trust) card is a list of plain sentences about what you have actually done — no score, no status, nothing that goes up or down against anybody else. There is exactly one badge, Weavers of the Commons, and you get it one way: by delivering real help to other members over time. It is earned, never bought, and it does not fluctuate. Once it is yours it stays yours; it goes only for cause, the same egregious bar an account goes for, and never because somebody was annoyed. That is the opposite of how the trafficking network hands out its positions, where everything is granted on the strength of the last favor and withdrawn on the next.

## What you stand to lose

Very little, and I want to be exact about why.

Nothing here runs through favors. You are not told to do anything to keep your place. You do not have to show up every day; there is no streak to maintain and nothing that nudges you about one. You can come in when you need something, use one part of the app, ignore the rest, and leave again. Nobody is keeping count against you.

And you have complete autonomy over your own account without contacting me. You can delete what you keep here, plugin by plugin or all of it, from your own account page. The one exception is the ServiceCredits ledger: records of credit movements stay, for auditing, with your identity taken off them. Everything else goes.

So the terms are these. You owe me nothing, I owe you nothing, and the exchange between you and whoever you find here is yours. Very little of how the global economy is arranged — largely by the behavior of traffickers — is reflected in that.
