The [last post](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/you-cannot-argue-a-smear-down) answered a question about false narratives. J H B (https://www.quora.com/profile/J-H-B-7) asked it. This is an invitation, and it starts with what they already do, because that makes the argument better than I can.

## What they can do

The Directory holds nine specializations, and they are broader than the question they asked would suggest.

Chemical dosing and safety. Diagnostic testing — microscopy, biochemistry, hematology. Pharmacology and prescription. Then a second group with nothing to do with medicine at all: audio recording and mixing, multimedia storytelling, video and audio editing, CCTV, and basic accounting and bookkeeping for small businesses.

That is the whole of what the list takes. The Directory records what a person can do, because that is the only part another survivor needs.

And this, which is the line I would have picked if I were writing it for them: you are never alone, just reach out.

## Six years of doing it already

Since January 2020: 363 answers, 225 questions, 62 posts, active in ten spaces and running two of them, answering in the spaces where survivors ask whether any of this is real. Read more than four hundred thousand times.

That is not a stated position. That is somebody doing the work, in public, for six years, with their name on it. The distinction worth drawing in this community has never been who holds an account — it is who is doing rather than saying.

## Medicine is what we are short of

The app carries a catalog of roughly 650 job titles and skills — the set a working economy of about five million people needs. Every profile is matched against that same list, and [only about a quarter of those roles have anyone in them at all](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/your-children-can-have-an-option). The rest of the list is empty rather than competitive.

Medicine is the shortage people feel most. SkillsHunt runs an open mission to find a doctor, on the board because a community this size needs one and does not have one. None of this is being a doctor and I am not going to pretend otherwise. But diagnostic testing is the work that produces the results a doctor reads, and chemical dosing and pharmacology are what stand between a prescription and somebody getting hurt. That is the neighboring ground, and there is nobody standing in it.

Sound is on the list too. So is training somebody else in what you know.

## What using it looks like

Not a support group, and not another place to describe the targeting to people who already live it. A Directory profile states what you can do, and other survivors find you by it. Someone needs to know whether two things they were prescribed should be taken together. Someone needs a recording that does not sound like it was made on a phone in a stairwell. Someone is trying to work out where a camera should go and what it will and will not capture.

It is deliberately transactional, and that is the safety mechanism rather than a coldness. Traffickers do not hand people material help — it is the one thing they consistently will not do — so a place organized around giving each other something real filters them out before anyone has to accuse anybody of anything.

There is a Directory profile for J.H.B. in the app: [app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/2c69e55c-9558-4418-8304-a87590507ee0](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/2c69e55c-9558-4418-8304-a87590507ee0)

The app labels it community-generated and names who made it, because I made it rather than its subject. It exists so other survivors can find them by what they can do. J.H.B., it is yours: claim it by creating an account, or tell me to delete it and it goes. Nobody who has been asked has wanted theirs removed, but the offer is real either way.

And the invitation is not conditional on the account. The listing is the thing survivors actually want — a findable list of people and what they can do. It is already doing its job.
