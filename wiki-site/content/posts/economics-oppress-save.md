In an [earlier post about classified ads for TIs](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/chargingthefuture/guides/scoketrelay/classifed-ads-for-tis), I quoted a writer named John Mixon — not a Targeted Individual, writing about American cities generally. He said that in some communities, organized criminals — the evolutionary offspring of gangs — have significant influence on social and economic matters, and that they know their limits and operate within them to avoid crackdowns.

He was describing a component of what I call the Specterati. He just did not have a name for it.

## Argue about who; you cannot argue about how

Many will say it is the government. Some will always say that, and I am not here to settle it. What nobody can argue with is the how: the operation runs on economics. Jobs lost on cue. Housing that falls through. Sales that die, rides that never come, prices that change when you show up. Whoever sits at the top, the machine underneath is economic — and it works.

That should change how we respond. You do not out-argue a machine. You build a counter-machine.

## The secret society is costume

They tell people they are the Illuminati. It is dressing, worn to create fear.

Look at how it actually recruits. The basis is an evaluation: test whether someone breaks easily. If they fold, fold them in. If they do not, they become a target. That is not initiation into a hidden order — that is churn, at volume. An operation most of a community participates in cannot be a secret society. The secrecy is the story, not the structure.

And the story changes to fit the listener. One survivor is told it is a secret society. Another is told it is God. Another, the government. I was told it is because I act like MLK. Another, that it started when they divorced a narcissist. Different stories, told to keep each of us alone inside our own explanation — while the record shows the same individuals doing the terrorizing.

## If economics works for them, it works for us

Here is the part they would rather we not notice: they have already proven the method. Economic pressure, applied consistently by a network, changes lives. They use it to close doors. The same force, pointed the other way, opens them.

That is the case for the Skills Economy — not as a charity, not as an app for its own sake, but as a barrier. Every skill traded inside the community is a door they cannot close. Work found through the Directory, help exchanged through Foundation, a vetted ride, a listing from a trauma-informed host — each one moves a piece of your life out of the machine's reach. Call it economic reform if you like. I call it the obvious move: the thing that oppresses us is economics, so economics is the thing that saves us.

Exit their economy, exit the psyop.
