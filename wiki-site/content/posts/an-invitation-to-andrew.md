A person is not one job title.

The skills map counts somebody at every level and in every sector their skills reach, so the same person can show up in several places at once ([Counted by skills, not job titles](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/counted-by-skills)). Andrew Maze's listing is one trade, keeping machines and tools working. A mine, a food plant, a utility, a factory and a laboratory all run on equipment somebody has to keep in order, so the catalog files that trade in five sectors, and one listing can fill a gap in all five.

## Andrew Maze

Andrew Maze (https://www.quora.com/profile/andrew-maze-5), in the United States. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/7bf0650d-163a-4c23-9029-25ef25528331.

The listing carries six specializations:

- Equipment maintenance, and equipment operation and maintenance: running machines and keeping them in repair.
- Preventive maintenance, and preventive maintenance planning: servicing machines on a schedule so they do not break down, and setting that schedule.
- Equipment calibration and maintenance: keeping instruments measuring true, and in working order.
- Tool selection and maintenance: choosing the right tool for a job, and keeping it sound.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on October 14, the Directory's 321 people held 260 of them.

Counted by skills, Andrew is in five sectors:

- Mining and Extractive, where equipment maintenance is filed under technicians, and 1 of 12 skills is held.
- Food and Agriculture, where equipment operation and maintenance is filed under food-processing workers, and 6 of 49 are held.
- Energy and Utilities, where preventive maintenance is filed under maintenance crews, and 6 of 20 are held.
- Manufacturing and Industry, where preventive maintenance planning is filed under maintenance engineers and tool selection and maintenance under machinists, and 16 of 28 are held.
- R&D and High-Tech, where equipment calibration and maintenance is filed under lab technicians, and 23 of 42 are held.

A title would put Andrew in one of those boxes. The skills put them in all five, and the map counts every one.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what Andrew said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs: what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work is keeping other people's machines running needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The listing that makes them findable makes those people findable to them.
