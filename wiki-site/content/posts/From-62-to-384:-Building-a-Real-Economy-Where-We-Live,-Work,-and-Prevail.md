# From 62 to 384: Building a Real Economy Where We Live, Work, and Prevail
We have the potential to reach 5 million TIs (human trafficking survivors) who come together to reclaim our lives. But today, I want to focus on the power of right here, right now. We have 411 people in our Quora community. Of those, 62 have already stepped forward to join the mission. My goal is simple: to connect with 384 people who are ready to thrive. With 411 Quora members, that milestone is not just possible—it is within our grasp.
But what does a circular economy actually look like when it starts with just 411 of us? Does it require high-tech skills or distant corporations? No. It requires the everyday skills we already possess.

# The Power of Everyday Skills
If we apply our model of mutual support to this group of 411, the growth potential is profound. We aren't waiting for permission to succeed. We are building a self-sustaining loop of care and value right now.
* Total Economic Activity: In a fully realized state, this group (384–411 people) could generate $24.66 million in annual activity.
* The Service Engine: 70% of this ($17.26 million) comes from the services we provide each other. This is the heartbeat of our community.
* The Local Loop: The remaining 30% ($7.4 million) covers local goods, food, and essentials, keeping our resources close to home.

# Real People, Real Value: What You Can Earn

Imagine a community where another TI’s skills become your lifeline, and your skills become theirs. This isn't about abstract numbers; it's about dignity and connection. Based on our economic model, here is what a sustainable income looks like for three essential roles in our 384-person community:
* The Caregiver: One of us provides daily support and companionship to an elderly neighbor or someone recovering from trauma. Working a steady 25 hours a week at a community rate of $25/hour, this caregiver earns $32,500 in ServiceCredits annually. This income secures their housing and well-being, while ensuring their neighbor is safe and cared for.
* The Mechanic: Another member fixes cars for the community. Instead of paying a distant chain, neighbors pay in ServiceCredits. Working 30 hours a week at $35/hour (reflecting the specialized skill and tool costs). This mechanic earns $54,600 in ServiceCredits annually. This allows them to buy parts, upgrade their tools, and support their family, knowing their work keeps the whole community moving.
* The Babysitter: A parent offers childcare to other parents in the group, allowing them to work or rest. Working 15 hours a week at $20/hour. This babysitter earns $15,600 in ServiceCredits annually. This flexible income builds trust, frees up time for everyone to contribute more, and ensures the babysitter has the resources they need.

Note: ServiceCredits are not mandatory within the TI Skills Economy. You can offer free, barter, or fiat if desired. Learn more about ServiceCredits here:

# The Math of Connection
My commitment is to meet 384 TIs who are ready to be part of this.
* We have 411 people in this Quora community.
* We need 384 to say "yes."
* We have 62 who have joined.

This isn't a theoretical exercise. It is a reflection of our collective strength. Every time one of the 62 current members welcomes a new person, the economy grows. Every time we fix a car, watch a child, or care for a neighbor, the GDP rises.

# Why 411 is Enough to Start

Some might say 411 is too small to matter. But history shows us that resilience begins in small, tight-knit groups.
* Authenticity: In a group of 411, we know each other. Trust is built on real interactions, not algorithms.
* Clarity: We can see exactly how our contributions help. We know who needs what, and we can act immediately.
* Unity: When we face challenges, we don't face them alone. We have a network of support that understands our journey.
