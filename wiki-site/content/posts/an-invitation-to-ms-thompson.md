You already have a profile.

I built it from what you said about yourself in public and put it up under your name. It has been there, findable, marked community-generated, with my handle on it as the person who made it. That is how most of this list was made, one person at a time, from what people already said about themselves where anyone could read it.

I would rather tell you that than leave you to find it.

## Ms Thompson

Ms Thompson (https://www.quora.com/profile/ms-thompson-11), in the United States. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/a9cf7c99-cd65-47bb-96ef-30b7a6dfb37d.

The listing carries two kinds of work.

The first is legal:

- Case preparation and negotiation: getting a case ready, and settling it where it can be settled.
- Contract and statutory interpretation: reading what a contract or a law actually says.
- Legal advocacy and advice: standing up for somebody, and telling them where they stand.

The second is hands-on:

- Mechanical and electrical repair: fixing the machines and the wiring a building or a business runs on.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on October 14, the Directory's 312 people held 260 of them.

Where those skills sit in the catalog:

- The legal skills are filed under lawyers in Public Safety and Justice, where 4 of 22 are held.
- Mechanical and electrical repair is filed under maintenance crews in Energy and Utilities, where 6 of 20 are held, and under maintenance engineers in Manufacturing and Industry, where 16 of 28 are held.

Most of what people offer one another on the list is a trade done on trust. Sooner or later one of those arrangements needs writing down in terms both sides understand, and somebody who can read a contract is part of what makes that possible.

## What this is not

This is not a request for anybody's legal advice or repairs, and nothing here says Ms Thompson will give either. Being on a list of what a person can do is not an offer of anything, and the Directory has never worked that way.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs: what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work is other people's disputes and other people's machines needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The listing that makes them findable makes those people findable to them.
