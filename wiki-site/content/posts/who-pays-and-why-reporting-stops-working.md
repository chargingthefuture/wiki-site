A question on Quora, saved on October 31, 2025:

> Who pays the gang stalkers to stalk and harass you and why don't the police want to help?

The question is at https://www.quora.com/Who-pays-the-gang-stalkers-to-stalk-and-harass-you-and-why-don-t-the-police-want-to-help. While nobody has answered it, Quora serves it at https://www.quora.com/unanswered/Who-pays-the-gang-stalkers-to-stalk-and-harass-you-and-why-don-t-the-police-want-to-help instead. The [Dictionary](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/Dictionary) carries the words I use for this; the question keeps the words it was asked in.

## Who pays

Most of the people taking part are not paid in money. They are paid in kind: a job, an apartment, friends, a favor, sex, drugs. Somebody is told a story about the target, is swayed by it, and is offered something for acting on it. [Who teaches them](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/who-teaches-them) and [An epidemic, not a secret society](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/an-epidemic-not-a-secret-society) go into how that works.

The money is further up. The people running this launder it through the ordinary economy, and placing their own people in positions is about control and money. [Why nobody does anything](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/why-nobody-does-anything) says more. A person's exploitation is worth something to whoever is doing it.

I do not name one payer. My position is that it is a great many ordinary people in a distributed network of organized crime, so there is no single door to knock on. In my own case it is not a government: private groups use the same method, and mine started when I was five years old ([Picked off early](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/picked-off-early)).

What keeps any one person in it is what they were given and what leaving would cost them: the job, the apartment, the standing that came with going along. [Why people leave, and what it costs them](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/why-people-leave-and-what-it-costs) is about that.

## Why reporting stops working

I have called the police on individual incidents, and gone to a station over one. While I was there, an officer mocked a call I had made to a national hotline back to me, in person ([The Assistant Is Not a Clinician](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/the-assistant-is-not-a-clinician)).

A police report records an incident. What a single report was not built to hold is the pattern: many people, many days, each act small enough to explain away on its own. And a report describes something the people around you can all contradict, and will, because they were asked to. [Who to report it to](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/who-to-report-it-to) goes into what a report can and cannot carry.

Sherri Jenkins (https://www.quora.com/profile/Sherri-Jenkins-12) wrote that targets readily report law enforcement ignoring their pleas for help and taking part, along with firefighters, utility workers and post office employees. Her description is in [The best description of it I have read](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/best-description-i-have-read). Every one of those is somebody a person's week runs through.

None of this is legal advice, and it does not tell anybody what to do when a report goes nowhere.

## What that leaves

That list is the reason the answer here is an economy rather than an appeal. An operation that pays in jobs, rooms and favors has an economy underneath it, and an economy is a thing you can decline to take part in: depending on nobody who is being paid to harm you. [They Are Not a Secret Society](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/they-are-not-a-secret-society) made that case first.
