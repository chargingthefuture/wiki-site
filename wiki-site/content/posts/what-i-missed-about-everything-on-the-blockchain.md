Anybody who spent time around crypto before about 2022 has met the person who wants everything on a blockchain. Not just money — the whole application. Put the rental marketplace on chain. Put the ride sharing on chain. Put all of it on chain.

My position then was that this was tunnel vision. Crypto is useful for the financial part of a transaction. It is not necessary to run the software around the transaction, and bolting a chain onto a booking system makes the booking system worse. Somebody would say "put a rental marketplace on the blockchain" and I would think: you do not need that, you need a payment you can make without asking permission.

I have not been active in that space for a few years. In the narrow technical sense I still hold most of that position.

What I got wrong was the motive.

## What changed

I have been targeted since I was five years old. For most of that time it was covert — I could see it, but they did not want me to be certain. On January 1, 2023 it went overt, which is a deliberate stage: they want you to know it is happening, and it runs continuously.

Correction, 2026-09-18: this originally gave the date as January 1, 2024. The year was wrong; the overt phase restarted on January 1, 2023.

Living inside that changes what certain sentences sound like.

"I want this on a blockchain" is one of them. Read it again from inside a system where people can reach into your accounts, your records, your access, and your standing, and adjust them at will. It stops sounding like enthusiasm for a technology. It starts sounding like somebody saying: I want a thing that nobody can reach into.

I am not claiming that everybody who wants this is a survivor of organized crime. Plenty are engineers who find the technology interesting, and some are the people doing the harassing. Both are true. What I am saying is that there is a third reading I did not have access to before, and I suspect it is more common than the conversation lets on. The person is not asking for a database. They are asking to be out of reach.

## Why I am thinking about it now

On Thursday, September 10, I had a conversation with somebody on the app who is a Specterati operator. I wrote separately about what they said about the community, in [Nobody Calls to Tell You Your Project Is Dead](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/nobody-calls-to-say-its-dead), and I will not repeat it here.

The part that belongs in this post is what they asked for: advanced permissions in the app.

They wanted a lever. Somewhere in the system there would be a control that lets one person affect another person's experience — the ability to promote, restrict, approve, feature, moderate, or remove. That is the thing worth having if your purpose is to reach people. It is also the thing every platform hands to somebody.

There is no such control here, and the request is how I know what they came for. Anybody who has used the app for a week knows there is nothing to ask for, because everything is already available to them.

## What it actually means to have no lever

The Skills Economy is a peer-to-peer network. Two people arrange something between themselves, on their own terms, and the app's job is logistics — helping them find each other and keep track — not sitting in the middle of the exchange as a referee.

That works because of who it is for. It is for adults, all of whom are targeted, and every one of us is somewhere different in that experience. The schemes vary, and so does the technology used against any given person. Directed Energy Weapons are part of it for me now and were not when I was young; for somebody else the timeline is completely different. From outside the operation you cannot know exactly why, though it appears that the higher somebody is on the list, the more severe it gets. A person in that situation does not need a moderator deciding what they are ready for. They need to be able to act on their own read of their own life.

So, concretely:

There is no permission ladder. When somebody is approved, they have everything. There is no second tier to reach, nothing to be granted later, and no way to pay me for more.

You cannot buy access and you cannot buy influence over somebody else's account. Not for money, not for credits, not for being helpful to me personally.

The audio room is open. Anybody can listen without an account; signing in is what lets you speak. That much transparency early costs an operator a great deal, because fraudulent behavior is visible from the first minute rather than after somebody has been harmed.

Going further than the open room requires the other person to agree. A one-to-one conversation cannot be started cold — it is an invitation that has to be accepted, it only exists while both people are already in the room, and declining sends the person who asked nothing at all. Paid work runs through the Foundation board, in the open, rather than through a private channel somebody talked their way into.

What an operator is after is harm — a person's degradation is the material they extract value from. None of the moves that produce it survive this structure for long, and what does get through is small.

## What exiting actually costs, said honestly

I am not going to pretend the exit is complete, because it is not, and a person deciding what to do with their life deserves the real number.

Some people cannot leave the wider economy even if they want to. If you have a partner, children, or family depending on you, exiting means all of them exiting, and the Skills Economy cannot meet every one of their needs yet. That is a limit of what exists so far, not a failure of nerve on their part.

Some people do not want to, which is a complete answer and needs no defense.

And some things are simply not made here. The Skills Economy does not manufacture phones. If you want the current hardware and the current goods, you are going to obtain them through the ordinary economy, which means through people who are part of what is being done to us — and the device itself is compromised either way. Keeping that is keeping one foot in. It can be a reasonable trade. It should be a knowing one.

## Back to the blockchain

None of this runs on a chain, and it does not need to.

When two people arrange an exchange here, the exchange happens between them. A driver and a rider settle it however they both agree — crypto, ordinary money, a straight barter, or free, which is a real and common option rather than a rounding error. The app records that something was exchanged. It does not sit in the middle of it, take a cut, or hold anybody's funds.

That is the thing the maximalists were reaching for, in my reading of them now. Not a specific data structure. A system with no seat in the middle for somebody to occupy.

You can get there by putting everything on a chain. You can also get there by building software that never creates the seat in the first place, and by refusing to add one when somebody asks you for it — which somebody will, and recently did.
