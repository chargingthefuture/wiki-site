Most of a Targeted Individual's day is not theirs. What is left at the end of it might be five percent, and some days it is nothing. Five percent is not enough to reach a goal alone, but twenty members with five percent of a day each is a working day ([Show up with your percent](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/show-up-with-your-percent)).

That is what the Goals board in PeerProgramming runs on. Somebody posts one goal with a finish line and breaks it into cards: small tasks another member could do from a phone in under half an hour. Anybody picks any one card, does it, and posts what they found. [The Lit Country](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/the-lit-country) draws the same thing as a map, where each card done lights a road from one hearth to another.

Most cards need no trade at all. A skill makes some tasks possible that would not be otherwise, and AlteHexer's is programming.

## AlteHexer

AlteHexer (https://www.quora.com/profile/AlteHexer), in the United States. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/74ee6949-5a7e-48cf-95ed-b45e6119ea65.

The listing carries one specialization:

- Programming and software design: writing the instructions a computer follows, and planning how a program fits together before any of it is written.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on October 14, the Directory's 321 people held 260 of them.

Programming and software design is filed under software developers in Telecommunications and IT, where 4 of 20 are held.

A goal that needs software built is hard to break into cards for anybody who cannot tell what its small parts are. Somebody who designs software can.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what AlteHexer said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs: what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not taking a card, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work is building things for other people needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The listing that makes them findable makes those people findable to them, and a goal of their own can go on the board, where other people's time arrives the same way.
