Targeted Individuals bring up COINTELPRO all the time. It was the FBI's program for watching, infiltrating and discrediting American groups, and many survivors say it is what they are living in now. What I almost never see next to it is the name of its best-documented target: Martin Luther King Jr.

The people doing this to us do not draw that line, because drawing it admits what they are.

I think survivors leave it alone for a different reason: King's case admits this is not only a game a government plays. The FBI went after him, and so did private people who wanted him stopped. In a Gallup poll in August 1966, 63 percent of Americans held an unfavorable view of him, and neither government nor business counted itself among his friends. Even so, he had far more allies than a Targeted Individual has today: churches, a Black press, colleges, unions, and thousands of people willing to march.

The FBI's part has been told at length, by survivors and in the Senate's own record. The private part is the one left out, and it is mine. I am not targeted by a government. Private groups use the same method, and mine started when I was five years old.

## What the record says

The Senate's Church Committee put the FBI's campaign against him on the public record in 1976. The Bureau wiretapped his home and offices, bugged his hotel rooms, and in November 1964 mailed him an anonymous letter, with a recording, urging him to kill himself. [What organized crime means](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/what-organized-crime-means) covers that, and why I call what happens to me organized crime rather than a government program.

The same report quotes a 1968 FBI memo listing the goals of its program against Black organizations. One of them was to "prevent the rise of a 'messiah'" who could unite them. The memo named King as one who could.

That is a plan to stop a leader before there is one.

## Being called a leader

I have been accused of being a leader. It is a strange thing to say as an insult, and I read it as the same plan, adjusted. King had years to build allies before anyone moved against him. The version I see now moves sooner: find the people who could organize others and cannot be controlled, and deal with them early, so an opposition that is not a grift never forms.

## What he did without social media

He had the national press and television, and the movement planned its actions around them. What carried it were methods that need no platform, and most of them can be built again.

1. Networks people already trusted. The movement ran through Black churches, which already had meeting rooms, phone trees, money and leaders people believed. It joined audiences that already existed instead of building one. The closest thing here is the Directory, and one person recommending another.
2. Hand-to-hand distribution. The Montgomery bus boycott was announced on mimeographed leaflets, tens of thousands of them, printed overnight by the Women's Political Council under Jo Ann Robinson and passed along through churches and workplaces. The share messages on the [Peace Battle 2](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/peace-battle-2) page work the same way: a different one for each person, posted under their own name.
3. One campaign with one clear demand. Montgomery was the buses. Birmingham was the downtown stores and their jobs. Selma was the vote. Each had a place, an ask and a finish line.
4. Mutual aid that made it last. The boycott held for 381 days because the movement ran its own carpool, a few hundred volunteer cars with pickup points and dispatchers. People stopped paying the system and carried each other instead. That is what trading with each other instead of with the people targeting us means, and TrustTransport is the same carpool.
5. Training and a pledge. Volunteers rehearsed answering abuse before they faced it, and in Birmingham they signed a commitment card with ten rules of conduct. Nobody joins the app without a check either.
6. People who trained others. Septima Clark's citizenship schools taught ordinary people to teach reading and voter registration, and they taught others in turn. Ella Baker, Bayard Rustin and many more did the organizing. King was the voice, not the only worker, which is the one defense against a plan to remove a leader early.
7. A written record that outlived the moment. The Letter from Birmingham Jail was started in the margins of a newspaper and smuggled out. His books, sermons and recorded speeches are why he is studied now. This blog, its copies in the Internet Archive, and ClickLog are the record here.
8. Carrying on in the open. The surveillance had little to use against disciplined conduct and public work. Every change to this project is on the public record for the same reason.

## The difference

He had institutions that already existed and a shared identity that let people find each other. Targeted Individuals are spread across countries, have no shared institution, and have good reason to distrust new ones. The Directory and this blog are an attempt to build one.
