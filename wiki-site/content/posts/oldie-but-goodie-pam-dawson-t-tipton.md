Back in January I published a short post of shoutouts to survivors giving genuinely good advice on Quora, and said it would keep growing as I found more of you: [🤜🤛 Skills, Solidarity, and Support](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/chargingthefuture/%F0%9F%A4%9C%F0%9F%A4%9B-Skills%2C-Solidarity%2C-and-Support). An oldie but a goodie. It grows today.

[Pam Dawson](https://www.quora.com/profile/Pam-Dawson-32) and [T. Tipton](https://www.quora.com/profile/T-Tipton): thank you for your advice on Quora. You are appreciated.

## The profiles

Check out the profiles I made for them in the Directory.

![Pam Dawson's community-generated directory profile](images/pam-dawson-directory-profile.jpeg)

![T. Tipton's community-generated directory profile](images/t-tipton-directory-profile.jpeg)

Why make profiles for them? To make it easy to discover survivors to connect with. Discovery on Quora is not good — good voices get buried in the feed, and finding a survivor with a specific skill is close to impossible. A directory profile fixes that: skills stated in their own words, in public, placed on the skills map where anyone in the community can find them.

Each profile is labeled community-generated and names who made it. It links back to their Quora profile as the social proof — read their own words before you reach out. And it is theirs: claimable by its subject at any time, deletable on request.

## A worked sample

One of my own goals is a yard jockey job in Texas. Here it is placed on the Skills Taxonomy, with how many people on the skills map hold each skill it needs.

![A worked sample, details removed. The goal: A yard jockey job in Texas. Sector: Transport & Logistics. Job title: Truck Drivers. Vehicle operation and safety, 2 on the skills map. Load securement, 4 on the skills map. Cards anyone can take, from a phone, in under half an hour: Find three yards hiring near Dallas and add their numbers. Ask one yard whether they hire new Class A holders without OTR miles. Find one warehouse in Houston that trains its own yard drivers. Counts: people on the skills map who hold that skill, 2026-10-13. Nobody is named.](images/yard-jockey-texas-sample-2026-10-13.png)

The cards above are how a goal goes on the PeerProgramming Goals board, free in the Skills Economy, where anyone in the cohort can take one. [One Percent](https://farahbrunache.com) is the paid version of the same work, one-to-one: $7 for a first 30-minute session, after a short automated call, where the people behind these counts are matched by skill and introduced.
