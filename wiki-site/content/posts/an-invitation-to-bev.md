The objection to all of this is that a small number of people with no money cannot build anything.

Estonia in 1991 is the answer I keep going back to. When the occupation ended, the country had no money and infrastructure that did not work. What it had was 1.3 million people who already knew how to do things, and it built a functioning modern state out of that over about two generations. [How the math works](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/how-the-math-works) has more on why it is the model.

Infrastructure that does not work gets fixed by people who can read a drawing and make the part.

## Bev

Bev (https://www.quora.com/profile/Bev-419), in Canada. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/41cb5f1c-c564-493b-b0af-3b2a250349b9.

Nearly every specialization on the listing is one trade, seen from different stages of the work:

- Blueprint reading and interpretation, and technical drawing interpretation: knowing from a drawing what a part has to be.
- Quality control and precision work: machining it to that drawing.
- Manufacturing, and assembly and packaging: putting the pieces together and getting them out the door.
- Equipment troubleshooting: finding out why the machine stopped, and getting it going again.
- Product inspection and testing, and quality checks: confirming that what came out is what the drawing said.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on October 11, the Directory's 311 people held 255 of them.

Manufacturing and Industry is 28 of those skills, and 14 of them are held by anybody. The occupations Bev's listing touches, machinists, production workers, maintenance engineers and quality inspectors, are the ones that turn a plan into an object.

Most of what a community needs to stand on its own is a physical thing somebody has to make, or keep running.

## What this is not

This is not a request to make anything for anybody, and nothing here says Bev will. Being on a list of what a person can do is not an offer of anything, and the Directory has never worked that way.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what Bev said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs: what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody who can make and fix things needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The listing that makes them findable makes those people findable to them.

I cannot verify it, and neither can a reader.
