This is a Request For Skills, and it is the first one. The format is simple: name what this economy does not have, and ask people to go find it.

The first request is teaching and childcare.

## Why these two

The [last post](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/what-about-school) worked through schooling for a survivor community and reached a plain conclusion about childcare: it sits close to babysitting, the app already handles that shape end to end, and there is nothing to build. The gap is people.

Teaching is the same. A community arrives with children in it, and neither the teaching nor the care of them is covered by anybody currently on the map.

## What to pick

The list now carries both, under Education. Two occupations were added and one skill joined an occupation that already existed:

Childcare Workers — childcare and babysitting, infant and toddler care, after-school care, special needs care, child first aid and CPR.

Tutors — tutoring, and homeschooling and curriculum planning.

Vocational Trainers — adult education and training.

That second one under Tutors is worth stopping on. The last post asked whether homeschooling is what people are actually seeking and said I did not know. Somebody who homeschools can now be listed as somebody who homeschools, which is a better way to find out than asking.

## How to answer it

[SkillsHunt](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/skills-hunt). You nominate somebody: a name, where they are, and what they can do. You do not have to be certain the person is a survivor — the screen says so outright. An accepted nomination grants the scout credits, once per nomination and never twice.

The mechanics are all in [an earlier post](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/finding-people-earns-credits).

Nominate people who teach, tutor, or look after children. Also nominate anyone else. A request naming two things is not a restriction to two things — the catalog runs to roughly 650 job titles and skills, and only about a quarter of them have anybody in them at all.

## The part that makes it worth doing

The credits an accepted nomination grants you are the same credits members exchange with each other for housing, rides, requests and services. So a parent who finds and nominates a teacher earns the thing they would then exchange with a teacher.

That loop is the whole design, and it is worth being exact about where it currently stands: it does not close yet. The categories exist now, and nobody is in them. That is not a hole in the argument — it is the argument. The credits are worth what the map has on it, and the map is what a request like this is asking you to fill.

For a targeted family, closing that loop is a serious thing. Teaching and care are two of the largest recurring costs a household carries, and they are among the first things to become impossible when somebody's income is being taken from them on purpose.

## Public assistance, and why there is none

Where a Targeted Individual can get help is the question this whole app exists to answer, so it is not the narrow one worth asking here. The narrow one is public assistance, and it comes up constantly.

Public assistance means something particular. It is necessary goods and services — somewhere to live, food, care for a child, medicine — supplied free or at a reduced cost to people who cannot otherwise obtain them. Not advice, not a referral, not a number to call. The thing itself, for nothing or for less.

For somebody trafficked and harassed by the Specterati, there is none of it. No agency has a category for this, no form has a box for it, and no benefit is administered against the crime as survivors describe it.

Skills Economy is not public assistance and I am not going to call it that. It is not a government program, not a benefit, not an entitlement, nobody is owed anything by it, and there are legal reasons not to claim it is equivalent to one.

What it does is the same shape of thing, reached from the opposite direction. Necessary goods and services move to the people who need them — a place to stay, a ride, a child looked after, a subject taught — and they move on credits, which are earned by doing something for somebody else rather than bought. So a member obtains what they need without money changing hands. That is comparable to what public assistance delivers. It is not administered to recipients by a program; it is supplied by survivors to each other.

It is also the only thing I know of catered to this specifically. Not a general service that hands a survivor a leaflet, but people who already know what is being done to you and do not need convincing first.

That is why a request like this matters more than it looks. Nobody is going to build this list for us.
