Most of a Targeted Individual's day is not theirs. What is left at the end of it might be five percent, and some days it is nothing. Five percent is not enough to reach a goal alone. Twenty members with five percent of a day each is a working day ([Show up with your percent](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/show-up-with-your-percent)).

That is what the Goals board in PeerProgramming runs on. Somebody posts one goal with a finish line and breaks it into cards: small tasks somebody else could do from a phone in under half an hour. [The Lit Country](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/the-lit-country) draws it as a map: your five percent goes on one card, and a road lights from your hearth to theirs.

A skill is what makes a card possible to do. Adored Orange is listed for one that breaks into pieces that size.

The listing carries one specialization:

- Creative technique and portfolio development: making the work, getting better at the craft, and choosing and arranging pieces so somebody else can see what an artist can do.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on October 14, the Directory's 321 people held 260 of them.

Creative technique and portfolio development is filed under artists and illustrators in Creative and Media, where 36 of 92 are held.

An artist's eye does not need a free afternoon to be useful. A goal on the board can need it in pieces small enough for a card: which of three photos of something for sale to lead with, how a one-page flyer should be laid out, which of two sketches reads at a glance. Each of those is a half hour from a phone, and each one is somebody else's goal a step closer.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what Adored Orange said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

Adored Orange also asked on Quora what happens to a society as the number of targeted people grows. My answer is [Count the recruits](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/count-the-recruits).

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs: what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not taking a card, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work is making things worth looking at needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The listing that makes them findable makes those people findable to them, and anybody can post a goal of their own on the board: other people's time arrives at their door the same way.
