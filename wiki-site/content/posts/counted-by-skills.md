## What the model expects

The model is built for an economy of about five million people, on the pattern of small advanced nation states like Finland, Estonia and Singapore. Its goals for a working population of two million split this way:

- Foundational: 157,590, about 8 percent
- Intermediate: 1,352,924, about 68 percent
- Advanced: 489,482, about 24 percent

That is the usual shape. Most people work in the middle, with advanced and foundational work far behind.

## What the list shows

The first people on the list matched it. In December 2025 I wrote that most of them fell into the intermediate level.

At 311 recruited, the three are nearly even: 248 at foundational, 306 at intermediate, 300 at advanced.

The numbers add up to more than 311 because the chart counts a person at every level their skills reach. Almost everybody on the list reaches intermediate and advanced work, and most reach foundational work as well.

## Why

The Skills Economy records what people can do, not the job title they last held or the status that came with it. And what people can do crosses titles. One listing carries laboratory testing and packaging. Another carries blueprint reading, machining and quality inspection. A title would put each of those people in one box. Their skills put them in several, and the chart counts all of them.

That is also why this may hold well beyond us. Most countries count their workforce by job, one person to one title. Counted by skills, other industrialized countries may look the way this list does.

## What it means for us

Know-how that moves this freely between levels means we do not need anybody else's structure to cover the work. We can reach autonomy from the people trafficking us by acting together.

The past is the past, and none of us can get it back. We can focus on the here and now, and rebuild.
