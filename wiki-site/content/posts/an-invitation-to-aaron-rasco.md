A person is not one job title.

The skills map counts somebody at every level and in every sector their skills reach, so the same person can show up in more than one place. What people can do crosses titles, and [Counted by skills, not job titles](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/counted-by-skills) goes into what that does to the count.

Aaron's listing reaches two sectors that have little to do with each other on paper.

## Aaron

Aaron (https://www.quora.com/profile/Aaron-Rasco-5), in Colorado Springs, Colorado. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/0ad8b334-e757-4384-8034-6cc90b605077.

The listing carries two specializations:

- Loading and unloading vehicles: getting goods onto a truck or van and off it again, so what was sent arrives in one piece.
- Local knowledge and interpretation: knowing a place well, and explaining it to somebody who does not.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on October 14, the Directory's 321 people held 260 of them.

Counted by skills, Aaron is in two sectors:

- Transport and Logistics, where loading and unloading is filed under package handlers and warehouse workers, and 20 of 37 skills are held.
- Tourism and Hospitality, where local knowledge and interpretation is filed under tour guides, and 8 of 16 are held.

A move lands a survivor somewhere they know nobody, and the question worth asking before choosing a city is who is there when they arrive ([Move toward somebody](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/move-toward-somebody)). A move means things loaded at one end and unloaded at the other, and a place nobody has explained yet. This listing carries both.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what Aaron said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs: what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work is moving other people's things needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The listing that makes them findable makes those people findable to them.
