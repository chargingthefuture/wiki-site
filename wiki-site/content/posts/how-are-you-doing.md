There is a thing I want to name, because once you can see it you cannot stop seeing it, and it explains a category of interaction that otherwise reads as confusing or even friendly.

Performed kindness. Kind words, said in a way that is not kind. The words are the cover and the delivery is the content.

## What it looks like

Somebody holds a door for me and then says something as I go through it. The door was never the point — walking close enough to say the thing was. If I had opened it myself there would have been no opening to speak into.

Somebody walks past a woman and tells her she should smile more. Men stand around with the same face on and are never told. It is said to women, it is framed as encouragement, and it is an instruction from a stranger about what her face should be doing.

In a public laundry room, somebody took issue with me for moving a stranger's washing out of a machine and not putting it in a dryer. Every washer was full. Nobody is supposed to leave clothes sitting in a public laundry for hours, and when they do, the clothes get moved — that is how the room works, and moving them is not taking them. I did not put the load in a dryer on purpose. You do not run a dryer on somebody else's clothes unless you know them and know how they do their washing. Heat shrinks things. Some of it should be on a lower setting and some of it should not go in at all.

So the complaint was that I had failed to do the thing that would have wrecked a stranger's clothes.

That is the shape of all of it. Saying something costs nothing. It buys the appearance of being a good person, by their definition, and it usually comes with somebody watching.

## And then the question

Which brings me to the one in the title.

Their raw material is your pain. Not your work, not your time, not anything you produce. What they are collecting is how badly you are doing, and they collect it by asking.

So when the question arrives from that direction, it is not concern. It is a meter reading. A bad answer is a good day for them.

I want to be exact here, because this is the part that gets misread. This is not about Targeted Individuals asking each other how they are doing. That is an ordinary human thing to say and most people mean it. No survivor has ever asked me it in bad faith, and if you have asked me it, nothing in this post is aimed at you.

What I am describing is one direction only, and what I am announcing is my own choice, for myself.

## Why the raw material changed

It is worth being precise about what is being taken, because the answer is not what most people assume.

The transatlantic slave trade wanted labor. That was the entire economic mechanism: take the work for free, drive the cost of goods down, sell them, and never pay a wage. Horrific, and legible — the value being extracted was work, and the bondage existed to keep the work coming.

That is not a comparison and I am not making one. I am pointing at one difference, and only one.

What is done to Targeted Individuals now runs the opposite way. Your work is the first thing destroyed. References dry up, the role is restructured, the contract is not renewed, the business is driven to zero. If your labor were the valuable thing, nobody would spend years making sure you cannot do any.

You are pushed out of the economy and you still have every skill you had. The skills were never what was wanted.

So the raw material changed. It is the suffering itself — and an economy built on that has to keep producing it, which is why the harassment is continuous rather than aimed at anything in particular.

Slavery at least binds a person to labor, because the labor is the product. Here there is no labor anybody wants. That is a structural fact about the arrangement, not a ranking of whose suffering was worse, and I am not interested in that argument.

## Why I am not Dr. King

They tell me I act like Martin Luther King Jr.

I do not, and it is not modesty to say so. I have accomplished nothing next to what he did, and I am not on the same mission. When they say it, they are not describing me. They are describing what they want, or what they have already been doing to me since I was five.

But there is a real difference underneath, and it is worth writing down, because it is a difference about strategy rather than about him.

His last campaign was economic. Wages, jobs, a union for sanitation workers in Memphis, a Poor People's Campaign built on economic demands. The ask was to be let in, on fair terms, to a system that was refusing. He was killed in the middle of that work.

My ask is not to be let in.

I am not asking to be integrated into an arrangement run by people who have spent my whole life taking things from me. I do not want a seat at that table on fair terms. I want a different table, built by people who are not doing this, where what I can do is worth something to somebody who is also not doing this.

Coexistence over conspiracy.

That is a contrast, not a criticism. He was answering the question in front of him and the answer he reached was the right one for it. The question in front of me is different: the arrangement I am excluded from does not want my labor and will not trade for it, so being let in buys nothing.

## Coexistence over conspiracy

The other half of that phrase is about where the conversation goes.

I could spend the rest of my life on which organization it is, which agency, which person, who started it. I have written about who and why, and I will again — but only where it connects to what to do next. On its own it is a hole you can stand in for a decade.

My honest position is that it is not one organization. It is a great many ordinary people inside a distributed network of organized crime, and survivors are outnumbered by something like three hundred to one at the lowest estimate I can defend. There is no single door to knock on.

So I would rather talk about the other thing. Building somewhere to live and work among people who are not part of it. Not integration. Not exposure as a strategy. Coexistence — with each other.

## What I ask instead

I am not going to ask you how you are doing.

I already know. I am living the same week you are, and the answer costs you something to say out loud for no return.

I am going to ask what you need.

It is a question with an answer that somebody can act on. A ride. A room. A repair. An hour of a trade you do not have. If the answer is a thing, the thing can be arranged, and afterwards you have it.

That is not me being impersonal, and it is not a transaction standing in for care. It is the opposite of what the other direction does. They ask so they can take the answer. I ask so I can do something about it.

It also settles a question you are entitled to have about me. A Specterati operator is in the business of taking everything from you — anything you want and need, they will work to remove. Somebody who asks what you need and then arranges it is doing the reverse, in public, where it can be checked.

## So why delete the account

Here is a question I keep turning over.

If none of this worked, why does Quora keep erasing me?

I do not promote self-harm. I do not promote harm to anybody. Nothing I have written asks anyone to confront, expose, or retaliate against a single person. It is a peaceful thing with an economic answer at the end of it, and the accounts die anyway, forty-eight times now, with the same boilerplate reason attached every time.

You do not spend that much effort on something that is not reaching anybody.

## Being copied, and what that tells you

The other signal is on the platform itself.

I now see Targeted Individuals on Quora — some real, some not — reflecting parts of the Skills Economy back. The idea that there is a way to live other than in misery, that survivors could be trading with each other, that a list of who can do what is worth having.

Imitation is flattery and I mean that plainly. It is good that the alternative is being seen. I am not in competition with anybody, and I want to be very clear about that: more communities is the goal, not fewer. A distributed peer-to-peer network is exactly what this is supposed to be. If five of these exist and none of them is mine, the thing I wanted still happened.

But there is a difference in the copies that matters, and it is not a small one.

Most of them are open to anybody. Which means real survivors in those spaces are interacting with operators without knowing it, because nothing filters. A space with no filter hands them a seat at the table, and a seat at the table is the thing they were denied here.

I do not give them one. That is why the raw material stops being available, and I suspect that is closer to the real reason an account gets erased than anything in the posts.

## Where the tipping point is

So here is the question I do not have a clean answer to, and I would rather put it to you than pretend.

What happens at ninety to ten? A community that is ninety percent real survivors and ten percent operators — does it still function?

What about fifty-fifty? At what ratio does a group stop being able to make any progress at all, because everything said in it is being carried straight back?

I do not know the number. What I know is that there is one, and that every community has to find it before it finds them.

If you are running one of these spaces, you are going to have to work out two thresholds. The first is the platform's: how much reach you can have before the host decides to end you, which is a line Quora has drawn on me forty-eight times. The second is internal: how many operators can be inside before what you are building stops working.

For the Skills Economy my answer is one. One is too many.

The way that is done is not by interrogating anybody or accusing anybody. It is that the whole thing is built around exchange. You ask for something real, and it either arrives or it does not. Their raw material is your suffering, not output, so requiring output filters them out early without anybody having to make an accusation they cannot prove.

## Why I do this at all

People thank me for working on it, and the honest answer is that I get a mutual benefit. There is no charge for any of this and it is not revenue for me.

The real reason is simpler. For a long time the only place to find other Targeted Individuals in a virtual space was Quora, and it took me two years of looking to find people who could actually help me and who I could actually help back. Two years, by hand, filtering out operators pretending to be survivors, and survivors who did not want to help, and survivors who wanted to and could not.

Nobody survives this alone. That is not a slogan, it is arithmetic — you need somebody to exchange with, and finding them should not cost the next person two years.

## Buying an email list

There is a version of all this that grows faster and I am not going to build it.

The fast version is performed kindness at scale. Content written to be clicked. Gestures made where people can see them. Numbers that go up because the number going up is the product.

It is the same instinct as buying an email list. You end up with a bigger figure and nothing underneath it, and everybody involved can tell.

Somebody told me recently that the app is dead because not many people are on it. There is no gimmick in it to make that number move, which is deliberate. The work happens slowly, in the actual arrangements between two people, and most of it never shows up as a figure anywhere.

I would rather have the small true number.

## So

I am not going to ask you how you are doing.

I am going to ask what you need, and then I am going to see whether the Skills Economy can get it to you. That is the offer, it does not expire, and it is not collecting anything from you on the way through.
