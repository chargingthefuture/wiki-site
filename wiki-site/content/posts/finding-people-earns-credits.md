The Directory is the thing survivors ask for most: a findable list of people and what they can do. It did not appear on its own. It was built by hand — reading what people said about themselves in public, one at a time, and recording it.

SkillsHunt is that work, opened up. Anyone can do it, and an accepted nomination grants the scout credits for it.

## What a nomination is

![The SkillsHunt Scout screen, nominating for the Summer Skills Hunt round, active 6/20/2026 to 9/1/2026. Heading: Nominate a Survivor. "Think of someone you believe may be a survivor — you don't need to be 100% certain. Their Quora profile helps verify their identity, and their skills join our economy." Fields: Full Name, required, 2 to 100 characters. Bio, optional, up to 280 characters — for example, lives in Houston, works in construction, connected through mutual contact. Quora Profile URL, social proof, highly recommended — "Quora activity helps verify this is a real person — reduces risk of trafficker infiltration." Country required; State or Region and City optional. Skills, required, picked from the taxonomy, maximum 10, with an option to select a profession that fills the skills in for you, a keyword search, and categories including Creative and Media, Education, Emergency and Reserve Roles, Energy and Utilities, Environmental and Waste Management, Finance and Public Administration, Food and Agriculture, Health, Housing and Construction, Manufacturing and Industry, Microfinance and SME Support, Mining and Extractive, Professional and Business Services, Public Safety and Justice, R&D and High-Tech, Retail and Services, Telecommunications and IT, Tourism and Hospitality, Transport and Logistics, and Water and Sanitation. Free-text skills can be added if the taxonomy has none that fit. Submit Nomination — earn points on acceptance.](images/skills-hunt-scout-2026-08-27.jpg)

A name, where they are, and what they can do. That is the whole of it.

Two things on that screen are worth reading twice. You do not have to be certain the person is a survivor — the screen says so outright. And the Quora profile is asked for because activity on a real account reduces the risk of someone being planted here by the people doing the harm. It reduces the risk. It does not remove it, and nothing does; writing can be copied and an account can be built.

Skills come from a taxonomy — a fixed list of the jobs and titles a working economy needs — because a skill picked from that list can be matched against what another member is looking for. If nothing on the list fits, you can add free text, and those go into a queue for review rather than straight into the map.

## Points, and what ranks them

![The SkillsHunt Scout Leaderboard. "Ranked by accepted points · tie-break: first-match count, then earliest submission. Pending points convert to accepted points after admin review." One entry, in first place: farah (You), 36 accepted, 36 first-matches, 612 points, plus 450 points pending review.](images/skills-hunt-leaderboard-2026-08-27.jpg)

Points arrive as pending and become real when a nomination is reviewed and accepted. Taxonomy skills are worth more than proposed free-text ones, because a matched skill does more work in the economy than an unmatched word does. Ties break on first-match count, then on who submitted earliest.

The round on screen is the Summer Skills Hunt, open until September 1, 2026. My own standing is what you see: 36 accepted, 612 points, 450 more pending.

## Where the credits come in

Points rank you. Credits are a separate thing, and they are what this post is mostly about.

When a nomination is accepted, ServiceCredits are granted to the scout who found the person. The amount per acceptance is set for each round, and a round can also carry a ceiling on how much any one scout earns in it. The grant happens once per nomination and never twice — a nomination reviewed a second time does not grant again.

Those credits work like every other credit in the app: usable across housing, rides, skills, and requests, and sendable to another member. If your balance is empty, [community credit lets you send down to −500 and repay as you earn](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/spend-before-you-earn) — scouting is one of the ways that number climbs back.

## Missions

![The SkillsHunt Missions screen. Heading: Active Missions — "Complete missions to earn bonus points and unlock badges." One mission listed: Find a doctor, 0 of 1 complete, plus 1 point, with a Scout Now button.](images/skills-hunt-missions-2026-08-27.jpg)

Missions point at what the economy is short of. Find a doctor is on the board because a community needs one and we do not have enough. When a specific gap opens, a mission is how it gets named instead of hoped about.

## The part people ask about

Nominating someone means creating a profile for a person who has not signed up.

That profile is labeled in the app as community-generated and it names the member who made it. The person it describes can claim it at any time, and can have it deleted by asking. Nobody has asked. When the possibility of deletion has been raised, some people have been outraged at the suggestion — the profile is how other survivors find them, and taking it down takes that away.

Being listed is not a lesser version of having an account. It is the thing itself: a findable person with stated skills, which is what most survivors say they want first.

## Why the work is credited

An economy needs to know who is in it and what they can do. Every accepted nomination adds one real person with real skills to a list other survivors can search — and the work of finding that person, reading what they said, and recording it accurately is work. It was being done by hand by one person. It is something the community can do for itself, and be credited for.
