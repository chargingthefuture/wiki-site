A question I saved on Quora and never answered, as its title was recovered from the address it was saved at:

> Why do some whistleblowers become targeted individuals after exposing illegal activities

The address it was saved at is https://www.quora.com/Why-do-some-whistleblowers-become-targeted-individuals-after-exposing-illegal-activities. The [Dictionary](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/Dictionary) carries the words I use for this; the question keeps the words it was asked in.

## They have already shown which box they are in

What I am targeted by is organized crime, a distributed network of ordinary people rather than a government program ([What organized crime means](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/what-organized-crime-means)). That network sorts people. The aim is that everybody ends up on one side or the other, somebody who goes along or a target, and recruiting runs as a quiet test of whether a person can be moved. Somebody who spots the con early is no use as a recruit ([The best description of it I have read](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/best-description-i-have-read)).

A whistleblower has finished that test in public. They saw illegal activity, would not stay inside it, and said so. There is no recruiting left to try on them, so the only box the arrangement keeps for them is the other one.

It is not the only door onto the list, and usually not a dramatic one. On October 11, 2025 I wrote that if a big purpose is behind it, "you would surely know. For example, if you are a whistleblower." A whistleblower is the case where the person does know.

## What gets worked on is their word

What a whistleblower holds is an account of what happened. Stopping them means making that account worthless, so the method aims at credibility rather than at the facts.

The picture it builds is that the person is unstable: provoked until they react like anyone would, then described by the reaction ([The reaction is what gets written down](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/the-reaction-is-what-gets-written-down)). At its furthest that is a false diagnosis, or a false accusation. On February 2, 2026, to somebody who felt they were being followed into their house and car, I wrote: "That was an entrapment scheme. The goal is to discredit you." On December 14, 2024, writing about a whistleblower found after somebody called in a wellness check, I noted what that phrase suggests: that he was likely "being accused of being crazy."

A person described as unstable can be ignored without anybody having to answer what they said. That is what discrediting buys.

The FBI's program against Martin Luther King Jr. watched, infiltrated and discredited, and private people wanted him stopped too ([Picked off early](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/picked-off-early)). The method was not invented for one person.
