A question I saved on Quora in October 2024 and never answered:

> When a targeted individual gets to the stage of being nearly 100% isolated, what should that person do to survive?

The question is at https://www.quora.com/When-a-targeted-individual-gets-to-the-stage-of-being-nearly-100-isolated-what-should-that-person-do-to-survive and it is still up. It was asked anonymously on December 17, 2020, so there is nobody to credit by name and the question has been standing for nearly six years. Its page carries 20 public followers and 225.8 thousand views, and it has been answered by other people.

![The question on its Quora page, captured September 21, 2026. The heading reads: When a targeted individual gets to the stage of being nearly 100% isolated, what should that person do to survive? Below it, under Asked by, the asker is listed as Anonymous. The list I keep of saved questions holds three more on the same subject, saved over the following year: what happens to Targeted Individuals in the end, after family and friends have been turned against them; how to cope with losing friends who do not understand; and whether it is possible to survive the final stage at all. The [Dictionary](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/Dictionary) carries the vocabulary I use for the people doing this.

I have been at this since I was five, and I have been at the stage the question describes. Here is the answer I would have wanted.

## Isolation is the product

The first thing to get straight is that being nearly alone is not something that happened to you along the way. It is the aim.

The slander goes out first, and it goes to the people around you — that is what turns friends and family, and it is why the question about losing friends who do not understand has the shape it does. The work gets canceled. The income goes. And the [Forced Homecoming](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/why-they-want-you-living-with-family) is the scheme run on top of that: make a person destitute so the only door left leads back to relatives who can be bought, at any age, and then buy them.

A person with nobody left has to go through whoever is offered. That is the reason to isolate somebody: not the loneliness for its own sake, but that every ride, every room, every job now runs through hands that can be reached. The [three endings I have watched people arrive at](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/count-the-recruits) — destitution, a mass shooting, suicide — are all reached from there.

So "the final stage" in the fourth question is not a mystery. It is the point at which the isolation is complete enough that the person has no arrangement left that does not run through them.

## What survive means here

I have written already that [no method I know of stops it](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/what-can-be-stopped), mine included. What can be stopped is what it costs, and the largest of those costs is dependence — whose hands your week runs through.

Read the question again with that in mind. Nearly a hundred percent isolated means nearly a hundred percent dependent on whoever is left, and whoever is left was, in most cases, selected. Surviving it is not a matter of holding out. It is a matter of getting even one arrangement — one ride, one room, one hour of somebody's trade, one conversation — into hands that cannot be reached, and then another.

That is what an economy of survivors is for, and it is why this project is an economy rather than a support group. What follows is the order I would do it in, cheapest first.

## Read before you talk

The first step costs nothing and needs no account, and it matters because a person at this stage has usually been talked at for years by people with an interest in what they believe.

The [Commons](https://app.chargingthefuture.com) — the group chat — reads without signing in. So does the [conversation under every post on this blog](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/you-can-talk-under-these-posts-now). [TI Radio](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/ti-radio) publishes a week of live discussions that survivors host, in your own timezone, and reading the schedule needs no account either; the discussions happen in Chyme, where you can listen without speaking and without a camera.

A voice in a room, from somebody who is not part of what is being done to you, is not a small thing at this stage. It is the first arrangement that does not run through them.

## Write it down

The second step is ClickLog: an incident, a date, a location if you choose one, fifteen seconds. Your notes are private and nobody but you ever sees them.

It does not stop anything. What it does is take your account of your own life out of memory under stress, which is exactly where the isolation puts it. A person with no witnesses and no record is easy to describe as unstable; a person with a dated log is not, and they can put the noticing down instead of carrying it all day.

## Be findable

The third step is the Directory, and it is the one I would put above everything else if I had to choose.

It took me [two years on Quora to find three people](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/two-years-to-find-three-people) who made a life-changing difference — a job, not freezing, a way out of the Forced Homecoming, moral support — and finding them meant filtering by hand through operators posing as survivors and survivors who could not help. All three are listed in the Directory now. The listing is what made them findable; the help itself happened between people.

Being on the list costs nobody an account. Most of the entries were built for people rather than by them, from what they had already said about themselves in public, and anybody can claim theirs or have it taken down. A person who is nearly a hundred percent isolated and findable is in a different position from one who is nearly a hundred percent isolated and not, because the next person searching for a trade, a city, or a name can reach them without going through anybody.

## Ask for the smallest real thing

The fourth step is to ask, and to ask the way the [last post but one](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/mutual-not-equal) describes: with something in your hands, knowing who is receiving it, and measuring nothing against equal.

Ask small. A ride to one appointment. An hour of a trade you do not have. A room for a week. SocketRelay is where you post what you need and whoever has it answers; Foundation is where providers list what they offer and you reach one directly; LightHouse is where survivors offer rooms to survivors. Each of those is one arrangement moved into hands that cannot be reached, and one is enough to change the arithmetic of a week.

And bring something. Not because anybody is keeping score — nobody here is, and [nobody owes anybody anything](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/you-dont-owe-me-anything) — but because an exchange is what keeps a relationship standing, and the person at the other end needs things too. What you have is worth what it is worth to them.

## Keep one leg in the door

The last step is the one for the quiet stretches, and I say it to anybody who has been told it stopped. Once a name is on the list it is never taken off, and the quiet years are when people drift into destitution with nobody around. Stay connected anyway. You do not have to show up every day; there is no streak here and nothing that nudges you about one. Come in when you need something, use one part, ignore the rest. Just do not let the arrangements you moved drift back.

## What none of this promises

It does not promise the harassment ends, and nothing here is a crisis service or pretends to be one. Mood, the one part of the app that asks how you are doing, is a private check-in that alerts nobody and is read by nobody, and it says so on the screen.

What it promises is smaller. That the number of things in your week running through hands that can be reached goes down by one, and then by another. That is the direction the question was asking about, and it is the only direction I have watched work.
