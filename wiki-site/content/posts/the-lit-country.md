The Peace Battle 2 game is now a map. The earlier version asked you to set a split between teaching and working each round and showed where that led. This one puts you on the ground as one person with one trade and five percent of a day, and the country lights up around what you do with it.

Play it here: https://chargingthefuture.github.io/offline-os/apps/peace-battle-2/. Free, no account, no ads, and it runs offline once it has opened. Nothing about how you play leaves your phone.

## What you see

A dark map of hearths, one for each survivor, spread across a dozen scattered lands. Never one walled place, because the people this is for are in every country, and so the answer is too. Each hearth has a trade, drawn from the same list of sectors and job titles the app's Workforce runs on. Fog drifts over the dark parts: [the schemes](https://www.chargingthefuture.com/schemes). Thin gray threads run between dark hearths: the informant (Specterati) net.

On the day the game was built, 174 hearths were lit, in the same proportions as the Directory that day. Most of them are advocates and peer supporters. Nurses next, then community organizers. The country could supply itself 32% of what it needs, 230 of 713 skills. Two tiles stay gray for good: a phone and a vehicle. No community this size can run those supply chains, and the game does not pretend otherwise.

## What you do

At the top is your day: twenty segments. Nineteen are shadowed. The attacks took them. One glows. That is your five percent.

Tap it, then tap a card on the map. A card is a goal somebody posted with a finish line, broken into tasks small enough to do from a phone in half an hour: find three yards hiring near Dallas and add their numbers, ask one dealer whether they finance with no credit check, find one host with a spare room in Houston. Your five percent goes on one task. A road lights from your hearth to theirs. The day ends.

Over the following days other hearths' five percents travel the roads to the same card. When every task on it is done, the goal is reached, that hearth lights fully, and its trade joins what the country can supply. You can post a goal of your own, and other people's time arrives at your door the same way.

Some days fog sits on your hearth and takes the sliver. Nothing you put on a card is lost; the day passes. Fog cannot cross a lit road.

From day ten the game offers the lamp: turn your hearth into a workshop that names its first customer and lights one road a day on its own. It is optional, and the game is complete without it. It stands for [One Percent](https://farahbrunache.com), the paid tier, and the free parts stand on their own there too.

## Singing lands

When every hearth in a land is lit, the land sings. The fog is pushed out and cannot come back, and a road between two singing lands joins them into one country.

That is the Estonia in it. 1.3 million people, occupied by a power that outnumbered them by more than a hundred to one and watched through an informant network that reached into every town, kept their language and their identity and took their country back. [The two-generation goal](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/the-two-generation-goal) says why that is the measure here. The singing lands are named for it.

## Winning and time

Two figures on the screen and no others: how many hearths exchanged something today, with a mark at 384, and what share of its needs the country supplies, toward 99%. Fifty rings around the map count the years, one a week. Two generations.

You win when the country supplies 99% of what it needs from its own people. You lose when the fiftieth ring closes first. Every run has a seed you can copy and hand to somebody, so two people can play the same country.

## What it is a picture of

Every mechanic is something on the [Peace Battle 2 page](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/peace-battle-2). The five percent is the part of a Targeted Individual's day the attacks leave, and twenty members with five percent each is a full working day. The cards are the goal board in PeerProgramming, where [Show up with your percent](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/show-up-with-your-percent) says how a card works. The 384 is members exchanging with each other on one day, counted a day at a time. The 99% is what winning looks like. The game has a second tab, What this is, that walks through each of these without leaving it.

Nothing in it is a prediction. It is the shape of the thing, drawn so it can be held.
