A question I saved on Quora and never answered, as its title was recovered from the address it was saved at:

> What are some ways to deal with a cyber stalker without involving law enforcement or hiring a lawyer

The address it was saved at is https://www.quora.com/What-are-some-ways-to-deal-with-a-cyber-stalker-without-involving-law-enforcement-or-hiring-a-lawyer. The [Dictionary](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/Dictionary) carries the words I use for this; the question keeps the words it was asked in.

Where a report goes and what it can carry is in [Who to report it to](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/who-to-report-it-to). This answer is about what a person can do alone, online, with no one else's permission.

## Why not a lawyer

On Quora on October 11, 2025, asked whether a Targeted Individual can get a lawyer for an unrelated civil matter without the lawyer being reached, I answered: "I am 99% sure the answer is no. You can try. Just have low expectations. It is not even about being a lawyer." The same answer went on to say that anyone helping a target with anything is harassed and sabotaged too.

On February 2, 2026 I put it another way: "this is like asking where can you find a lawyer to stand up to the mob." And in the same comment: "I am not suggesting for you to not seek legal help." Try it if you want to. Do not wait on it.

## Close the accounts you no longer use

Every account you opened and forgot is still there, with your name, your email, sometimes your number and your history, held by somebody you never think about. Each one is a door into your life that you are not watching.

On August 2, 2025 I wrote that I had seen a Targeted Individual describe accounts "constantly being taking over," with "their passwords compromised." An account you do not use is the easiest one to take over, because nobody notices it is gone.

In January 2025 I was purging every unused account I had. On January 5, 2025 I posted the letter I used, a boilerplate I found online: a plain, formal request to delete the account and every piece of personal data attached to it, citing the law that requires deletion, with 30 days to complete it and a request to confirm when it was done. It needs no lawyer.

## Do not answer what comes back

One request went to a site run by somebody I had worked with briefly on contract. Three months passed with no response. The reply, when it came, said its writer knew what I had done without saying what, warned me against threatening them, and said they knew where I was at all times. Then it offered to get on a Zoom call so I could walk them through deleting my data.

That reply is built to get a response. A second message typed in anger is the one that gets kept and shown around ([The reaction is what gets written down](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/the-reaction-is-what-gets-written-down)). The request was already sent and dated. Nothing in it needed defending.

What I wrote about it that day: "I will not internalize their slander." And: "the reality is, not everyone is in on it."

Keep a copy of what you sent and what came back, with the date. A dated record you hold yourself does not depend on anybody agreeing to look at it.
