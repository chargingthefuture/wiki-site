A question I saved on Quora in October 2024 and never answered, as its title was recovered from the address it was saved at:

> What is your opinion of people who engage in stalking activities for revenge and or personal gain Do you think they have psychological issues If so what are they

The address it was saved at is https://www.quora.com/What-is-your-opinion-of-people-who-engage-in-stalking-activities-for-revenge-and-or-personal-gain-Do-you-think-they-have-psychological-issues-If-so-what-are-they. The [Dictionary](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/Dictionary) carries the words I use for this; the question keeps the words it was asked in.

## The question names the motive

Revenge and personal gain are ordinary motives. Wanting something another person has does not take a diagnosis to explain.

On Quora on October 2, 2025 I gave the plainest version I have of why people join: "some might only be motivated by money, others by jealousy. Some are extorted."

## What they get for it

Most of the people taking part are not paid in money. They are paid in kind: a job, an apartment, friends, a favor. The money is further up, laundered through the ordinary economy ([A badge is a job title](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/a-badge-is-a-job-title), [Why nobody does anything](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/why-nobody-does-anything)).

That is gain in the plainest sense, and the law already has a name for a group organized around it. The United Nations defines an organized criminal group as three or more people acting together over time to commit serious crimes for financial or other material benefit ([What organized crime means](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/what-organized-crime-means)). My position is that this is a great many ordinary people in a distributed network of organized crime ([What can be stopped](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/what-can-be-stopped)).

## Some are ill, and that is not the reason

Asked on Quora whether the people doing this are mentally ill, I answered on October 6, 2025: "Yes, some are." Earlier that year, while I was in Nebraska traveling for work, about a hundred people, their children included, followed and provoked me in public for a slander campaign. I described them as mentally ill, homeless or addicted.

Illness is not what organized them. On January 19, 2026 I wrote that for the homeless, "instead of working, they get “donated” goods and services" in the form of items used against the target. Somebody hands those out.

In the same October 6 answer I wrote that, generally speaking, most of the people doing it are cowards or sadists, and that neither is outright considered a mental illness. On September 21, 2025 I wrote that the arrangement hides behind ordinary groups to "round up sadists in becoming vigilantes and human traffickers." Sadism is something it recruits for, not a condition that explains it away.

## A reason is not a justification

Calling it a psychological issue does the work an excuse does. It asks you to see the people doing it as unwell rather than as people choosing something they get paid for. On December 22, 2025 I wrote: "There is a difference between a reason someone does something and a justification. All vigilantes have a reason, and having a reason is not the same as having ethics, morals or being humane." In the same answer: "Most of their operatives are aware they are not following criminals." [A lie for every bias](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/a-lie-for-every-bias) goes into how each one is recruited through a bias of their own.

The illness label in this story usually runs the other way. One version of the slander is a false diagnosis aimed at the target: working with the police and the mental health community to get them committed ([The reaction is what gets written down](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/the-reaction-is-what-gets-written-down)). None of this is legal advice.
