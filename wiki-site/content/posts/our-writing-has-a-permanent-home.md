Quora has deleted my account twice. Both times, years of answers, posts, and conversations
disappeared overnight — not because anything was wrong with them, but because that is what
platforms do to accounts like mine.

That stops mattering today.

## What changed

Everything I write now lives in one place: a public, version-controlled archive at this site.
Every post, every screenshot, every product update is a file in a public repository, with a
timestamped history of when it was written and how it changed. Nobody can quietly empty it.

This blog is the reading surface for that archive. It now has clear sections:

- Posts — essays and community writing, like this one.
- Product Updates — the weekly record of what changed in the app.
- Guides, Insights, Member of the Day — practical material.
- Archives — the historical record: everything from the closed Discourse forum, and the posts
  from the erased Quora accounts as they are restored.

## What this means for Quora (and any platform)

Platforms are now distribution, not storage. A Quora post from me is a short excerpt and a link
back to the full post here. If Quora erases the next account, the links from that account die —
and nothing else. The writing, the screenshots, the history of the app and the work: all
unaffected, all still here.

The posts from the two erased accounts are being restored to the archive section, each marked
with the account it came from, the date it was originally posted, and a link to the original
question where one still exists.

## Where to follow

Bookmark this site. It is the one address that does not depend on any platform's tolerance:
every new post appears here first, and platform posts will only ever point back to it.
