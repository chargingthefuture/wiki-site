Self-defense is more than a cultural idea. It has legal standing.

The plain case is the one everybody already accepts. A stranger comes at you and starts to attack. You answer with equal force. That is self-defense, and nobody in their right mind argues with it. None of this is legal advice.

## What the law does not see

Among Targeted Individuals, it is understood that what we do to protect ourselves is self-defense. What is done to us is built to fall below the line where the law starts looking. It runs on plausible deniability: each act small enough to deny on its own. Microaggressions is the nearest word I have, and I am not sure it is the right one, or that a word for it exists yet. Either way, microaggressions are not part of the legal frame of self-defense.

A shove in passing is almost never treated as an assault. That is why it is one of the methods used on me most. But a shove repeated, and organized, is something else, and taking steps so that it stops happening is self-defense by any ordinary understanding. The legal category may not exist yet, or nobody has found how to prosecute it. What it would describe exists.

## The objection

People say the Skills Economy might not work, and some of that is about its age. I started it in September 2025, so it is a year old, and interference with employment and not being able to pay for hosting have disrupted it along the way.

Targeting has existed for centuries. The modern version, aimed at the people this is built for, has run for decades.

The argument against Targeted Individuals coming together is nearly always made from the outside. What would they think? What would they do? Will they come harder?

## Nobody defends themselves that way

Self-defense does not start by assessing the other person's next move. Something is happening, and the question is how you protect yourself. Nobody in the middle of it asks what the person attacking them will think of them for defending themselves.

When a stranger does it, nobody debates it. When a network does it, the same reasoning holds as a matter of culture, even though the law does not yet name the crime. Defending yourself gives you more than a reason. It gives you a justification.

## A toolbox

That is the frame for the Skills Economy: a set of self-defense tools that do not require harming anybody.

Set aside whether you agree about who is doing this. The tactics are the same whoever is behind them. A dated record of what happens, in [ClickLog](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/click-log). People and what they can do, in the [Directory](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory). Help exchanged between members, in [Foundation](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/foundation). A ride, in [TrustTransport](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/trust-transport). A place to stay, in [LightHouse](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/lighthouse).

There is no legal framework that gives us self-defense against this. These give us a position of self-defense without one.

## Will it make things worse?

Whether coming together makes them angrier is a different conversation. I have been targeted for more than thirty years and have spent time around other Targeted Individuals, and my targeting did not change because of it.

Each person has a set plan, an abuse plan written for them. It is built to torture that one person, so who is around you does not change it.
