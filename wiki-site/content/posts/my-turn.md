Every week I ask somebody to look at what they can do and consider offering it here, as work or as a hobby, on their own terms.

It is unreasonable to keep asking that without answering it myself. So this post is my turn.

## First, an artist

Before anything else, I am an artist. That is not a recent decision and it is not a job title.

I remember the first drawing I gave away. It was for a school project. Somebody saw it and asked to keep it — not politely, but with real enthusiasm, the way you ask for something you actually want.

I already knew I could draw. What I did not know until that moment was what it feels like when something you made lands on another person. That part felt like magic and it has never stopped feeling that way.

This is not about selling art. Selling art is fine and plenty of people should. It is that I drew for myself, I enjoyed every minute of it, and it made somebody else happy at the same time. Those three things arriving together is the thing I have been chasing ever since, and the artist in me has shown up in everything I have built since, including this app.

## Then 2023

It has been going since I was five. For most of that time it came in cycles — a stretch of it, then quiet, then a stretch of it again.

In 2023 it went continuous. It also got bigger. It used to be a selective few; more exposure brought in more of the groups, and the head count went up with it.

That changes the question a person asks about their own future. Not what do I want to be, which is a question for somebody with a straight road in front of them. More like: given this, what am I now.

## Can I say sociologist

I majored in sociology. For years I did not feel I could claim the word.

That hesitation was not modesty. It is what happens when you learn to describe yourself in the vocabulary of employment: a title is something an employer confers, so without one you are nothing in particular. The Skills Economy exists partly because that vocabulary is wrong. A person is a plumber because they can do plumbing. The title is a separate object and it belongs to somebody else.

By my own standard, then, the honest answer is yes.

Not because of the degree. Because of what the last two years have actually consisted of. Naming and maintaining a list of methods so incidents can be counted. Building a taxonomy of the work a community needs, and one of the ways it gets taken apart. Collecting incidents with tags so a pattern can be seen that no single person could see alone. Running a survey about account removals, and a separate observational census of the accounts still standing, because self-report and observation answer different questions and have to be read together. Refusing to gate that survey behind approval, because gating it would have sampled only the people already reached.

That last one is the giveaway. Worrying about who your sample excludes before you collect it is not a thing a person does by accident.

So: an artist who does sociology. I am not employed as one and I do not publish in journals, and I am not going to pretend those are the same as what I do. But I study how a group of people organizes itself and what that does to the people inside it, I do it with method, and the record of it is public and dated.

I will name the limits, because leaving them out would undo the claim. I am inside what I am studying, which is a real method with a real name and a known cost: there are things your own position hides from you. My sample comes mostly from one platform. And I have no comparison group, which is the largest gap in all of it and the hardest to fix.

## What I set down

Something else changed and it belongs in an honest accounting.

I used to place myself firmly as pro-Black. I do not any more.

Everybody already knew that space had its own operation running through it. What changed it for me was seeing how many Black people are involved in trafficking, inside a psyop shaped the same way. Once I had seen that from the inside, the category stopped describing anything I could organize around.

I do not regret the years, and not as a consolation. Those years were spent reading, watching, and working out how a thing is structured and who benefits — and that is the same work I do now. It made the Skills Economy possible to design, and it has been what kept me functional inside being trafficked. The training was real even though the conclusion did not survive.

## What I can offer

Which brings me to the part I am actually announcing.

Beyond building the app and running the logistics inside it, what I think I can offer is this: helping other Targeted Individuals reach their one percent.

There is a tab in [Workforce](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/workforce) called What's your 1%? It reads the skills already on your Directory listing, you set a rate you would actually charge, and it shows you what serving one percent of five million survivors — fifty thousand people — comes to in a year.

The main Workforce screen splits the whole economy evenly and gets about $142,500 a person. No real outcome is an even split. However you define doing well, at work or at the thing you do for love, it comes out of three parts: talent, luck, and how much work you put in. The even split has all three of those averaged away.

So the figure on that tab is not a prediction. It is a target with your own arithmetic under it, and the question it leaves you with is the interesting one. What would you actually have to do to get there? What is the first thing? Who would you need?

That is what I want to help people work on. Not signing up — the app is a logistics tool, and no logistics tool has ever been the reason anybody's life changed. The thing underneath it is a person deciding there is a version of this where they are doing their work again, and then having somebody to work it through with.

## What I am going to do

I am going to start reaching out to Targeted Individuals about more than joining something.

The invitation posts will carry on, because being findable matters and most people on that list still do not know they are on it. But the next thing I want to be doing is sitting with people over their own figures. What you can do, what you would charge, what fifty thousand of them would mean, and what stands between you and the first one.

I built the map. Walking it with people is the part I can offer, and it is the part I would enjoy — which, going back to the drawing, has always been how I can tell a thing is mine to do.
