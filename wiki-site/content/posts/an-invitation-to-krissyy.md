Dr. King's last campaign was economic. In 1968 he went to Memphis for sanitation workers on strike for a union, after two of them, Echol Cole and Robert Walker, were crushed in a garbage truck. He was killed there on April 4. Twelve days later the strike ended with the city recognizing the union and agreeing to terms.

A march makes a demand. When the demand is won, it becomes an agreement, and somebody has to be able to write it: find what the rules already say, and put the new terms into words that still hold when the other side reads them differently.

## Krissyy

Krissyy (https://www.quora.com/profile/Krissyy-2), in Minneapolis, Minnesota.

Their Directory listing carries one skill: legal research and drafting. Research is finding out what a law, a lease, or a policy already says. Drafting is writing the thing itself — a letter, an agreement, a complaint — so that it says what it means and nothing else.

## Why that one skill matters here

Read on September 28, the Directory lists 163 people. The catalog behind it carries 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do. 226 of them are held by somebody on the list, and 487 by nobody.

Legal research and drafting appears on exactly one listing, and it is Krissyy's.

Much of what survivors carry is paper. A lease somebody else wrote. A notice with a deadline. A report that was never filed, or was filed and went nowhere. The people on this list arrange things between themselves, a room or a ride or an hour of a trade, and most of that never needs anything written. Some of it does, and when it does, it needs somebody who can read the paper and write the answer.

## What this is not

This is not a request for legal advice or representation, and nothing here says Krissyy is a lawyer or offers to act as one. Research and drafting is a skill. Practicing law takes a license. The listing claims the first and says nothing about the second.

It is also not a request to take on anybody's case. Being on a list of what a person can do is not an offer of anything, and the Directory has never worked that way.

## How the listing came to be

Most of this list was built by me, one person at a time, from what people already said about themselves where anyone could read it. Those listings are marked community-generated and carry my handle, so anybody reading one knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs — what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody who writes things down for other people needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The same listing that makes them findable is what makes those people findable to them.
