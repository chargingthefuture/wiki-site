Two things have to be true for the targeting to work.

The first is that the people around you can be reached. A neighbor, a landlord, a clerk, the front desk at a clinic. What you are told depends on who you ask, and whoever you ask can be reached first.

The second is that there is nobody else. That one is not a starting condition. It is what the first produces, given time.

Lab work is built around the first of those. A test is a procedure written down before anybody runs it, followed the same way every time, so the result is the same whoever is holding the sample. It is worth having people nearby who know how that is done.

The listing describes Marilyn as a lab tech and carries three specializations:

- Laboratory safety and SOP adherence is working to a standard operating procedure: the written steps that keep a result repeatable and the person running it safe.
- Laboratory testing (basic) is running the routine tests a sample goes through, the kind a treatment plant runs on water every day.
- Packaging and labeling is getting a product sealed and marked so whoever picks it up knows what is in it.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on October 8, the Directory's 187 people held 233 of them.

Marilyn's three skills sit in three different sectors:

- Basic laboratory testing is filed under treatment-plant operators in Water and Sanitation, one of the thinnest: 3 of its 19 skills are held by anybody.
- Packaging and labeling is filed under food-processing workers in Food and Agriculture, where 6 of 49 are held.
- Laboratory safety and SOP adherence is filed under lab technicians in R&D and High-Tech, where 21 of 41 are held.

Water and food are the two things nobody goes a day without, and both are checked by somebody before they reach anybody. A community that means to provide for itself needs people who know how that checking is done.

## What this is not

This is not a request to test anybody's water or food, and nothing here says Marilyn will. Being on a list of what a person can do is not an offer of anything, and the Directory has never worked that way.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what Marilyn said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs: what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work is checking things for other people needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The listing that makes them findable makes those people findable to them, which is the second of the two things turned around: not nobody else, but somebody.
