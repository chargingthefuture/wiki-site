Anybody can open [chargingthefuture.com](https://chargingthefuture.com), tap what they need, and see how many people on the list could help. No account and no name. When a need comes back with nobody, the page notes it so I know to look ([See who could help you, before you make an account](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/see-who-could-help-without-an-account)).

One of the choices is legal help. The count behind it is made of people listed for legal work, and Ric Vaughn is one of them.

## Ric Vaughn

Ric Vaughn (https://www.quora.com/profile/ric-vaughn), in the United States. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/1ae9ca6e-4069-4380-937e-a469718c5dd1.

The listing carries three specializations:

- Case preparation and negotiation: getting a case ready, and settling it where it can be settled.
- Contract and statutory interpretation: reading what a contract or a law actually says.
- Legal advocacy and advice: standing up for somebody, and telling them where they stand.

## Why that matters here

The app keeps a catalog of 713 skills, the things a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do, grouped by sector. Read on October 14, the Directory's 321 people held 260 of them.

All three of Ric's skills are filed under lawyers in Public Safety and Justice, where 4 of 22 are held.

A count on the front page says how many people could help and never who they are. Names, and a way to reach anybody, stay behind a free account and the check.

## What this is not

This is not a request for anybody's legal advice, and nothing here says Ric will give it. Being on a list of what a person can do is not an offer of anything, and the Directory has never worked that way.

## How the listing came to be

I built it from what Ric said about themselves in public and put it up under their name, which is how most of this list was made, one person at a time. They did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why it is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else's skills, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs: what it says, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

## This does not run one way

Somebody whose work is other people's disputes needs things too. A ride. A room. An hour of a trade they do not have. The listing that makes them findable makes those people findable to them.
