## What the questions ask

Jessica's questions on Quora are public. Three of them:

> Who should I report my gang stalking to?

> How can I find out whether or not I'm being watched or spied on? How can I catch them?

> Will they ever stop? What if I move?

Read together, they ask for three things: somebody to tell, a way to know for sure, and a way out. Nobody should have to work those out alone, and none of them is a question this list is new to.

## Somebody to tell

I answered the first question in [Who to report it to](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/who-to-report-it-to). The short version: I do not know of an office that takes a report of the entire pattern and acts on it. A police report carries one incident, described as the crime it is.

The telling that helps most is often not a report. The [Commons](https://app.chargingthefuture.com) is a group chat of other Targeted Individuals, and nobody there needs the first hour of a conversation spent on whether it is happening.

## A way to know

Wanting to catch them usually starts with wanting to be sure. What gets you there is a record: dated, written as it happens, not reconstructed from memory months later. [ClickLog](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/click-log) takes about fifteen seconds an incident, and your notes are not shown to anyone else here. Over weeks, a record shows you the pattern yourself, before anybody else has to believe it.

## A way out

Moving is the question almost everybody on this list has asked. [It followed you](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/it-followed-you) is the longer answer. The short one is that a move holds better when there are people at the other end of it.

That is what the [Directory](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory) is for: 163 people, where they are, and what they can do. [Foundation](https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/foundation) is where members offer and exchange help: a ride, a room, an hour of a trade somebody does not have.

## How the listing came to be

Jessica did not sign up, and nobody asked them first. That is why the listing is marked community-generated and carries my handle: so anybody reading it knows it is my account of somebody else, and knows who to come to about it.

## The invitation

Claiming the listing takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that it is theirs — what it says, whether the placeholder gets replaced with something they can actually do, and whether it stays up at all.

Nothing else is required. Not using the rest of the app, not posting anything, not agreeing with a word I have written. No is a complete answer, and the listing comes down on request with no account involved in that either.

I cannot verify it, and neither can a reader. Even with those, there is no such thing as a hundred percent.
