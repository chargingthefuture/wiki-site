The objection I hear most is not that the idea is wrong. It is that the number is too small. A hundred and forty-seven people on a list cannot do anything.

Estonia in 1991 is the answer I keep going back to. When the occupation ended, the country had no money and infrastructure that did not work. What it had was 1.3 million people who already knew how to do things, and it built a functioning modern state out of that — largely in software, because software was what it could afford — over about two generations, while the pressure from next door never let up. I have written the [numbers](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/how-the-math-works) and the [posture](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/estonia-did-not-get-to-solve-it-either) up before, and nobody here is forming a nation state; the country is the yardstick and nothing more.

The part of it that bears on this post is simpler than either. Nobody waited for the money. The people who could do things were already there, and what changed was the arrangements between them.

One of the things a place has to be able to do for itself is put clothes on people.

## Syah

Syah (https://www.quora.com/profile/Syah-Neal-AdoreTM), in Kansas City, Missouri. The Directory profile is at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/fdb8086c-e241-4f51-baf9-4992892a31b8 and it carries three specializations: fit and sizing, garment construction, and textile selection and sourcing.

Those three are not three versions of one skill. Together they run from a bolt of cloth to a garment on a person who can move in it.

Textile selection and sourcing is knowing where the material comes from, what it is worth, and which of it is worth buying. Garment construction is turning it into the thing. Fit and sizing is the part most people never think about until a garment does not have it: making the thing fit a real body rather than a chart, and knowing how to alter what already exists so it does. A community that can only do the middle one of those has a sewing machine. A community that can do all three can dress itself.

## Where it sits on the list

The app keeps a catalog of 657 skills — the set a working economy of about five million people needs somebody to be able to do. Apparel and fashion design is its own occupation in that catalog, filed under creative work, and making what cannot be ordered in is the job of a team the app calls Making & Repair, one of the thirteen a community has to be able to staff before it runs at all.

Clothing sits between those two and is usually forgotten by both. Planning conversations run to water, power, food and a roof, and clothing is assumed, the way it is assumed in a country with a store on every corner. It stops being assumed the first winter.

On 14 September the Directory's 147 people held 184 of the 657 skills, and 473 had nobody at all. Creative work is where the list is deepest, and it is still a list where most of the catalog is empty. Somebody who can carry a whole chain on their own is not joining a queue. They are the reason the row exists.

## How they came to be listed

Syah did not put themselves in the Directory. I did, after reading what they said about themselves in public, and the listing is marked community-generated and names me as the person who made it, so that nobody has to guess.

That is how most of the list was built, one person at a time. The Directory is the thing survivors ask for more than anything else: a findable list of people and what they can do. Building it by waiting for people to sign up for another app would have meant building nothing.

## The invitation

Syah, the listing is under your name, and the three things on it are a chain nobody else on the list carries end to end.

Claiming it is the step that makes it yours. It takes a free account and a few minutes, and after that the entry is under your control rather than mine — you set what it says, and you can take it down.

Nothing else is required. You do not have to use the rest of the app, you do not have to post anything, and you do not have to agree with a word I have written about why any of this is happening — including the paragraph about Estonia.

If the answer is no, that is a complete answer and nothing about the listing changes. It can come down at any time, with no account involved anywhere in that. Nobody who has been asked has wanted theirs removed, and the offer has been real since the day the list went up.

## This does not run one way

An invitation that only asks is not worth much to somebody already carrying a full week.

Somebody who sources cloth needs it moved, stored and paid for without going through anybody who should not be handling it, and the Directory holds more entries in transport and logistics than in any sector but creative work and health. Somebody who sews needs a ride, a room, a repair, an hour of a trade they do not have. Those are on the list too, under other people's names, and the same listing that makes them findable is what makes those findable to them.

That exchange is deliberately the first thing here rather than the last. Asking somebody for something real is also what filters out the people who will never hand anybody anything.

Estonia did not import the people who rebuilt it. They were already there, and so is Syah.
