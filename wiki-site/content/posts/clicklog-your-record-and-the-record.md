ClickLog does two jobs with one entry. You log an incident once; what it becomes is up to you.

## Job one: your record

Privately, ClickLog is your incident log. Each entry is dated, located if you choose, with notes nobody but you ever sees.

Why keep one? Pattern matching. A single incident is noise even to yourself; thirty dated entries are a pattern you can read. Where it happens, when it happens, which schemes repeat. A person who can see the pattern can route around it — different times, different places, fewer of the Specterati in your day.

And the record is the start of justice. Whatever path you ever choose to pursue, it begins with a dated, consistent, contemporaneous record — the kind you cannot reconstruct after the fact, only keep as you go.

## Job two: the record

Shared, your entries feed something that has never existed anywhere: real-time, self-reported global trend data on the movement of traffickers and their activities.

Every count of the people in our situation so far has been assembled by outsiders, after the fact, from scattered reports. This is the reverse: the people it happens to, logging it as it happens, onto one map. Where activity clusters. Which schemes run where. How it moves.

Two things follow from that.

The first is avoidance. A live map of where the activity is helps every member steer around it — the same pattern matching as job one, at the scale of all of us.

The second is standing. The UN Special Rapporteur on Torture's 2020 report to the Human Rights Council (A/HRC/43/49) addressed coordinated harassment as a category and said the volume of allegations warrants investigation rather than dismissal. A body that says "investigate" needs data, and trend data is exactly the form a submission takes. Nobody's individual claim has to be adjudicated for the aggregate to be worth looking at — that is what makes trends powerful.

And further out: I believe visible patterns can help reduce suicides and mass shootings. A person who can see their experience on a map of thousands is a person with proof they are not imagining it and not alone — and despair runs on exactly those two beliefs.

## The three rules

Privacy is the whole design, and three rules cover it.

One: your notes are always private. Nobody but you ever sees them. Not shared, not ever.

Two: a private incident does not need a location. Add one only if you want it in your own record.

Three: tagging an incident with problems or schemes requires a location and trend sharing, because tags exist to feed the global map. What sharing sends is grouped trend data only — the date, a rough area, and the tags. Never your notes, never your exact location. An incident you keep untagged shares nothing unless you choose otherwise.

Log privately forever and it is still worth it — that is job one. Share when you are ready and every entry makes the map sharper — that is job two. Both run on the same thirty seconds of logging.
