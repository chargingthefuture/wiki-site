A question I saved on Quora and never answered, as its title was recovered from the address it was saved at:

> What does remove gangstalking mean

The address it was saved at is https://www.quora.com/What-does-remove-gangstalking-mean. The [Dictionary](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/Dictionary) carries the words I use for this; the question keeps the words it was asked in.

I saved it two times.

Whether anything stops it is answered in [What can be stopped](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/what-can-be-stopped). This answer is about the word itself, because "remove" is a word I mostly see in offers.

## As a promise, it means nothing I have seen

On Quora on October 11, 2025, asked what resources exist and whether they can be trusted, I answered about anything claiming to end it: "No, I have not seen anything trustworthy. It is either trolling or naive/ignorant of who orchestrates targeting."

That still holds. A thing that promises removal has to say who it is removing it from, and the people doing this are a great many ordinary people paid through ordinary work. No product, prayer or program reaches all of them at once.

## How the offers read

The offers come anyway, and they look alike. On July 7, 2025, I left a spam comment up in a Quora space so readers could see one, and wrote: "You can tell it's spam from the heavy marketing rather than a post providing constructive help and information." On July 3, 2025, answering another one: "If the organization is real why not immediately share the information? If it has legitimately helped you why not state how they have? This is copy and pasted marketing jargon."

So the test is simple. Help that works can say what it did, plainly, without a sign-up form in front of it. An offer that is mostly urgency and testimonial, and has no information inside, is selling something. In that same July 7 comment I said I first took it for somebody taking part, and that if it was not, it was "another run of the mill scammer." Either way the person who answers it pays.

## What can be removed

The narrower version of the word does have an answer. In the October 11 answer I went on to describe what my Quora space works on instead: mitigating the aftereffects, "such as the loss of transportation, jobs, and housing. The resources we are excluded from if we are targets."

Those losses are concrete, and each one can be replaced one at a time. A ride from somebody vetted replaces a ride from a stranger. A survivor who fixes cars, cuts hair or keeps books can be found by what they can do, without going through anybody who takes part. Nothing there is bought from a seller promising an ending ([Exit their economy, exit the psyop](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/mono/EXIT-THEIR-ECONOMY,-EXIT-THE-PSYOP)).

That is the reason the Directory exists. It lists survivors by their skills, and being on it costs nobody an account. TrustTransport is where vetted rides are arranged.

None of this removes the people doing it. It removes what they were counting on you needing from them.
