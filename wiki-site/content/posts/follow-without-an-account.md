There is now a feed for this blog, at
`https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/feed.xml`.

If that means nothing to you, the next section is for you. If it does, that address is all you need.

## What a feed is

A feed is a plain list of what has been published here — each post's title, its date, a few
sentences saying what it is, and its address. It is written for software to read rather than for a
person.

You point an app at it once. From then on the app checks the list for you and shows you what is
new. You do not have to remember to come back, and nothing decides what you see first: posts arrive
in the order they were written.

This is old technology and it is still the plainest arrangement anybody has come up with. It is
also how every podcast in the world works. If you have ever subscribed to a podcast, you have used
a feed and did not have to know it.

## Why it is here

Quora has erased my accounts forty-seven times.

What those erasures took was never the writing. Every post is in this repository and I can rewrite
anything. What they took was people knowing where I am. Each time an account went, everybody
following it lost the thread, and most of them never found their way back — not because they
stopped caring, but because there was no route from where they were to where I went.

A feed cannot be taken away like that. There is no account to suspend, no address I hold, and no
company standing between you and the list. It is a file on the blog, the same blog that carries the
posts. Anybody who has it stays connected to this whether or not any platform still lets me
exist on it.

## What it will not do

I would rather say this plainly than oversell it.

Most people do not use feed readers. They were common until Google closed the best one in 2013, and
since then they have been a thing for people who already knew about them. If you have never
installed one, you probably will not install one for a blog, and that is a reasonable decision.

It also does not reach you the way a message does. Nothing buzzes. You open your reader when you
feel like it and the posts are sitting there, which some people much prefer and others will find
useless.

So this is not the thing that solves being erased. It is one route that cannot be closed by
anybody, sitting alongside the ones that can.

## How to use it, if you want to

You need a reader app — there are many, most are free, and they run on a phone. Any of them will
do; nothing about this feed is particular.

In most of them you can paste this blog's address on its own and the app will find the feed by
itself. If yours asks for the feed address specifically, it is the one at the top of this post.
There is also a "Follow by RSS" link in the footer of every page here.

The feed carries the newest fifty posts across everything published here — the posts, the product
updates, the guides, the archive. It is rebuilt every time this blog publishes, so it is never
behind. When I bring in old material from the erased accounts it is filed under the date it was
originally written, which means it lands where it belongs in the record instead of arriving in your
reader as fifty new things.

## Why not an email list

Several people have asked, and the honest answer is that email is the thing most people use and
also the thing most easily taken from me.

A mailing list lives or dies on whether the large mail providers think you are wanted. That
judgment runs mostly on how many recipients mark a message as unwanted, and the threshold is low
enough that a small number of people acting together can cross it for everybody. Once crossed,
every subscriber stops getting mail, including the ones who asked for it. The service sending it
removes the account and the list goes with it — which is the same shape as everything above, with a
different company's name on it.

That is not a reason to never do it. It is a reason to build the thing that cannot be taken first,
and to design the fragile one so that losing it costs me a send rather than the people.
