A question on Quora, asked by Slimfiguree (https://www.quora.com/profile/slimfiguree) on March 1, 2025:

> How do you guys protect yourself against energy weapons and frequency?

Its page on Quora carries 1 public follower and 890 views. Slimfiguree is also listed in the Directory, at https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/directory/profile/8bf5873b-3ff7-4ad1-97e3-e7a945dc924d.

## I do not have an answer

I do not know what protects a person from energy weapons. Not a partial answer, and not one I am holding back. Anything I wrote here would be a guess, and on this subject a guess costs people money: one of the posts below is somebody's account of several hundred dollars spent on a suit that did not work.

So this post is a request. If something has helped you, say what it is.

## What is already on the blog

- [The DEW List: Naming Every Kind of Attack](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/dew-attack-types) names the kinds of directed energy attack, so each one can be logged in ClickLog under its own name.
- [Knowledge That Survives a Ban](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/what-works-knowledge-that-survives-a-ban) is Gn0b0dy Pneuma's account of shielding fabric and faraday cages, from having bought and used them, including what did not work.
- [Mutual, not equal](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/mutual-not-equal) is about treating directed energy weapons as part of the same harassment as the slander, the street harassment and the theft, rather than a separate problem.
- [Unite around what?](https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/unite-around-what) answers a question that named directed energy weapons, and sends those attacks to the list above to be logged.

None of them says what protects a person. That part has to come from the people who have tried things.

## Where to answer

Two places, for two kinds of answer.

Under this post, in Fireside. The conversation sits underneath this post, and anybody can read it without an account. To write, press the button under the post: it opens this conversation in the app. What you write appears publicly once you are approved through Unlock. Say what you tried, what happened, and what did not work. The failures help as much as the rest, because each one is money somebody else does not have to spend.

In WhatWorks, for a specific thing. WhatWorks is one shared list organized by problem. Under each problem are tools somebody here bought, used, and said helped, each with a link to get it. Suggest a tool under the problem it helped with, with the link and a note on why; it is reviewed before it appears. If a tool already on the list worked for you, mark it helpful. The count beside each tool is how many people said it helped them, and that count is how one tool is weighed against another.

A count is people saying a thing helped them. It is not a test, and it does not mean a tool will work for you. It is still more than any of us has alone.
