The phrase "It takes a lot of effort not to be free" is a statement that challenges our conventional understanding of freedom and effort. Attributed to the philosopher Alan Watts, this idea delves into the paradoxical nature of human experience. 

Watts, known for his insights into Eastern philosophy, particularly Zen Buddhism, suggests that true freedom comes from a state of being rather than from constant striving or effort. This relates to the fight against human trafficking and the "Specterati."

Understanding the Paradox
1. Effort and Freedom: Watts discussed the idea that true freedom comes from a state of being rather than from constant striving or effort. In this context, "effort" refers to the mental and emotional struggles we often engage in to control our lives, suppress our emotions, or conform to societal expectations. The more we try to control and restrict ourselves, the more effort we expend, and the less free we become.
2. Natural State: The phrase suggests that our natural state is one of freedom and spontaneity. When we try to control or suppress this natural state, we create tension and effort. In other words, it takes effort to maintain a state of unfreedom because we are constantly fighting against our natural inclinations.
3. Acceptance: Watts' teachings often emphasize acceptance and living in the present moment. By accepting ourselves and our circumstances, we can reduce the effort required to maintain a false sense of control, thereby experiencing greater freedom.
4. Paradox: The phrase is a paradox intended to challenge conventional thinking. It suggests that the more we try to control and restrict ourselves, the more effort we expend, and the less free we become. Conversely, by letting go of control and accepting our natural state, we can experience true freedom with less effort.

Applying the Paradox to Human Trafficking
The fight against human trafficking is a complex issue that requires a multifaceted approach. The phrase "It takes a lot of effort not to be free" can be applied to this context in several ways:
1. Impossible vs. Possible:
    * Impossible: Thinking we will end human trafficking by pleading and talking our way out of the existence of evil people.
    * Possible: Recognizing that the "Specterati"—those who operate like parasites, taking advantage of others and profiting from their suffering—are an unavoidable part of society. The only way to deter these leeches from a Target's life is by focusing on our own prosperity and building strong communities.
2. Parasites and Solutions: Parasites, like those that cause malaria or intestinal worms, need special treatment to get rid of them. Talking about them won't make them go away. We need medicine, better hygiene, and sometimes we need to get rid of where the parasites live. The legal and medical field is a byproduct of treating parasites—not the solution. Human trafficking is similar. It happens where people are vulnerable. We need to make big changes and take direct action to stop it.
Strategies to Combat the Specterati
To stop the Specterati, we need to focus on empowering individuals and building strong communities. Here are two strategies:
1. Help Targets Make Money: Give people chances to make their own money so they are not easy targets. Economic empowerment is a key factor in reducing vulnerability to exploitation.
2. Build Strong Communities: Create places where people help each other and share resources to stay safe and prosper. Strong communities can provide support and protection against exploitation.

The phrase "It takes a lot of effort not to be free" encourages a shift in perspective from one of constant effort and control to one of acceptance and natural flow. By applying this philosophy to the fight against human trafficking, we can focus on empowering individuals and building strong communities, rather than relying solely on legal and medical interventions. True freedom comes from letting go of control (fixating on the Specterati’s actions and pleading about them) and accepting our natural state, and this principle can guide us in creating a more just and equitable society.
