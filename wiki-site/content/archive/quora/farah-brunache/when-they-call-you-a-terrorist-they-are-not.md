When they call you a terrorist, they are not accusing you. They are telling you what they want done to you.

They walk past and say “pedo.” Or “terrorist.” Or “schizo.” Or “racist.” Or “dog hater.” Sometimes it runs the other way and they compare you to Martin Luther King.

You know none of it is true. That is the part that feels insane. If they wanted to lie about you convincingly they could pick something believable. Instead they pick the thing that is most obviously false, say it where you can hear it, and keep walking.

Most of us hear that as an accusation. So we do what people do when they are accused. We defend ourselves. We explain. We try to clear our name with people who never asked. And when it does not work, because there was never a real charge to answer, we decide nobody will ever believe us.

I do not think it is an accusation. I think it is a threat, and the label tells you which one.

Call someone a terrorist and you are naming a treatment: watched, listed, raided, locked up, and nobody objects because of what he was called. Call someone schizophrenic and you are naming a different one: committed, medicated, disbelieved about everything afterward. Call someone a pedophile and you are naming what happens to a person carrying that word in a jail or on a street. Compare someone to Martin Luther King and you are naming a man who was wiretapped for years, sent a letter urging him to kill himself, and then shot.

They are not describing you. They are describing the plan.

This holds for every version of it. Replace terrorist with whatever they use on you. Replace it with the backhanded compliment, the one that sounds almost like praise and leaves you unsettled without being able to say why. The content changes. The function does not. Each one names a category, every category has a standard treatment attached, and the treatment is the message.

It is said this way for a reason. Nobody threatened you. Somebody said a word. There is nothing to report, nothing to point at, nothing you can repeat later that sounds like anything out loud. That deniability is not a side effect. It is why they use words instead of sentences.

I have written before about why so many of us say this is the government, and I gave two reasons. Some of us already hate the government and it fits. Some of us are embarrassed to be targeted, and a government makes it sound like something that happened to us instead of something we failed at. Both are denial, and I include myself in having to check for it.

There is a third reason I missed, and I now think it is the biggest one. It is not denial. It is a misunderstanding.

When you hear the word as an accusation, you start looking for who would have the authority to make it stick. Who can actually list you, raid you, commit you. That points straight at a government. So people conclude a government agency is behind it, because that is who could make an accusation real.

But it was never an accusation. It was a threat. A threat only requires that the person saying it wants you to picture the outcome. Anyone can do that. It costs nothing, and it explains why the people doing it are ordinary people around you rather than an agency.

I am not a mental health professional and I am not going to pretend I can fix what this does to a person. But I have watched what the accusation frame does to us, and I think it is killing people.

If you believe you are being accused, you fight. You escalate. You try to prove a negative to strangers who never charged you with anything. You get louder in public, which is the footage they were waiting for. Or you go the other direction and decide that since the name will never be cleared, there is no point to any of it. Both of those roads have bodies on them. Both start from reading a threat as a charge.

Understanding it correctly does not improve your situation. It changes what you do with it. There is no name to clear. There is nobody to convince. There is a thing that was said, a date, a place, and you write it down. You do not answer it, because being answered was the entire point.

So the next time one of them says the word, try hearing it as what it is. Not “he thinks I am a terrorist.” He is telling me what he would like to see happen to me, and he is telling me instead of doing it because he cannot do it himself today.

And if you are anywhere near the point where this feels like a reason to end it, or to do something that ends your life a different way, tell someone*. Not because they can fix it. Because being alone with it is the actual mechanism, and it is the one part of this they cannot arrange if you refuse.
