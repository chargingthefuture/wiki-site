External intervention is usually a byproduct of internal resistance rather than a substitute.

I am not asking Targets to not voice what is happening to them. It is why in this Space the introduction refers people to other spaces I find effectively provides that space.

History shows oppressed people largely win their freedom by organizing and acting for themselves, not by waiting for outsiders—slave revolts, resistance movements, and uprisings against genocidal regimes make that clear. Asking “who will free me?” assumes an external rescuer; asking “who can help me end this?” is the practical question. If you’re truly a Target, start with other Targets: peer networks, collective action, and careful documentation are the most realistic, immediate steps. Outsiders who are insulated from harm or who profit from the system rarely have the incentive to act — though outside pressure can help when combined with internal resistance.
