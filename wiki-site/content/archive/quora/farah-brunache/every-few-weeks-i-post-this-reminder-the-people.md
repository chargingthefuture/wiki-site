Every few weeks I post this reminder: the people I follow, invite, or interact with are not necessarily TIs. Use TI Skills Economy’s built-in features to tell signal from noise.

I recently interacted with someone who is likely a perp. I don’t want to rush to conclusions — but either way, who I follow, invite, or interact with should never be a reason to trust them.

Here’s the thinking behind that.

[Matthew](https://www.quora.com/profile/Matthew-Cappadocia-aka-The-Wizard-of-Oz%0A) was once asked by a TI on Quora (paraphrasing): “Do you realize you’re sometimes responding to perps’ comments and posts?” His answer (also paraphrasing): yes — but I answered truthfully, so it doesn’t matter that it was a perp, because a real TI will read the same answer and get value from it.

I follow the same reasoning. So, a periodic reminder: I give people the benefit of the doubt, which means I sometimes invite people who might be perps into TI Skills Economy (TSE). Two things follow from that:

1. It’s our community’s responsibility to tell each other when someone is a perp. When I’m told, I delete their account as soon as possible.

2. To limit a perp’s impact in the meantime, the app gates access and transparently shows who is providing material value to the community and who is not — so you can see who is worth interacting with.
