> There are different ways. The most plausible:    They are following you and asking you questions. This will influence the algorithm to push their gaslighting content to you.    You are accidentally following a perp, and &hellip;

<p>There are different ways. The most plausible:</p> <ol> <li> <p>They are following you and asking you questions. This will influence the algorithm to push their gaslighting content to you.</p> </li> <li> <p>You are accidentally following a perp, and as stated above, the aglo will assume you like the content</p> </li> <li> <p>The spaces you follow have perps and perp content, as stated above, algo will be tricked into pushing certain content to you</p> </li> <li> <p>You accidentally upvoted perp content, as stated above, the algo will think you like that content</p> </li> </ol> <p>This is why up- and down-voting is important, as well as who you are following. Also, muting and blocking will clean up your feed.</p> <p>It would not have to be your neighbors doing it. Anyone who wants to troll you can do the above.</p> <p>Lastly, if they compromised your network, you are experiencing a man-in-the-middle attack.</p> <p>“man-in-the-middle (MitM) attack”: the hacker intercepts the communication between your browser and the website without compromising the actual server hosting the website.</p> <p>This allows them to manipulate the HTML content that is sent to the user’s browser, effectively altering what the user sees on their screen, such as injecting malicious code, ads, or phishing forms.</p> <p>Additionally, another similar technique is called “browser hijacking,” where malware affects the user’s browser, causing it to display altered web pages. However, MitM is more focused on intercepting traffic rather than altering the browser itself.</p> <p>Quora: <a href="https://qr.ae/pCnJhE">https://qr.ae/pCnJhE</a></p>
