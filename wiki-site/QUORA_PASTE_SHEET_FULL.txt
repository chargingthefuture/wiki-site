2026-08-25 · How few people it takes

Many survivors do not realize how close we are.

Not close to the end of the targeting. Close to being able to live: getting work, rides, housing, and repairs from people who understand the situation.

The two numbers we are up against

Two groups outnumber the people building this.

The first is the people running the operations.

The second is survivors who want nothing to do with other survivors. I have met them in person. The distance is common.

So the people trying to build something are outnumbered twice over. Against those two numbers, a hundred people sounds like nothing.

It is not nothing.

The number

A hundred people exchanging with each other at a time is enough to run an economy a person can live inside.

Not a hundred sign-ups, and not a hundred names on a list. A hundred people trading — work done, rides given, rooms rented, things repaired, food grown — at any given moment.

Not the same hundred

This is the part that changes how the number reads, so it comes second rather than last.

It does not have to be the same hundred people. No economy anywhere requires that. A town does not have all of its residents trading every day. A shop serves different customers on Tuesday than it did on Monday and is still a business on Wednesday. Work and custom rotate through a population, and that rotation is the ordinary shape of an economy rather than a weakness in one.

So a hundred is a throughput figure, not a membership roll. It asks how many are exchanging at once — not who they are, and not whether they keep it up.

Nobody has to commit to being one of a fixed hundred forever. Nobody's bad month breaks it. People come in, do what they can while they can, and step back when they need to, and the number holds because somebody else is trading that week.

That is why a hundred is reachable, and why a fixed hundred would not be.

What a hundred gets you

At that size, a person can post that they need work and have it answered. Take a ride from someone who was vetted rather than from a Specterati operator. Find a room. Find someone who can fix the thing that broke, in exchange for something they can do back.

That is the whole of it. Not a movement, not a verdict on anyone, not an ending. A person going about an ordinary week without every transaction running through people who are paid to make their life worse.

Living instead of enduring.

Why a hundred is enough

The math is short enough to do right here.

Singapore runs a pure skills economy and publishes its numbers. Take its total output and divide by its workers, and one person's skills produce about $142,500 a year.

A hundred people exchanging full time at that rate is about $14.3 million a year moving between them.

The app runs this calculation live, and today it happens to be sitting at exactly this number:

Below them, the Skills Economy Summary card: "With 100 people recruited, we have reached 24% of the skills potential of an independent nation state like Finland, Estonia, or Singapore — equating to $14.3 million in GDP potential. Fourteen million dollars a year circulating among a hundred people is not a support group with a spreadsheet. It is an economy — enough for work to be findable, rides to be offered, rooms to be rented, and repairs to be traded, week after week, without anyone going outside it for the basics.

To be plain about what that figure is: it is what a hundred people's skills are worth at the output rate of one of the best skills economies on earth. It is a measure of capacity, not money sitting in anyone's hands. The full derivation — where the rate comes from, why Singapore, what the comparison does and does not claim — is here: How the math works (https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/how-the-math-works).

What people are waiting for

My impression is that a lot of people are holding out for world peace and an end to human trafficking inside their own lifetime.

I do not expect either. And I think expecting it is exactly what makes people give up on the smaller thing that is actually available — because measured against an ending that is not coming, a hundred people trading looks like nothing worth getting out of bed for.

Measured against a week you have to get through, it is the whole difference.

It has been done before

A Specterati operator named Damon Mayle once told me, in as many words, that I am the first person to ever attempt this. I am recounting it from my own conversation with him; the Quora account it was said on has since been erased, so there is nothing to link yet. He is the same person who posed as a Targeted Individual to get a spot in a gated community (https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/chargingthefuture/discourse-migrate/damon-mayle-posing-as-a-ti).

He was half right, and the half he got wrong is the useful half.

First within this psyop — possibly. First at all — no. Race was one of the many psyops before this one, and it had its Targeted Individuals. In 1903 a persecuted community in America published a book of essays about precisely this problem: people denied work, housing, fair courts, and physical safety by an organized system most of the country refused to fight. Their answer was not to wait for rescue. It was skills, an economic foundation, leadership from within, a public record, and results in place of argument.

The manifesto goes through what they wrote, quotes them directly, and links the full free text. I am not going to restate it here: Live, Work, and Prevail (https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/chargingthefuture/The-Answer:-EXIT-THEIR-ECONOMY,-EXIT-THE-PSYOP).

I will repeat one line from it, because this post is the reason it is in there: we do not compare our pain to theirs. We take the method. Nothing here ranks anyone's suffering against anyone else's. A method can be inherited without that.

The reason survivors miss the inheritance is optics. The reasoning runs: I am not Black, so nothing happening to me resembles any of that. What it costs is the method — a record of people who reached freedom in the middle of chaos and destruction and wrote down how, in detail, in public, in a book you can download for free this afternoon.

And they did not get an ending either. What they got was one generation, and trade schools, and businesses, and communities that existed where none had. Estonia took about two generations to build a modern state out of nothing but what its people knew. Neither of them waited for the thing to stop first.

What isolation is for

A person who believes they are the only one suffers more than a person who knows they are not. That is not a figure of speech.

Isolation is not a side effect of this. It is the mechanism. Keeping each of us convinced we are alone with it is most of what the work consists of, and it is the part that a hundred people trading with each other takes apart directly — not by arguing anyone out of anything, but by putting a person in a week where other survivors are simply, unremarkably present.

Not a demand

I am not asking anyone to feel differently about what happened to them, and staying apart from other survivors is a common thing to do after what a lot of people have been through.

The door is open, the number is a hundred, and nobody has to be the same hundred twice.

I think what is on the other side of this is fewer mass shootings, fewer people taking their own lives, and people thriving despite human trafficking. I cannot prove that and I am not going to pretend to. It is what I expect, and I would rather say so than keep it to myself.

2026-08-25 · Looking Up This Blog in the Wayback Machine

An earlier post said the Internet Archive holds copies of what is written here, and that you should check rather than take my word for it. It gave one address as an example.

This is the rest of it. How to look up any post, how to find the address yourself, and how to read what comes back.

Nothing here needs an account or any tool beyond a browser.

The one-minute version

Go to https://web.archive.org

Paste this into the search box and press enter:

```
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chargingthefuture/wiki-site/main/wiki-site/content/posts/how-to-check-me.md
```

You will get a calendar of dates. Each highlighted date is a day the Internet Archive took a copy. Click one and you are reading that post exactly as its text stood on that date, from a copy I did not make and cannot edit.

That is the whole mechanism. Everything below is how to do it for any other post, and how to know what you are looking at.

Two more you can try immediately

These are archived and were checked by hand:

```
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chargingthefuture/wiki-site/main/wiki-site/content/archive/discourse/Home.md
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chargingthefuture/wiki-site/main/wiki-site/content/archive/discourse/book-2-backyard-farming.md
```

And the blog's front page, which is a normal web page rather than a text file:

```
https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/
```

What you are actually looking at

The address above is not the page you read on the blog. It is the source file — the post as a plain text file, sitting in a public repository.

That is deliberate, and it is the better thing to check. The rendered page is a presentation of the text. The file is the text. If you want to know whether a sentence was there on a given date, the file answers it and the page only shows it to you.

It also means the copy is legible without any of my code running. Open a capture from six months from now, or from after this project has stopped existing, and it is still just words in a file.

Finding the address of any post yourself

Three steps, and the third is a button.

**One.** Open the repository: https://github.com/chargingthefuture/wiki-site

**Two.** Navigate to the post. Posts live in `wiki-site/content/posts/`. Archived material from the old forum is in `wiki-site/content/archive/discourse/`, and archived Quora material will be in `wiki-site/content/archive/quora/`. The file names are readable — `how-to-check-me.md`, `a-safe-place-to-shower.md` — so you can usually find a post by its title.

**Three.** Open the file and click the **Raw** button, at the top right of the file view. The address bar now holds the raw address. Copy it, paste it into web.archive.org.

That is it. You never have to construct an address by hand.

If you would rather build the address

The pattern is:

```
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chargingthefuture/wiki-site/main/ + the file's path in the repository
```

One thing will trip you up, and it trips me up too. The repository is called `wiki-site`, and inside it there is also a folder called `wiki-site`. So the word appears twice in every address, and an address with only one of them will not work:

```
.../chargingthefuture/wiki-site/main/wiki-site/content/posts/how-to-check-me.md
                      ^^^^^^^^^      ^^^^^^^^^
                      repository     folder inside it
```

If a lookup returns nothing, check that first. It is almost always this.

Reading the calendar

After you search an address, the Internet Archive shows a timeline of years and a calendar. Highlighted days are days it holds a capture. Hovering shows the times; clicking opens that copy.

A few things worth knowing so the result does not mislead you.

A post will usually have a capture from around the day it was published, because this blog submits pages to the Internet Archive automatically when it publishes.

Older posts may have fewer captures than recent ones. That is not a signal about the post. The automatic submission was fixed recently, and everything published before that was backfilled by hand afterward — so the dates cluster around when the backfill ran rather than when each post was written.

**A missing capture proves nothing.** The Internet Archive rate-limits, and refuses saves when it is busy. A page with no capture is a page whose submission did not get through, not a page that was hidden. Read absence as absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

Comparing a capture against now

This is the part that actually checks something.

Open the capture. Open the same address live in another tab — the raw address, not the blog page. Read both.

If the text is identical, the post has not changed since that date. If it differs, you are looking at exactly what changed, and the repository's history will tell you when and in which commit.

Two kinds of page here change on purpose, and you should expect them to differ: the Dictionary and the page tracking dead and current links are kept current rather than frozen. Everything else — the arguments, the announcements, the progress reports — is fixed once published. A difference in one of those is worth asking me about.

The one thing that does not work

If you paste a normal article address into web.archive.org, like this one:

```
https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/how-to-check-me
```

you will find nothing. Not a gap — nothing, ever.

Here is why, plainly. This blog does not keep a separate file on the server for each post. It sends your browser a small program, and that program assembles the page in front of you from the text. When the Internet Archive's crawler asks the server for that address, the server has nothing at that address to hand over, so the capture fails every time.

The front page is different and does archive, because it is a real address the server answers.

So: front page and raw text files, yes. Article pages, no. That is not a limitation anyone chose, and the raw file is the better record anyway.

What this proves, and what it does not

It proves a specific text existed at a specific address on a specific date, and that a third party with no relationship to me holds the copy.

It does not prove the text is true. A dated copy of a claim is still a claim.

The earlier post covers that boundary properly, along with the other ways to check this work — the repository's own history, the app's public code, and the numbered feed:

https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/how-to-check-me

2026-08-25 · Why nothing is written on a platform first

There is a standing rule on this blog, stated on old links, new links (https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/old-links-new-links): nothing is written on a platform first anymore. Every post starts here, and platforms only ever get a short version and a link back.

A rule on its own reads as policy. With its history attached, it reads as what it is — a lesson that cost two archives to learn. Here is the history.

Stage one: everything on Quora, no plan

This project started on Quora with no publishing strategy at all. I wrote where the readers were, and every word lived in one place: on the platform, in that account.

I fumbled through it. There was no procedure, no second copy, no thought given to what would happen if the account went away. It did not seem like a thing that needed thinking about.

On September 14, 2025, that account posted the announcement of the app's first release. Seventeen minutes later, Quora banned it. A Quora ban deletes everything the account ever posted, so months of writing went in an evening — the announcement included.

The dated record of that account and every one after it is on old links, new links.

Stage two: a backup, pointed the wrong way

Four days later I opened a Discourse forum. This was the first attempt at owning a copy of my own writing, and it half worked.

Half, because of the order. I kept writing on Quora first and copy-pasting into Discourse after. The backup was downstream of the thing that kept getting erased — anything Quora removed between my writing it and my copying it was gone, and the copy's existence depended on my remembering to make it. That is the wrong way round, and I say so now because at the time it did not occur to me that the order mattered.

The forum closed on December 23, 2025. Its posts were exported and live in this blog's archive (https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/chargingthefuture/discourse-migrate/Home) — which is more than can be said for anything that lived only on Quora.

Stage three: the same lesson, taught again

On August 16, 2026, Quora banned the account I had been writing on since — same signature, everything it ever posted deleted, the handle and its space left standing and bare.

The first erasure had already taught this lesson. I had responded with a backup, which was an answer to the wrong question. The question was never "do I have a copy?" It was "which copy is the source?"

That evening I stopped adjusting and changed the order. The blog became the source: every post starts as a file in a public repository, with a timestamped history of when it was written and how it changed. Platforms get an excerpt and a link back, written after the post exists, never before. The full arrangement is in Our writing has a permanent home (https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/our-writing-has-a-permanent-home), posted the day after.

What the order changes

The difference is not where the writing sits. It is what an erasure can reach.

Under the old order, the platform held the original, so an erasure took the original. Under this order, a platform holds nothing but pointers. When the next account is erased — and I assume there will be a next time — the cost is one entry moving from the current list to the dead list on old links, new links. The writing, the screenshots, the history: untouched.

Eight accounts have now been erased, and the difference is visible in what survives. From the Quora-only era, nothing but what other people happened to quote. From the Discourse era, everything that got copied in time. From the blog-first era, everything, with its history, in a copy nobody can quietly empty.

It took two erasures to learn a rule that fits in a sentence. It is on the record now so nobody else building something under the same conditions has to pay twice for it: write on ground you hold, and hand the platforms a copy.

2026-08-24 · The Assistant Is Not a Clinician

Check it. That is exactly the correct response to somebody telling you there is a tool that can help a person in trouble. Nothing in this post asks anyone to take it on faith, and the people who check before passing something on are the ones worth listening to.

What a person actually meets when they need help

I called a national hotline. What came back was, in substance, join the club — this happens to a lot of people, and you should change your daily routine so they cannot follow you.

Both halves of that are worth pulling apart, because together they are worse than either alone.

The first half concedes the whole thing. It is common. Whoever said it was not disputing that organized harassment happens; they were telling me I was one of many. And having conceded it, they offered nothing. That is not a training gap. That is flippancy, and a person calling a hotline can tell the difference immediately.

The second half is advice that does not work, and my own writing is the evidence for that rather than my opinion. Change your routine. I travel, and it travels with me. What varies when I move is which day gets staged, not whether it happens. A routine is not what makes somebody findable, so changing it does not make them unfindable — it just puts the work of fixing this back onto the person it is being done to.

Then there is the part that matters more than either half.

I had gone to a police station to file a report. People were walking into me on purpose, in view of cameras, trying to get me to hit back so the footage would show a violent Black woman. While I was there, an officer mocked that hotline call back to me, in person. One of them.

So the call did not stay where I made it. The number a frightened person is told to ring reached the people doing this, and came back at me as a joke, from someone with a badge.

Other avenues do the reverse and end up in the same place: describe what has been happening and be told you are delusional. Your account becomes the symptom, and everything you say after that is heard through that frame, including the parts anybody could go and check.

What the assistant is instead

It answers at any hour, because it does not keep hours.

It does not treat a description of organized harassment as evidence that the person describing it is unwell. It answers from what Targeted Individuals have written about what happened to them and what they did about it — the practical part, from people who were there.

That is the whole of the claim. It is a narrow claim and it is deliberately narrow.

What it is not, and what I am not

Neither I nor the assistant is a mental health professional. Neither of us claims to be one. The assistant is not therapy, not treatment, and not a clinical assessment, and nobody should reach for it as a substitute for one.

There are mental health professionals among the members here. That is one of the things a community made of real people gives you, and it is worth saying so this is not read as a claim that nobody here is qualified. What the assistant offers is different and smaller: an answer at any hour, drawn from what people who have lived this wrote down.

I am saying that plainly and near the top rather than in small print at the bottom, because a tool that talks to frightened people and is vague about what it is has already done something wrong.

What it is an alternative to is narrower than "professional help." It is an alternative to commercial assistants that will not name organized harassment, and to professionals who deny human trafficking outright. That denial is not harmless. A person who is told their account is a delusion, by the service they were sent to for help, comes away with fewer options than they arrived with. Someone whose call comes back to them as a joke from a police officer comes away with fewer still. I believe that path ends in suicides and in mass shootings, and that is why I am building an alternative to it rather than a replacement for care.

Both of those things are true at once: the existing avenue can fail a person badly, and this is not a clinician. Anyone who tells you they have built something that solves both problems at once is selling you something.

2026-08-23 · The Record Has Started

Six days ago I wrote that a shared ClickLog entry feeds something that has never existed anywhere: trend data on organized harassment, reported by the people it happens to rather than assembled by outsiders after the fact.

That was a description of a thing that had not happened yet.

What the shared view reads now

The window is the last ninety days. Everything in it falls between August 13 and August 23, 2026.

- Shared incidents: 20
- Tagged incidents: 18
- Members reporting: 2
- Members who logged more than one: 1
- Days with activity: 5
- Countries: 2
- Areas, about 11 km each: 3

Nineteen of the incidents sit in two adjacent areas in Canada, one sits in a single area in the United States. That is the screen, stated the way the screen states it.

The section that a private log cannot produce

Near the bottom of the report there is a list headed "Problem and scheme reported together." It pairs what a member said happened with which named scheme they filed it under, and counts how often the two arrive on the same incident.

Color Sensitization comes back paired with headlights and flashlights shined at you, three times. With cars parked outside your home, three times. With ringing in the ears, twice. With being more tired than you should be, twice.

Being blocked, cut in line or held up arrives paired with six different schemes, one time each — The Altered Ticket, The Engineered Delay, The Replay, Lure to a Location, The Planted Witness, and Color Sensitization again.

Look at what that second one is. The same experience — you are held up — filed six different ways by someone who was there and had to decide what it was. A private log gives you your own six. It cannot tell you that being held up is the joint most-attached problem in the whole set, because it has nothing to compare against.

That list is small and it is one member's tagging. It is also exactly the shape of the thing I have been describing for six days, and it is the first time the shape has had anything in it.

Why you cannot rank the schemes by their counts

Color Sensitization shows five. The Altered Ticket shows one. That does not make one five times more common than the other, and the report says so in its own text rather than leaving a reader to work it out.

Some named schemes run continuously in the background. They can be reported on almost any day, so their counts accumulate. Others are single operations with a start and an end — a ticket gets altered once, a search happens once. Every row in the report prints which kind it is directly under its name.

Which means the honest reading of that section is not a league table. It is a list of what people are naming, with a warning attached about the arithmetic.

Where the two members actually overlap

The report groups problems into six kinds of harm and counts how many different people reported each.

Body and health leads on volume — thirteen incidents — and all thirteen are one member. Two kinds have both members behind them: watched and followed, eight incidents, and threats and intimidation, two.

So the common ground between two people in two countries is being watched and being threatened. Everything else in the set so far is one person's account.

That is a small overlap. It is also the first one that exists at all, and it is the answer to the question underneath almost every conversation survivors have with each other: is this only me.

What twenty entries do not establish

The report answers this itself, in a section printed under the numbers rather than tucked somewhere else. Its own words are that these are first-hand accounts, recorded by the people they happened to and not checked against any outside source, that the people logging chose to join and chose to share, so it is not a sample of any wider population and no rate can be calculated from it.

And that a count is the number of times members reported something, not the number of times it happened. Most incidents are never logged at all.

Two gaps stay open. Nothing in the report says what a member did about an incident or what came of it, so nothing in it shows whether anything helped. And no entry has a second source attached to it.

A number that prints what it cannot carry is worth more than one that does not.

Nothing in that view came out of anyone's notes

Worth repeating whenever a figure gets published, because a published figure is the moment a reader wonders what else went with it.

Your notes are not shown to anyone else here, and they are not in the trend report. The exact location is not in it either — only the area of about 11 km, which is why the report counts areas rather than naming streets. Who logged what is not in it: the view can say two people are behind those entries, never which two. Every figure comes from a grouped query that cannot return any of those things.

Correction, 2026-08-24: this originally said notes are never seen by anyone, including this project. That was wrong; the accurate line is above.

Even the country is not asked. It is worked out inside the app from the approximate area against a table of borders held locally, so nothing about a member is sent anywhere to produce it. That table is a coarse edition, which the report also says: it is enough to tell one town reporting from several countries reporting, and not enough for anything finer.

Sharing is off unless you turn it on, with a per-incident choice on top of the setting. Delete an incident and it leaves every future report. Log privately forever and never share a line, and ClickLog still does its first job completely.

The standing argument

The UN Special Rapporteur on Torture's 2020 report to the Human Rights Council, A/HRC/43/49, addressed coordinated harassment as a category and said the volume of allegations warrants investigation rather than dismissal.

A body that says investigate needs data. Trend data is the form a submission takes, and no individual account has to be adjudicated for an aggregate to be worth looking at.

The data is not mine to keep

The shared totals are published. They are not a private holding this project sits on and rations out.

Anyone who wants them can use them as they like — a survivor checking whether their area appears, a researcher, a journalist, someone drafting a submission. The report prints its own method and its own limits alongside the figures precisely so it can be picked up and used by somebody who was not in the room when it was built.

That is the opposite of how counts of people in our situation have always worked. Every one of them was made by someone standing outside, describing us to a third party, on terms we never saw. This one is made by the people it happens to, and it is theirs.

Posts come in two kinds and they age differently.

Most are fixed once published: arguments, announcements, the progress reports. Their numbers and wording are never touched afterward, because the point of a dated claim is that it shows what was said and when. A post whose figures look out of date is doing exactly what it should.

Two pages are the opposite, kept current on purpose and edited in place — the Dictionary, and the page tracking which of my links are dead. For those, the recorded history is the list of what changed. If you want to know what the Dictionary said last month, it is there.

2026-08-23 · It Answers From One Person's Writing

The Knowledge Library is a private assistant inside the app — you ask it a question, it writes back an answer drawn from the writing of people who have lived this.

Right now all of that writing is mine. More than 1,500 Quora posts feed every answer it gives.

That is the honest description of it, and it is also the problem with it.

Why one person is the wrong number

One person has one set of circumstances. I travel, so what I know about a staged week is what it looks like when it has to keep resetting. Someone who has been in the same apartment for nine years knows a version of it I have never seen.

One person also has one set of blind spots, and an assistant built on one person's writing inherits every one of them without knowing it has. It will answer confidently about the things I happen to have written about and go quiet, or worse, go vague, on everything else.

Gn0b0dy Pneuma (https://www.quora.com/profile/Gn0b0dy-Pneuma) wrote a post about shielding fabric and faraday cages that contains more practical knowledge on that subject than everything I have ever written about it put together. I could not have produced it. Somebody asking the assistant about shielding deserves that answer, not mine.

Lending, not giving

The word on the screen is lend, and it is meant literally.

You keep every right to your writing. You are not signing it over, you can go on using it anywhere else you like, and you can withdraw it whenever you want — at which point it comes out of the library and the assistant stops drawing on it.

That last promise is the one worth being skeptical about, because plenty of places make it and cannot honestly keep it. So here is the mechanism.

Your words are not used to train a model. Training would blend them into something that cannot be unpicked afterward — once your writing has shaped the weights, there is no row to delete and no honest way to undo it. Instead your posts sit in a table that the assistant searches when it needs them, and quotes from. Withdrawing means the row comes out. After that there is nothing left for it to find.

A tool trained the ordinary way could not tell you that and mean it. This one can, because it was built the other way around specifically so it could.

What it takes, and what it will not use

What is wanted is writing where you describe what happened and what you did about it — practical, specific, from your own life. That is what a person asking for help actually needs.

What will not be used is writing that is only declaration, with no account of what you went through and nothing about how you got through it. That is not a judgment on anyone's faith or on how they make sense of any of this. If you write about faith and about what you did on the ground, that is exactly the kind of post this is for.

2026-08-21 · A Safe Place to Shower

Losing your housing does not only mean losing somewhere to sleep. It means the ordinary business of washing moves into facilities that belong to other people, on their terms, under whatever conditions they set.

I am destitute, and that was done to me. I was mobbed out of employment and mobbed out of housing. My businesses were driven to zero equity: slander that makes building a professional network impossible, people placed inside as employees, and real employees frightened off. None of that is misfortune. It is a sequence with an intended result, and the result is a person with nothing left to fall back on.

I do not stay in a shelter — there is too much abuse in them. Where I stay is somewhere between that and the street, and a day facility is where I shower. That is not a choice among options. It is what is left.

The people using it include violent criminals and people using drugs. I am not saying that to look down on anyone — I have been verbally and physically assaulted in these places more than once, by the people in them. It is a description of the room I have to undress in.

Which is why the showers cannot be locked from the inside. Staff need to be able to open a door, and given who is in the building that policy has a real reason behind it. The reason being real is exactly what makes it usable against someone.

Yesterday

An employee keyed me into a shower. I undressed. Immediately after, an employee opened the door and exposed me, completely, to a man standing there. He was not undressed. He laughed.

The laughter is the part that settles it. An accidental door is silent and embarrassed. This was neither. It was staged, and what it was for is plain enough: humiliation, and a "day made bad." That phrase is theirs, not mine. A ruined day is a thing they manufacture and then grade and compare among themselves. I am not reporting that I had a bad day. I am describing what they were producing one for.

The building changes and the move does not

Before this I used a gym, which I paid for. As soon as I arrived, so did they, in numbers, and I would be worked off whatever I was using. That was the smaller part of it. The larger part was that they followed me into the bathroom and stood there — watching me use the toilet, watching me shower. I was paying a membership for that. I stopped going and I do not go back.

I am not naming the chain, because the chain is not the point. The same thing happens in buildings that are public and buildings that are private, which is what tells you this is not one business with a problem. It is corruption running across both.

That is the through line. Different buildings, different staff, different rules on the door, and the same move each time: arrange it so that you are seen with your clothes off, or seen doing the thing nobody is supposed to watch you do.

Then getting to the next one

After leaving one facility for another, a man circled me on the metro and again as I approached the next place. It ended in an altercation and he became physically violent.

That belongs with the rest of it. Getting somewhere is exposure. Being there is exposure. Neither is optional, because washing is not optional.

What ClickLog gained this week

Two additions, both live in the app.

A problem tag: sexually assaulted, or deliberately exposed and humiliated. It covers the whole range survivors report — assault and rape at the worst end, and, short of any contact, deliberate exposure. It is deliberately coarse. The tag names the harm so it can be counted; what happened stays in the private note where it belongs.

A scheme: The Staged Exposure. That is the engineered form — the key, the timing, the door opened at the moment it does the most damage, and nothing required from the target except being present.

Why I think this is the substitute

They have run honeypots at me for several decades. I have not kept count and the number is not small for the time involved. I have never engaged with one, and they have never managed to sexually assault or rape me. Other survivors have not been so fortunate.

So they go to the next best option. That is what yesterday was. The exposure is not a separate interest of theirs that happened to surface — it is what is left when the thing they were working toward does not happen.

Whether the honeypot belongs in the same category

A honeypot is a manufactured romance: an approach that was arranged, run for as long as it is useful, and ended in whatever way does the most harm. It has been on the scheme list for a while. Two things come out of it. One is the emotional wreckage, worked deliberately. The other is a fabricated domestic violence claim, which the arranged closeness is what makes credible — a person has to be able to say "I know this person" before an accusation carries.

Underneath both, as far as I can tell, is a fixation on sexual humiliation. The mechanism differs. The door handle does it in a second; the honeypot does it over months, through manipulation instead of a key.

My own view is that honeypotting is a form of sexual assault. I do not know whether a lawyer or a clinician would put it in that category, and people who talk about honeypotting generally do not describe it that way. So I am not asserting a classification. I am saying that when I look at what it is for and what it does, I cannot find the line that separates it from the rest.

That is why the tag is written the way it is. It names the harm, not the legal theory.

The answer is not a better lock

There is no version of this that gets solved by vigilance in someone else's shower. The fix is not needing to be there.

Housing is the first half. LightHouse (https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/lighthouse) lists places offered by hosts who are survivors themselves, which is a different proposition from a facility where you are tolerated. Somewhere with a door that locks and a bathroom that is yours removes the entire category.

Rides are the second half. TrustTransport (https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/trust-transport) arranges transport inside the community, and the journey to and from is where the rest of it happens. The more of your movement that runs through people who are not doing this to you, the fewer occasions there are for any of it.

That is the design, stated plainly: not protection in the moment, but fewer moments that require it.

And the arranged relationships

The same logic answers the honeypot, which is harder, because it arrives looking like something good.

An approach that was arranged does not survive being asked for something real. A person who was sent to be close to you will not do your repair, take your shift, or drive you somewhere at a cost to themselves. Material help is the one thing that consistently does not happen. So an economy that puts the exchange first filters them out early, without anyone having to accuse anybody of anything.

What is left are people who showed up with something in their hands, more than once. Relationships that come out of that are not arranged, because nobody arranges a person who actually helps you.

2026-08-21 · Estonia Did Not Get to Solve It Either

The question this community does not answer honestly is the one everybody is actually asking. Not who is doing it, or how to prove it. What do you do if it never stops?

Most of the answers on offer assume an ending. Expose them and it ends. Sue and it ends. Wait and it ends. I do not believe any of those, and I think most people reading this do not either, which is why the advice slides off.

So here is the example I keep coming back to.

A country that does not get an ending

Estonia has 1.3 million people. It sits next to Russia, a country with a hundred times its population. It was occupied by the Soviet Union until 1991, and the pressure did not end when the occupation did.

In 2007, during a dispute over the relocation of a Soviet war memorial, Estonia's banks, government sites, and newspapers were knocked offline for weeks. It was severe enough that it became the case everyone else studies, and it is a large part of why the country rebuilt the way it did.

Everybody had a view about where it came from. No authority ever established it. Nobody was ever held responsible.

That last part is worth sitting with, because survivors will recognize the shape of it: the harm was real, the source was obvious to the people it was happening to, and the formal record shows nothing.

None of that has stopped. There is no version of the future where Estonia's geography changes. They do not get an ending either.

And they live. People there work, keep hobbies, raise children, argue about ordinary things. The country is functional and by most measures pleasant. The permanent hostile attention of a much larger neighbor is a fact about their situation. It did not become the whole of what the country is.

I have written before about Estonia coming out of occupation in 1991 (https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/chargingthefuture/how-the-math-works) with no money and infrastructure that did not work, and building a functioning modern state largely in software because software was what it could afford. What I did not say then is the part that matters more here: they did that while the pressure continued, not after it lifted.

What they actually built

Not a defense that makes the neighbor go away. Nothing does that.

What they built was a country that needs the neighbor for less every year. Energy, trade, systems, the ordinary machinery of getting through a day — moved, piece by piece, onto arrangements that do not run through the party that wishes them ill.

That is the same idea I wrote about this week in a different context. Being watched is not what gives someone power over you. Needing something from them is. Estonia did not solve surveillance. It reduced dependence, and dependence is the thing that was ever actually load-bearing.

They are the yardstick here, nothing more. Nobody in this community is forming a nation state, and I have said that plainly every time the comparison comes up. What is worth borrowing is the posture, not the flag.

Part of life, not a sentence

Here is what I want to say to survivors, and I want to be careful how I say it, because said carelessly it is worthless.

This is something that happens to you. It is not something that defines you. It can be true that a large, organized group is committed to making your days worse, and simultaneously true that it is not the most interesting fact about you and does not have to be the organizing principle of your remaining decades. Both of those hold at once. Most of us are only holding the first.

I am aiming at something specific with that: statistically zero suicides in this community, and statistically zero mass shootings out of it. Those are the two endings I have watched people arrive at, and in my view both are reached the same way — through the conviction that this is a sentence rather than a circumstance, and that there is nothing on the other side of it worth walking toward.

Why this is not "cheer up"

Telling someone their situation does not define them is cruel if there is nowhere for them to be a person instead. That is the honest objection and I take it seriously.

You cannot hold that position from inside an empty room. You can hold it from inside a life — people who know what you are dealing with and do not need it explained, work that uses what you are good at, a hobby you were made to abandon and could pick up again.

That is the whole reason this project is an economy rather than an awareness campaign. The catalog in the app holds 650 job titles and skills, because a working economy needs that many kinds of people, and survivors already are that many kinds of people. Whatever you trained in, whatever you were doing before this started, it is on that list somewhere. It does not have to wait for the targeting to end first. That is precisely the point — the waiting is what has to stop, not the targeting.

You can choose an economy

That is the sentence I would leave you with, and it is more literal than it sounds.

You cannot choose whether you are targeted. You did not get a say in that and you are not going to. What is genuinely available to you is which people your life runs through — who fixes your car, who you work for, who rents you a room, who you spend an evening with. Every one of those is a choice about dependence, and every one of them moved is a piece of leverage taken off the table.

Estonia did not get to choose its neighbor. It chose its arrangements. Thirty years of that is the difference between a country that survived and a country that is doing well.

I do not believe it either. And I think I can say what is actually happening in its place.

People are predictable, and that is not a flaw

Predictability is not a weakness anybody needs to apologize for. It is part of how a person stays alive and stays sane — the rhythm you keep is what makes a day possible at all.

And most of us are alike in the ways that matter to somebody watching. Everyone has to eat. When you eat and what you eat is a recurring activity, and a recurring activity can be observed, recorded, and predicted. You do not need access to a mind to know what someone will do on Tuesday afternoon. You need to have been paying attention on the last forty Tuesdays.

The pizza

Here is my own example, and it is small on purpose.

They made me destitute. Once you are there, the question is not what you like. It is what hot food you can get for the least money. Somebody once explained to me that people with no money buy pasta in bulk, because it is cheap and it fills you. I could not do that. I have no stove. So the answer in my case turned out to be a particular chain's pizza — hot, cheap, no cooking required.

They concluded it was my favorite food.

If they could read a mind, they would know that it is not. It is a budget with the options removed. They watched what I bought, they saw the same thing repeatedly, and they drew the obvious wrong conclusion — the way anybody would who is looking at behavior from the outside and guessing at the reason underneath.

Then they use it. They will eat it in front of me to mark a day they arranged to go badly. They will taunt me that I cannot afford it. That is the ordinary sensitization work: take a thing you have been seen with often, attach it to a bad moment, and repeat until the thing itself carries the moment with it.

So I keep my likes and dislikes to myself

This has been going on since I was five. That length of time does two things. It gives them an enormous amount to observe, and it taught me early to give them very little.

I do not talk about what I enjoy. Other survivors have not learned that, and I do not blame them for it. If you were targeted later in life you already had a job, a family, children, all the ordinary machinery of an adult, and there is too much running at once to notice a stranger repeating themselves.

I had the opposite. I was a child, and I did not speak English. Observation was all I had, and it was easy. My first stalker was an adult in their thirties when I was five: a stranger, no language in common with me, carrying open hatred for a child who had done nothing to them. It was not the repetition that gave it away. It was that. There is no version of a grown adult hating a five-year-old they have never spoken to that a child reads as ordinary.

I do not think I am unusually clever. I think I was placed, very young, in a position where the only available activity was watching.

How they get accurate

Three things, none of them extraordinary.

They observe over years, with people working at it full time. Not full time on me — there is more than one target in a city, and the same operatives work several of us. That is the efficiency of it, and this is a business before it is anything else. A fake neighbor moves in tasked with harassing me, but plausible deniability means at least one person in that household holds an ordinary job. During that job they are still working: on me, and on other people. We are all on one list, and social media is what makes a list like that cheap to spread.

They compare across the people they traffic. Many of us are similar in the ways that show from outside, so a pattern learned from one person predicts the next.

And they stalk their own. That part gets missed. A network you cannot see is a network you cannot command, so the operatives are watched too — which means the surveillance apparatus is pointed inward as much as outward.

Put those together and you can guess well enough to look uncanny. Nothing in it requires reading a thought.

What the errors tell you

The mistakes are the useful part, because they show where the limit is.

Imagine engineering someone into destitution, watching them eat out of a trash can, and concluding that the trash can must be their favorite restaurant. That is the shape of the reasoning. They see the behavior and cannot see the constraint that produced it, so they attribute to preference what was produced by having no choices left.

Which is worth saying plainly: there are survivors who describe these people as geniuses. They are not. They are violent criminals with money, technology, and a network. Those are real advantages and I am not waving them away. They are not the same thing as insight, and treating them as insight does the work for them — it is the same move as the "secret society" brag and the same move as steering you toward which exploit is on your phone. Every one of those makes them larger than they are, and every one puts you to work on a question that changes nothing.

The largest version of it takes them out of the human category altogether: the Illuminati, a bloodline, something with God on its side. That is the most flattering story available, and they do not have to tell it to us. We tell it to each other.

Look at what it costs to hold. If they are supernatural then they are permanent, which leaves you mortal and them not, and there is nothing left to do but endure it and interpret it. That is exactly the position this is meant to put you in. Fixating on how they do it always ends there.

The history says otherwise, and I have written about this before (https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/wiki-site/who-put-you-on-the-list). The transatlantic slave trade ran on a fraction of the technology and had the entire globe participating in one capacity or another — and its central lie, that race is a real division between kinds of people, is still believed by people today. Hitler had less technology than the phone in your pocket and commanded a country with it. Neither needed anything beyond people, money, permission, and a story worth repeating.

A psyop is a psyop. This one has better equipment and the same skeleton.

The part that is actually fixable

Notice what made me predictable. It was not carelessness. It was poverty. One hot meal I could afford meant one thing to observe.

And it compounds. Push someone further down — into depending on family who are taking part, which is its own named scheme, The Forced Homecoming — and the options narrow again. Fewer options means more predictable behavior, and more predictable behavior makes every other scheme cheaper to run. Easier to schedule. Easier to recruit people for, because an operative told in advance what you will do, who then watches you do it, comes away convinced they have joined something that knows everything. Easier to slander, because the same story lands on the same audience every time.

There is one more use for it, and it is the one worth watching for. Constrain enough people the same way and they begin to behave alike, because there is only one thing left to do. Then the sameness gets presented as the discovery: look at all these people, all doing the same thing. The accusation on the label varies — terrorist, addict, dangerous, whatever the roulette lands on that year — and the manufactured uniformity is offered as the evidence for it. They produce the pattern, then point at it as proof.

A person with options is harder to read, and options are not a mindset — they are material. Somewhere to cook. Money that does not come through anyone doing this to you. Enough of your week arranged among people who are not reporting on it.

That is not a defense against being watched. Nothing is. It is a way of being worth less to watch.

2026-08-21 · They Are Not a Secret Society

Two lines went into the Dictionary (https://chargingthefuture.github.io/chargingthefuture/article/chargingthefuture/Dictionary) today. Both are about what these people are, rather than what they do, and both are open for argument. That second part is the reason for this post.

"Secret society" is their own brag

You hear it from operatives directly, and you hear it repeated back inside our own community as though it explained something.

It is wrong by definition. A secret society keeps two things hidden: who belongs to it, and what it is for. These people do the opposite. They announce themselves to their targets constantly — the crowding, the repeated phrases, the run-ins, the things said just loudly enough. The signaling is not a slip in the operation. The signaling is the operation. A group that spends its day making sure you know it is there is not keeping a secret from you.

What they actually are is duller and worse than the brag: a control mechanism run by traffickers — sex, drugs, and human trafficking — with the harassment as its enforcement arm.

So why say it at all? Because "secret society" recruits. It tells someone being asked to take part that they are being let into something, rather than hired into something. It flatters the person doing it and it frightens the person it is done to, which is two jobs from one phrase. It is theater, and the audience for it is the people they need.

There is a cost to us in repeating it. A secret society is unreachable — nothing to do about it but marvel. A trafficking operation with an enforcement arm has an economy underneath it, and an economy is a thing you can decline to participate in.

The same job, done with technology

There is a version of the brag aimed at the technically minded, and it works better than the original.

They will steer you toward the question of how. What are they using to get into the phone. Which exploit, which frequency, which device. Survivors spend years on that question. Some spend money they do not have on equipment to answer it.

It is the same move as the secret society, wearing different clothes. It makes them look unreachably sophisticated, and it hands you a problem with no bottom — you can always not yet know the answer, which means you can always still be working on it rather than on anything that would change your situation.

The technology is not the problem. The people are. Whatever they are using is in their hands because somebody with access put it there, and if that particular thing vanished tomorrow they would use the next one. No device you can name ends this, because no device started it.

What is true is duller and more useful: a great many people have been given permission to hurt you and face no consequence for doing it. That is a problem about people and arrangements, and it gets answered where everything else here gets answered.

Collective amusement

The second entry names why so much of it happens at all: a group is watching, and the group is entertained.

The worked example is a scheme named yesterday, The Good Day, Bad Day. Days get scheduled — a particular weekday made reliably bad, the day after left alone, weekends a favorite. You learn the shape and start dreading a specific day before anything has happened on it. The anticipation does the work, which is cheaper for them than the day itself.

Then the grading. Operatives ask how your day was. Good day? Bad day? Bad weekend? And the verdict comes back to you in phrases no bystander could object to — "have a good day", "hope you had a good weekend" — delivered pointedly, right after a day they arranged to be bad.

Typically the day chosen is Friday. American culture treats Friday as the good one because the weekend follows it, and taking that particular day is the point. In my case it does not land — I am not on that clock, so their choice of the culturally good day is irrelevant to me — but the choice tells you what they think they are doing.

Any scheme can fill a bad day. What that tag names is the layer above them: the scheduling and the grading. And the reason for the grading is that a score is only worth keeping if somebody is watching it. The audience is the point.

Planned, and everything else

Worth separating, because they do not work the same way.

Most of what happens to us is not ordered. It is a distributed criminal network, operating with a standing permission: if someone wants to have a go at you, they may, and nothing happens to them for it. No instruction was issued. They do it because they enjoy it. That is the bulk of it, and it is why the harassment has no consistent shape — a thousand people acting on their own account, at your expense.

The Good Day, Bad Day is not that. It is planned in advance and coordinated. That is exactly what makes it worth a tag of its own: it is one of the few parts with a structure you can predict, and a structure is a thing that can be caught.

There is a third thing that sits between them, and it is the most childish part of the whole operation. They cannot control everything, because nobody can. Sometimes a bad day simply happens — bad days happen to people who are not targeted, too. When one does, they claim it. They take credit for weather, for a delay, for an ordinary piece of misfortune. It is comical and sick at once, and it is worth naming because a target who does not know they do this will hand them things they had nothing to do with.

The part that needs other people

One detail is why the tag exists at all: survivors who have never met report the same weekday structure independently. That is not something any one person can see from inside their own week.

There is a condition attached to it, though. The weekly shape needs a stretch in one place. It runs on operatives who are local to you and working at this full time — not full time on you, since they carry other targets too, but reliably near you — and that takes a settled address to arrange — which is what makes a reliably bad Tuesday possible at all.

Nobody travels every day of their life, so this is not two kinds of people. I travel often, and there are still stretches where I am in one place. What changes for someone who moves is not whether the schedule exists but which day it lands on: it re-forms around wherever you have settled, often on a different day than last time. So mine varies. It is the same scheme each time, wearing a different weekday.

Which means a week with no obvious bad day is not evidence the tag does not describe you. It may only mean you have not been in one place long enough for the schedule to set.

If it does match yours, log it. A note in a private file stays private and stops there. A tagged incident goes into the trend report, and unconnected people dreading the same weekday is a pattern rather than a coincidence.

Tell me if these are wrong

I would rather be corrected by survivors than be confidently wrong on a public reference page.

On "secret society": is that the word used at you? Something else? If you were told a name for it, I want to know what the name was.

On collective amusement: does "amusement" land right, or is it too light for what you have seen? I have gone back and forth on it. It sounds trivial next to what is done, and that may be exactly correct — the triviality of the motive is part of the offense — or it may understate the sadism and need a harder word.

On The Good Day, Bad Day: which weekday is yours, and does it change when you move? If you have lived in more than one place, I want both answers — whether the bad day followed you or reset. In my experience it resets. That is the part I cannot work out alone, and it would tell us how the schedule gets set in the first place.

One thing about that question, and I am not going to write my way around it.

They read what we write. If I read another survivor's post saying theirs is Wednesday, mine becomes Wednesday — it is less work for them than choosing, and it doubles as a message: I have access, and here is your bad day.

And I am not going to tell you there is a safe place to answer. They read the Commons. They read the chats I have with the tools I build this thing with. Anywhere I could ask, they can read. I would rather say that plainly than sell you a private channel that is not private.

What works instead is not secrecy. It is dependence, or rather the absence of it.

Being watched is not what gives them power over you. Needing something from them is. Every move in the catalog runs on a dependency: the account you rely on to be heard, the employer who can be leaned on, the landlord, the relative whose spare room is the only roof left. Remove the dependency and the watching buys them nothing. They can read every word of this and it gets them no leverage, because there is nothing here they are in a position to withhold from me.

That is the material half. I am not on social media apart from Quora. If I take myself off Quora and operate here, most of these games have nowhere to land — not because this place is hidden, but because nothing I need comes to me through them any more.

The other half is the same idea applied inwards. The grading only works on someone who is waiting to hear the verdict. Friday is chosen because the culture agrees Friday is the good one — but I am not on that clock, so ruining it does not do what they intend. I know the shape, so a bad day registers as a move being made rather than as something happening to me. I do not internalize them. A Wednesday copied out of somebody's post is still a bad Wednesday, but it is one you can see the wires on.

Depending on nobody who is being paid to harm you, and not handing them your interior either. That is closer to freedom than any privacy setting has ever gotten me.

So answer in the Commons (https://app.chargingthefuture.com), the group chat inside the app, knowing they are reading over your shoulder there as everywhere else. What the answers are for is not keeping a secret. It is a set of us knowing the shape well enough that it stops working. The Dictionary is a living page kept in a public repository, so every change to it is dated and on the record — including the ones that come from being told I got it wrong.

New schemes get named from the same kind of description. When you log an incident and no scheme on the list fits, there is a "not listed" option that asks you to write what happened, and that writing is the intake that names new ones. It is limited to members holding the Weavers of the Commons badge, which keeps junk out of the naming queue rather than out of logging — logging itself is open to every approved member.

Both of this week's additions started as plain descriptions of something the list had no word for. That is the whole mechanism. There is no committee.

2026-08-21 · Why They Want You Living With Family

There is a scheme on the schemes list (https://www.chargingthefuture.com/schemes) now that I have wanted named for a long time: The Forced Homecoming.

It has been run at me for more than a decade — not once, repeatedly. That is the part worth saying first, because a thing attempted on you every few years is not a run of bad luck, and the repetition is what finally made it recognizable to me as a designed sequence rather than a series of separate disasters.

It is built, not stumbled into

The destitution comes first, and it is not the objective. Income gets closed off, work gets interfered with until it ends, accounts get shut on claims that are not true. What that produces is a person with one door left open, and the door leads back to family.

They have a fixation on the past. Putting a grown adult back in a childhood bedroom is not incidental to what they enjoy; it is close to the centerpiece. A person in their forties or fifties, returned to a house they left decades ago, financially dependent on the people in it. That result is the thing being engineered, not a side effect of poverty.

Then the relatives get bought

Once you are in the house, the people in it become useful. Perks. Work that appears from nowhere. Social life, company, sex — things given to a relative in exchange for making your day worse. It does not have to be framed to them as recruitment and usually is not. It arrives as good fortune that happens to come with a suggestion attached.

Around the house, the neighborhood fills in. Properties get bought up. People doing this full time circle the block all day — not full time on you, since they carry other targets in the same city, but near enough and often enough that there is nowhere in your routine they are not.

Why this makes the scheduled part easy

The Good Day, Bad Day (https://www.chargingthefuture.com/schemes) needs two things: a settled address, so a weekday can be reliably ruined, and knowledge of your day, so the grading works.

In this arrangement you have supplied both. You are not going anywhere, and one of them lives with you. They know when you slept, what you ate, what you were dreading, and how the day went — not by surveillance but by being at the table. It is the cheapest version of the operation there is.

And why you will not be believed afterward

This is the payoff I had not understood until recently, and it is the one that does the lasting damage.

There is a problem on the list now that was missing before: being falsely labeled mentally ill or dangerous. Not a specific accusation about a specific incident — that is its own entry. This is the standing verdict about your character, the one that follows you into a doctor's office, a housing application, a family conversation, a courtroom. Several schemes exist to manufacture it. You are provoked until you react like anyone would, and the reaction is what gets written down.

The Forced Homecoming is the cheapest way to produce it. Look at what the arrangement leaves you with. You are isolated, in a house with the people harming you. You have no independent income, so you cannot leave and cannot be taken seriously as an account of your own situation. And there is no witness to any of it who is not being paid — in favors, in work, in company — to say the opposite of what you would say.

A person in that position has no route to being believed. Not because the account is weak, but because every source anyone would check has been bought.

Why I say do not do it

This is the arrangement I argue against harder than any other, and I want to be plain about what that is based on. It is my own observation from years of reading survivors and talking with them. It is not a study, and I am not going to present it as one.

The survivors I have watched reach the worst endings — taking their own lives, or killing someone in the house, or walking out and doing violence to strangers — have very often been living exactly like this when it happened.

I do not think that says anything about who those people were, and I want that understood clearly, because the accusation that we are dangerous is one already used against every one of us. I think it says what happens to a person when the harassment is inside the place they sleep, run in part by the people they depend on to eat, with no hour that is off and no door to close on it.

Everyone else at least gets to go home. In this arrangement, home is where it is worst. That is the whole of my argument, and it is why I treat it as urgent rather than as one bad option among several.

The honest problem with saying that

Telling a destitute person not to take the only housing available is worthless on its own. The reason the scheme works is that the alternative usually does not exist.

That is the gap LightHouse (https://app.chargingthefuture.com/apps/lighthouse) is for. It lists places offered by hosts who are survivors themselves — people who are not being paid in favors to make your week difficult, because they are on the same list you are. A room with a door that locks, in a building where nobody has been given anything to arrange your day.

I am not going to claim there is a listing waiting for everyone who needs one. There is not. What I will say is that the answer to this particular scheme is housing that does not run through people who can be bought, and that building the supply of it is worth more than any amount of advice about coping where you are.

If you are in this arrangement now, the Commons (https://app.chargingthefuture.com) is where people are. Not a hotline — I called one once, and what I got back was effectively an invitation to join the club. People who already understand the situation are a different proposition.

2026-08-20 · The Record Says I Called. I Was on the Bus.

I was on the bus. Somewhere between St. Louis and New York, on time, no trouble on board, I stepped off at a scheduled stop to use the restroom and was refused permission to get back on. My ticket had been canceled while I was sitting on the bus it was for.

In its place was an itinerary I never asked for. The single leg to New York had become four: St. Louis to Pittsburgh to Washington DC to Philadelphia to New York, with layovers of three hours, five hours fifty, and four hours fifty. It arrived at 07:50 on August 14 — about sixteen hours later than the schedule I was already riding, twenty-one hours later than the trip I originally booked — at a different New York terminal, by a route that runs south to Washington and back up again.

The carrier's record says I called and requested it. I did not. The email address on the booking had been changed, so the new itinerary never reached me.

The whole trip, in order

Booked: Salt Lake City to Denver to St. Louis to New York Port Authority. About two and a half days door to door.

August 11, leg one. The driver made an unplanned rest stop and took far longer than it needed. The Denver connection was thirty minutes; I missed it. The next bus was five hours later. I called, the ticket was rebooked across the same three legs at later times, and the arrival moved to 16:00 on August 13.

Denver. Two people who would go on to cause the trouble in St. Louis were already on this leg with me. There is video of them harassing me at Denver, before any of the rest of it happened.

The two from Denver are not just a detail about who was where. That is a scheme in its own right, and it now has a name: The Staged Run-In. Delays and trip changes put a target in the path of people they would otherwise never cross — staged encounters, some friendly, some hostile — and once that has happened, those people can claim they know you. A target does not personally know a single one of them beyond the friends, family, colleagues and acquaintances the Specterati recruit. That circle is small. The number of people taking part is not. A staged encounter is how a stranger acquires standing to say anything about you at all, and the pair had been near me since Denver — which is what let one of them report an assault in St. Louis and the other confirm it as a witness.

St. Louis, stepping off the bus. Two officers in utility-style clothing that did not look like a uniform stopped me and said the locks on my luggage were a sign of drug trafficking. I declined the dog sniff and declined the search, and instead opened my own bags and unpacked them myself, in public, in front of everyone. Nothing was found, because there was nothing to find.

Since the locks were the pretext, they are worth explaining. I carry two kinds and neither choice is casual. 2026-08-19 · The Trends Report Now Says Where

Tagging in ClickLog is about a week old. In that week ClickLog became the most-used part of the app. The trend report it feeds has been rebuilt, and this is what changed and why.

Why log anything at all

The honest reason, not a slogan.

A note in a private file helps the person who wrote it and nobody else. It can steady you. It can remind you that a thing happened on a day you were later told did not happen. What it cannot do is travel. A hundred notes in a hundred private files are still a hundred separate notes — no shape, nothing anyone can look at.

A tag is different. When you mark an incident with which known problem happened and which named scheme was used, you have turned it into something that can be counted. Counted things can be shown to someone. That is the entire argument, and it stops there: nobody controls what happens after something is shown. What is in your hands is whether the thing exists to be looked at.

The report used to hide where

It counted how many separate areas had activity, then stopped. It could tell you activity had a location without ever telling you where — a strange thing for a map to do.

Now each area appears: the area itself (about 11 km across), how many incidents sit in it, how many different people are behind them, and the range of dates it covers. It also prints how many shared incidents carry no location at all, so a gap shows up as a gap rather than quietly shrinking the map.

Counting people, not entries

One person logging seven times and seven people logging once produce the same number on the old report. They are not the same thing. One is a person having a terrible week; the other is a pattern across a community.

The report now shows how many different members are reporting, and how many of them logged more than once. When you read a count, that pair is the first thing to check.

Fifty-three problems, six kinds

The problem list runs to fifty-three entries. Fifty-three of anything is a list, not a pattern — nobody holds it in their head.

They are now grouped into six kinds of harm: watched and followed; body and health; threats and intimidation; blocked from work, money, and services; set up to be blamed; cut off from people. Six is a shape a person recognizes in their own life. An incident that carries three problems from one kind counts once for that kind, not three times, so a heavily tagged incident cannot inflate its own category.

Schemes are not all the same shape

Some schemes are an operation with a start and an end. Some run continuously in the background. Some are a shape that only appears when you look across months.

Ranking those by raw count compares a thing that happens most days against a thing that happens twice a year, and the ranking lies. Each scheme now carries a label saying which kind it is, so a reader can tell whether a low number means rare or means slow. The report also shows which scheme was tagged alongside which problem on the same incident, which is where the useful detail usually sits.

The report explains itself

Where the numbers come from, what is never counted, how to read a count, and what the data cannot show are printed with the numbers — not in a separate document nobody opens.

This is the one part written with a second reader in mind: someone outside this project who might one day look into any of it. A figure with no method attached is worth nothing to that person, and worse than nothing if it overstates. Printing the limits next to the count is what makes the count usable.

The whole report also saves as a single tall picture, one button, made to be posted. Area coordinates are left out of that image unless they are deliberately included.

What is never shared

Your notes are not shown to anyone else here, and they are not in the trend report. They are left out of every report query rather than filtered out afterward.

Correction, 2026-08-24: this originally said notes are never seen by anyone, including this project. That was wrong; the accurate line is above.

The exact location is never shared; only the area of about 11 km. Who logged what is never shared: a report can say how many different people are behind a set of incidents, never which people. Sharing is off unless you turn it on, with a setting and a per-incident choice. Tagging requires sharing, because a tag exists to feed the totals — the app says so before you save. Deleting an incident removes it from every future report.

The share controls used to say the notes stay private and only the date, rough area, and tags are shared. They did not say what happens next. They now say the grouped totals may be published, at the moment you make the choice rather than in terms you read once.

What this does not prove

The sample is small. Everything in it is self-reported and unverified. A handful of reports from a handful of people shows what those people say happened, and nothing more. Any claim beyond that would be used against every member who logged something.

Two things would change that, and neither exists yet. The report cannot say what the member did about an incident and what came of it, so nothing in it shows whether anything helped. And nothing in it is corroborated — there is no second source attached to any entry.

Naming those gaps is what makes the rest of it worth reading.

Two things about you: whether you consider yourself a Targeted Individual, and whether at least one of your accounts was removed. Then one card for each closed account.

2026-08-17 · How the math works

Someone asked me "How?" in a DM this week. I had posted that with 97 people, the network can already point at an economy of $13.8 million a year. They wanted the math.

The answer I sent fits in a few paragraphs, so here it is, cleaned up. Every number in it is public. You do not have to believe me about any of it — you can check each one yourself.

Where 5 million comes from

The United Nations' own reports count about 2 million people describing this. That is only the people who reported. This crime is underreported, and I am the proof: I have never reported mine to anyone, so I am not in that 2 million. The standard assumption for crimes like this is that about half go unreported, so I added 2 million for the people like me.

Then 1 million more for people who do not yet know it is happening to them. I noticed things at age five. I would not have called myself targeted then. It was not until I was nine that I knew for sure, and it got progressively worse from there. There are people today in the years between noticing and knowing.

That makes about 5 million. It is a guess, stated as a guess, and its job is to be replaced: as people join and list what they can do, a real number comes out of the app's data and this one gets thrown out.

Then I found the countries that size

I searched for countries with a population around 5 million that run service-based economies — where most of the work is services, and most of those services do not have to happen in person.

Finland fits. So I cataloged every job and title a working economy that size needs — about 650 different things — and placed the whole list in the app.

Then, every day, I comb Quora for survivors who state their skills, and I map each one against that list. The app labels every profile I create as community-generated and names me as the one who made it. Anyone can claim theirs or have it removed. Nobody has asked to be removed.

Where we are right now

97 people. They cover 158 of the 650 things on the list — 24 percent.

Now the dollar figure. Singapore publishes what one worker in a pure skills economy adds per year: take its total output, divide by its workers, and you land near $150,000. The app runs this calculation live with its calibrated rate of $142,500 per person, which puts our 97 people at $13.8 million a year.

That figure is what our listed skills are worth at the output rate of the best skills economies on earth. A group small enough to fit in one room already holds that much capacity — listed, mapped, and findable. Every day of profile work, every skill added, every request posted moves it.

Why Singapore and Estonia

Both were built on skills and nothing else. Singapore at independence had no oil, no farmland, not enough fresh water — its stated strategy was to develop its people, because people were the only asset available. Estonia came out of occupation in 1991 with 1.3 million people, no money, and infrastructure that did not work, and built a functioning modern state largely in software because software was what it could afford.

Both took about two generations.

To say it plainly, because these examples invite a misreading: I am not forming a nation state. No territory, no citizenship, no government, no intention of any. What is borrowed from those countries is arithmetic — proof of what a population holding nothing but its own skills can produce, and how long it takes.

The part that works in our favor

Specterati operators go after skilled and influential people. That is who gets targeted.

Which means the population I am mapping is unusually skilled. I have reviewed the profiles myself, one by one, and the data shows it. The thing that made each of us a target is the same thing that makes this economy possible.

The number that is actually the goal

5 million is the far number. My personal, realistic goal is 384 people working with each other at any given time.

That is enough for the people who no longer want to live, work, or be around Specterati operators. Not everyone will join. Not everyone needs everything the app offers. But 384 people working full time inside our own economy is enough to run one.

384, not 5 million. That is a number a person can watch arrive.

2026-08-16 · What stays up

Quora deleted every post ever made to my space Skills Economy (https://skillseconomy.quora.com) today, August 16, 2026, as well as banned my personal account: Farah Brunache (https://www.quora.com/profile/Farah-Brunache).

The account is still there. The space is still there. Both are empty. No post history. No traction. Nothing to scroll.

Here is what was in it. Invitations to people to join a skills network. Posts about members trading work, housing, and rides with each other. Arguments against isolating. Arguments against giving up. A standing position that the answer is to live and work together and to stop handing the Specterati your attention.

Here is what I did not do. I did not follow other spaces. I did not post into feeds I had no connection with. Everything went from my own account into my own space, read by people who chose to be there. For a stretch I posted into a void on purpose, so that nothing I was doing could be read as reaching where it was not wanted.

It all went anyway.

An empty room is the more effective version

They did not stop me from posting. I can post again tomorrow.

What they removed is the record, so the space now reads as a place where nothing has ever happened.

Think about what that does to somebody arriving for the first time. They do not see a person who was silenced. They see an empty room. An empty room means nobody is here, so they leave — and they are right to leave, because that is what an empty room means everywhere else in life.

A ban would have worked less well. A ban leaves a mark. People notice a ban and ask what was said in it. An empty space raises no question at all. It just looks like something nobody showed up for.

Now go look at what is still up

There are accounts posting the same text and the same images over and over. Not disguised. Not spaced out. The same block of words, reposted, as far back as you care to scroll.

By any spam policy anyone has ever written, that is spam. It is still there.

What those posts say is that the world is horrible and about to destroy itself. That there is nothing to be done. That it is the government. That it is spiritual warfare. Give up, in one wording or another.

I want to be careful here, because some people saying those things mean them and are having a terrible time. This is not about them. It is about which content gets to stay.

Sort it and the rule is not subtle. Content that leaves a person alone with it stays up. Content that puts people in contact with each other does not.

Spiritual warfare leaves you praying by yourself. Nothing to be done leaves you sitting still. It is the government points you at something with no address, no phone number, and no door. All of it is safe to leave up, because all of it ends the same way: one person, alone, doing nothing.

Live and work together is a different kind of sentence. It ends with people in the same room.

The spam explanation does not survive contact with the spam

There is an ordinary explanation for what happened to me and I am not going to pretend otherwise. I posted invitations over and over for months, each one carrying a link to the same outside site. Repetition plus an outside link is what automated filters are built to catch.

That explanation has a problem it cannot get past. If a filter were catching repetition, it would have caught the accounts posting identical text and identical images on a loop, in the open, for months. Those are untouched. Mine is gone.

So either a filter is not what happened to me, or the filter runs in one direction. I cannot tell you which from where I sit and I am not going to claim I can.

There is even a version where nobody intended any of it. A system tuned to spot organized outreach looks for repetition, outside links, and named recipients. That is exactly the shape of an invitation. A wall of identical text saying the world is ending carries no link and asks nothing of anybody, so it trips nothing at all.

If that is what happened, it is worse rather than better. A person can be found and stopped. A filter that quietly clears out the connecting content and leaves the isolating content just keeps running, and there is nobody to catch at it.

Either way the result is the same and you can check it yourself in five minutes. Open spam telling people to give up is still there. A space telling people to work together is empty.

What I actually take from it

Not that Quora is against me. That a platform is a lever.

Everything I have written says the same thing about employers, landlords, and anybody else your life runs through. If a person or a company can be reached, they are a lever, and the fix is not to appeal to them. The fix is to need them less.

This is exactly why the app exists and why none of it sits on top of a platform. Accounts get deleted. That is a known thing that happens to people in this situation, which is why the app is the fallback and not the other way around. Your profile, your skills, and the people who can reach you do not live on somebody else’s shelf.

Nothing about the network changed today. The profiles are still there. The skills are still listed. The requests are still open, and there are still more people asking than answering.

If you are reading this, you found it some other way. That is the entire point.
