you-cannot-argue-a-smear-down

You cannot argue a smear down.

A question I opened as a Quora answer draft on August 6, 2025, and never wrote:

How do I overcome a gangstalker's false narrative? How can I distract myself from their tactics?

Quora's export kept the words of the question and not the name of the person who asked it; the name is here because the address still resolves to them. They are also in the Directory — a community-generated profile, built from what they say about themselves in public: a pharmaceutical assistant at a compounding pharmacy, studying medicine administration. It is labeled as community-generated and names who made it, it is theirs to claim whenever they want it, and theirs to have removed by asking.

Screenshot: The question on Quora, captured August 28, 2026. Topic tags above it read Coping Mechanisms, Harassment, Gangstalkers, and seven more. The question reads: "How do I overcome a gangstalker's false narrative? How can I distract myself from their tactics?" Below it the page shows Follow with a count of 3, Request, Answer, and More, and a row reading All related (35).

The vocabulary I use for the people running this is in the Dictionary; the question keeps the words it was asked in.

Two halves, and they are the same question. The second one decides the first.

What a false narrative is for.

A smear is not an argument. It is not offered so it can be examined — it is offered so it can be repeated.

It is built to be unfalsifiable. Vague enough that nothing you produce settles it, specific enough to embarrass, and always attached to somebody who will not say who told them. So the reply that would work against an ordinary claim does nothing. Worse, replying has costs: it says the thing again in your own voice, it puts you in the frame as the person the accusation is about, and it takes the day.

Those costs are not a side effect. They are the purchase. The rebuttal is what the narrative is being spent on.

The staged kind.

Mine was often built in the street rather than said behind my back. For years Specterati operators started physical fights with me in public. One threatened to hit me with a glass bottle. Another threw a full soda can at the back of my head. Around the same time they kept asking me to call the police, which is part of the same game.

The fight is not the point of the fight. The picture is. New recruits get shown a person scuffling in the street, and that picture is the narrative — manufactured in front of witnesses who were brought there to see it, so that it arrives as something they saw with their own eyes rather than something they were told.

Not supplying that is not composure and it is not turning the other cheek. It is declining to make the footage.

What actually beats one.

Nothing you say. Something anyone can check without taking your word.

A dated record you own. ClickLog is an incident log: date, and a location if you choose one. It takes seconds and it exists whether or not anyone ever asks you for it. The value is not that it convinces a skeptic — it is that your account of your own life stops depending on your memory under stress, and it becomes something with timestamps that were not applied afterward.

Work other people can see. A Directory profile is a public listing of what you can do. It is not a rebuttal and it never mentions the accusation. It just sits there being a specific, checkable fact about you that the narrative has no answer for.

People who know you because you did something for them. This is the strongest of the three and the only one that is not a claim at all. Someone who got a ride from you, or a repair, or a place to stay, is not evaluating testimony about you. They already know. Trust is the app's version of that: standing built out of participation that actually happened, rather than out of what anyone says about you.

And one structural thing, which is why this app is built the way it is: there is no score here. No credit score, no social score, no rating, nothing about who has complained about you feeding into what you are allowed to do. Slander is exactly what scoring systems eat — you get denied and nobody tells you which file said what. An economy that scored you would import the smear and call it risk management. This one does not score, and that is a decision, not an oversight.

Everything above is checkable without trusting me either. How to check me is the whole method.

The second half.

Now the harder one: how to distract yourself from the tactics.

The word is the problem, and I do not say that to correct whoever asked it — I asked it the same way. Distraction concedes the center. It grants that the operation holds the day and asks what to do with what is left over. Something that has to be distracted from is still the subject.

Two things are true at once. You cannot decide to stop noticing — noticing is a survival function, it is often accurate, and switching it off is neither possible nor safe. But noticing does not have to be carried. That is what the log is for: put it down somewhere dated, in fifteen seconds, and stop holding it all day.

Then the rest of the day goes on something that compounds. Not a hobby to occupy you — something that is still there next month regardless of what the Specterati operators did this week.

I spent nine months getting this wrong. Almost everything I wrote in my first nine months studied the people running the operation: what their texts install, how they are recruited, whether they enjoy it, who is above them. I consider myself an independent thinker and I fell for it anyway, because it is not an intelligence test. An operation this theatrical is built to be studied. It rewards attention with fresh puzzles, and every hour spent mapping one is an hour not spent on you. The turn is dated on the public record, and the nine months are there so the next person can skip them.

The same shape shows up in the question of who put you on the list. It is built so you never finish it, and you do not have to settle it before your life can start moving.

What none of this promises.

The narrative does not disappear. People who already believe it mostly go on believing it, and some of them were never going to be reachable by evidence — believing it is what they are there for.

What changes is smaller and more useful. It stops being the only checkable thing about you. It stops setting your schedule. And the people who matter — the ones who will actually turn up with a ride or a room — are not deciding based on it, because they are looking at what you did.

The targeting is real. It is not the most interesting fact about you.

present-the-math

If you say it cannot be done, present the math.

There is a thin line between offering an opinion and manipulating somebody. It is thinner than people think, and the thing that decides which side you are on is whether there is anything underneath what you said.

An unsupported claim, stated with confidence, to people who are looking for direction, does the same work as a lie. It does not matter whether the person meant it as one.

The claim.

An economy run by Targeted Individuals (TIs), with no reliance on the Specterati, cannot be built.

I have read that on Quora many times. I have never once seen anybody show it.

Mine first.

The first simulation replays something that already happened, and the figures in it are not mine.

W. E. B. Du Bois, "The Talented Tenth", in The Negro Problem, 1903. He is describing what the college founders had already built after the war:

they founded colleges, and up from the colleges shot normal schools, and out from the normal schools went teachers, and around the normal teachers clustered other teachers to teach the public schools; the college trained in Greek and Latin and mathematics, 2,000 men; and these men trained full 50,000 others in morals and manners, and they in turn taught thrift and the alphabet to nine millions of men, who to-day hold $300,000,000 of property.

2,000 to 50,000 is 25 times over. 50,000 to 9,000,000 is 180 times over. In the United States alone.

The simulation gives you those two rates and one decision each round: how much goes to teaching, how much goes to the work itself. Put it all into the work and you reach 360,000 people. Teach first and you reach the 9,000,000 he recorded. Same effort, same number of rounds.

The people Du Bois was writing about were trafficked in public. It was overt, and it was openly not treated as a human rights violation. A modern TI is trafficked just as overtly, and it is just as unrecognized. The two situations are not the same and I am not saying they are — the routes differ, the spectrums differ. What matches is the operating condition: it happens in the open and no institution names it. That is the condition those 2,000 people built under. They did not wait to be believed first.

Outnumbered at least three hundred to one.

In a town I had just arrived in, I counted the people involved on foot within 24 hours. I stopped at 300.

Not 300 over years. Not 300 who know me. Three hundred inside a single day, in a place I had never been, already there when I got there. That is not a hometown holding a grudge. Coverage like that is administered, and it is not administered locally.

300 is where I stopped counting. It is not where it ends.

None of that changes a number above. The rates describe what teaching does; they say nothing about how many people are working against you. Compounding is not a contest — 25 times over is 25 times over whether one person opposes it or a thousand do.

Thirty years is the conservative reading of that, which is why I hold a two-generation goal rather than a shorter one.

Where this actually stands.

The obvious answer is that I do not have 2,000 people. That is correct, and the number is not a secret.

The simulation has a second preset for it. On August 28 it read 101 people recruited, covering 160 of 657 skills. Those figures come off the Workforce screen, which is where the skills map lives — open it and count.

101 against 2,000 is about a twentieth. It changes where the arithmetic starts and not what the arithmetic does, because a rate is a rate regardless of the number in front of it. That preset is in the simulation for the same reason the source is open: the weakest version of my own case should be the one you can see.

Now yours.

If you say it cannot be done, present the math.

Not an opinion about whether people will participate. Not a prediction about human nature. Not a story about somebody who let you down. The numbers: a starting count, a rate at which capability passes from one person to the next, and the arithmetic that lands lower than the figures above.

Both simulations are free, run offline once opened, need no account, and send nothing anywhere.

The source is open. The first one and the second are both readable in full, and Du Bois's book is committed beside them. The rates are not something you have to take from me. They are two numbers in a file, and every figure I have quoted comes out of them. Check the arithmetic before you argue with the conclusion, and if the arithmetic is wrong, say where.

Change the inputs and show me what you get.

What the question sorts.

Some of the accounts repeating it are there to repeat it. Spreading the belief that nothing can be built is a cheap way to keep people apart, and it costs nothing to post.

I cannot prove that about any particular account and I am not going to try. The question does that work instead. Somebody who is there to spread it has nothing to bring, because there was never any arithmetic behind it — that was the point of saying it.

And if you are not one of them, and you believe it, and you go looking for what your version rests on and find nothing there, that is worth knowing.

I am not shaming anybody for being isolated.

Isolation is a reasonable response to what has been done to people. Pulling back after being set up, followed, lied about and cut out of work is not a defect, and I am not asking anybody to feel bad about it.

What does not hold together is complaining about having nothing while arguing against the thing that would produce something. Those are two positions and they cancel each other.

Here is how somebody holds both without noticing. Pain that comes from outside gets weighted heavily. Pain we cause ourselves gets discounted — the same act, the same cost, and it registers as smaller because we were the one who did it. We forgive ourselves faster than we forgive anybody else, and much faster than we forgive somebody who wronged us.

For a Targeted Individual that gap is wider, and it is wider on purpose. The apparatus is built to hold your attention on them. Every incident is an invitation to spend another day accounting for what they did.

So the outrage points outward at full volume, and the cost of staying apart barely registers, because that cost is self-inflicted and self-inflicted is the kind we discount.

Constantly obsessing over them is the reverse of freedom. It is the outcome they are working toward, reached without them having to do anything further.

The targeting is done to you. That was never a choice and it is not what I am talking about. But "it cannot be done" is a position, and a position can be checked. If it turns out there were never any numbers under yours, then what is keeping you apart from other survivors at that point is the position, not them.

The second simulation.

The second one is about the part Du Bois did not have to solve.

His people were denied education. The deficit was skills, and the answer was to build them. Skills Economy starts from the opposite end: the people here are already highly skilled, and what is missing is discovery. Somebody with twenty years in a trade is not short of the trade. They are short of anybody knowing they have it.

So it is a map, and what spreads across it is isolation — a place where somebody needs something and nobody findable can do it. Four people with skills move against it. Clearing a place puts it in the Directory, and it stays covered from then on.

The Directory is a running list of Targeted Individuals alongside what they can do, one person at a time. It is the thing survivors ask for most. Being on it costs nobody an account: most entries were built for people rather than by them, and anybody can claim theirs or have it taken down. Seeing it does take a sign-in. It was public in version 2, and everything in it came from public sources — but gathered and sorted this way it becomes something else, so version 3 put it behind a login.

two-games-in-the-arcade

Two games in the arcade, and what each one is for.

Two games are live. They are free, they run offline once opened, there is no account, no ads, and nothing about how you play leaves your phone.

Peace-Battle · Peace-Battle 2 · the arcade.

Peace-Battle — two rates from 1903.

W. E. B. Du Bois, "The Talented Tenth", in The Negro Problem, 1903.

He is not proposing a plan. He is describing what the college founders had already done after the war, and he is emphatic about where they started, against the people telling him they should have started lower:

Where ought they to have begun to build? At the bottom, of course, quibbles the mole with his eyes in the earth. Aye! truly at the bottom, at the very bottom; at the bottom of knowledge, down in the very depths of knowledge there where the roots of justice strike into the lowest soil of Truth. And so they did begin; they founded colleges, and up from the colleges shot normal schools, and out from the normal schools went teachers, and around the normal teachers clustered other teachers to teach the public schools; the college trained in Greek and Latin and mathematics, 2,000 men; and these men trained full 50,000 others in morals and manners, and they in turn taught thrift and the alphabet to nine millions of men, who to-day hold $300,000,000 of property.

25 times over, then 180 times over. Those two rates are the only numbers the game runs on.

He called it "the most wonderful peace-battle of the 19th century." That is where the game's name comes from.

Why this sits next to Skills Economy.

The starting points are opposite, and the difference is the part worth being precise about.

Du Bois was writing about a population denied education outright. The deficit was skills, and the answer was to build them — colleges first, then teachers out of the colleges, then teachers around those.

Skills Economy starts from the other end. The people here are already highly skilled. What is missing is discovery. Somebody with twenty years in a trade is not short of the trade; they are short of anybody knowing they have it. That is a different problem and it needs a different answer.

The answer is a list. The Directory is a running list of Targeted Individuals alongside what they can do, one person at a time. It is the thing survivors ask for most. Being on it costs nobody an account: most entries were built for people rather than by them, and anybody can claim theirs or have it taken down. Seeing it takes a sign-in, because everything in it came from public sources but gathered and sorted this way it becomes something else.

What the two share is what produced the deficit and what it cost. Both populations face outsized oppression, and in both the result was destitution — reached by different routes, on different spectrums, destitution either way.

And the remedy has the same shape: a connected network of trading. In his it was the colleges, then the teachers those colleges trained, then the nine million those teachers reached — holding $300 million of property by the time he wrote it down. In this one it is people being findable and exchanging services directly with each other.

That is also why there are two games rather than one. The first is his problem — building skills where they were denied. The second is this one — finding the people who already have them.

We have the education. The children will not.

What he described ran from the founding of those colleges after the war to his writing it down in 1903 — about one generation. This could too, which is what the arithmetic actually says but I hold a two-generation goal rather than a shorter one.

The math is not what stops it. Depression is. Being targeted causes it, and it makes a number like 25 times over impossible to look at. Freedom is closer than people think.

Adults being targeted now have the education. Children being targeted now will not. That is what this does to a childhood, and denied education is where Du Bois started — a harder place than where we are.

So we hand it down now.

Playing Peace-Battle.

You decide each round how much goes to teaching and how much to the work itself. Put everything into the work and you reach 360,000 people. Teach first and you reach 9,000,000 — same effort, same rounds, 25 times the reach. That second split is the one Du Bois recorded, and the game says so when you land on it.

They are his figures, written in 1903 to make an argument rather than to pass an audit. He was not describing a hypothetical; he was describing what a small number of trained people had already done.

There is a preset for where Skills Economy actually stands. As of August 28 it reads 101 people recruited, covering 160 of 657 skills. The rates do not change — only the number it starts from, 101 against his 2,000.

Nobody argues with the arithmetic once they have done it themselves.

Peace-Battle 2 — a map, and nobody wins alone.

The second one is a knock-off of Pandemic, a board game from 2008, with the polarity turned around.

In Pandemic a disease spreads across a map of cities and you race to contain it. Here what spreads is isolation — a place where somebody needs something and nobody findable can do it. Four people with skills move against it. People are never the contagion. Being cut off is.

Sixteen real cities, sized by population. Four actions a round: move along a link, or help where you stand. Helping where the need matches your skill does twice as much, and clearing a place puts it in the Directory — the real one, borrowed for the board — covered from then on, its people counted, isolation never returning to it. Then it grows in two more places, and one already at its limit cuts off and pushes into every place beside it. Six cut off and it is over.

Cover the whole map and every place on it is reachable. It is cooperative. There is no winner, and nobody gets there by beating anyone.

That is the difference between the two games. The first shows what a decision about where to put effort is worth. The second makes you produce the outcome where everybody is reachable, and it needs the same thing the real version needs: somebody has to be findable before they can be asked.

Seats are filling. Enrolling holds a deposit of 50 credits, and you get every one of them back — they are returned to you as your trainer signs off each milestone, and if you leave the cohort the whole remaining hold comes back at once. So the course costs nothing in the end. What it asks for is 50 credits you can put down while you are in it.

I did not pick those three. SkillUp generates its own cohort recommendations from the distance between what the skills map holds and what people are looking for, and those came back as the widest gaps. People are enrolling against them now, and nobody is scheduled to teach any of them.

Screenshot: The Trainers tab in SkillUp, headed Trainers — survivor-advocates who lead the cohorts. The list is empty. An icon of two people sits in the middle of the screen above the words No trainers listed yet, and below that: Trainers are survivor-advocates who lead cohorts. New trainers appear here as they join.

So this is a Request For Skills, trainer edition.

What running one involves.

You set the schedule and the cadence. Not me, and not the app.

Remote by default, because the cohorts are open globally — somebody who enrolls is as likely to be five time zones away as five miles. Run it inside the app or on whatever video tool you already use. The app does not care which one, and I am not going to recommend one.

In person is acceptable on one condition: it stays open to anybody who enrolled and cannot be there in person. A call running alongside it, a recording, a second sitting — whatever it takes. A cohort does not become a local one because the trainer is local.

You do not need to be a professional instructor. You need to know the work well enough that somebody who does not can follow you.

Two things the app checks rather than anybody approving you. Your own Directory listing has to carry a skill belonging to the occupation the cohort trains, and the listing has to be one you have claimed. That is the entire gate — a match between what you say you can do and what the cohort teaches, with nobody sitting in judgment of it.

Training earns credits. A trainer receives 10 credits per learner each time they sign off a milestone, up to double that where the skills map shows the widest gap. Those credits are minted for the work. They are not taken out of what the learner put down, which comes back to the learner.

Or nominate a trainer you know.

You do not have to be the one teaching. If you know somebody who could run one of these, nominate them in SkillsHunt — a name, where they are, what they can do. If the nomination is accepted, you receive credits for it.

They do not have to be a survivor, and they do not have to have an account. Most of the skills map was built by people naming somebody else.

If the software is what stops you.

If you would run a cohort but the tooling would not carry what you want to do, say so in the Commons group chat and I will build it.

Describe what would let you train more people, or train them better, and it gets built.

nobody-calls-to-say-its-dead

Nobody Calls to Tell You Your Project Is Dead.

On Thursday, September 10, I had a conversation with somebody on the app who I had already concluded was a Specterati operator. I had been keeping our contact short for a while. They kept asking for phone calls that turned out to be about nothing, and after enough of those I stopped treating the requests as ordinary.

They asked for another call. On it, they told me the app is dead. Nobody is on it. They are getting no value from it.

Then, in the same conversation, they asked me to ask them to stay.

Then they asked for advanced permissions, so they could help bring more people in. They have not brought one.

Three tells in one call.

Take the first on its own. A person who opens an app, finds nothing they want, and gets nothing out of it does not schedule a call to say so. They stop opening it. Nobody in the history of software has picked up the phone to inform a developer that their project has no users. That sentence is not a review. It is something you say to a person you want to stop.

The second one is stranger than the first and gives it away. If the place is empty and worthless, there is nothing to be asked to stay for. Wanting to be invited to remain somewhere you have just called dead is not a position anybody holds. It is a move, and the move needs the first sentence to set it up.

The third says what the first two were for. Admin access, to go and get users — from somebody whose contribution to date is zero invitations. The sequence is the app is failing, I am still here, let me in deeper so I can fix it. Each part exists to make the next one sound reasonable.

I want to be exact about what I am claiming. I am not saying anybody who criticizes the app is an operator, and I am not saying this because the criticism stung. Criticism is useful and I get plenty of it. What I am describing is a shape: an unprompted declaration that the project is finished, delivered by voice, to the one person whose continuing is the thing at stake, followed immediately by two asks that only make sense if the declaration is believed.

There are no advanced permissions to give.

What's Your 1%?

There is a screen in the app called Workforce. It models what a population of five million people looks like as an economy: how many workers, which sectors, which jobs, how much output. It is where the $142,500-per-person figure comes from, and I explained all of it in How the math works.

It has one problem. It is entirely about everybody, and it never once speaks to the person reading it.

So people read the whole thing, follow every number, agree it adds up, and close it thinking: fine, but none of that is about me.

Today there is a new tab at the end of that screen. It is called What's your 1%?

What it does.

It runs the same math backwards.

Workforce works forwards. Take a thriving economy's output, divide it evenly across everybody in it, and each person contributes about $142,500 a year. That number comes from Singapore, which publishes it, and it is the only place in the app where anything is stated in US dollars.

An even split is an average, and an average flattens exactly the thing worth seeing. Most people do not work full time. They have children, or illness, or a second thing they are doing, or they simply do not want to. The average has all of them in it. That is what makes it an average and not a ceiling.

So the new tab starts from the other end. One percent of five million is 50,000 people. The question is not what the average person contributes. It is: if you reached 50,000 people, what would that be?

What one person pays you, once, in a year, Across 50,000 people, Compared to the average.
$5, $250,000, 3.5×.
$20, $1,000,000, 14×.
$50, $2,500,000, 35×.
$100, $5,000,000, 70×.
$250, $12,500,000, 175×.

You do not have to believe a forecast. Find the row closest to what you already charge one person, once, and read across.

Five dollars. Not five dollars an hour, five dollars a year, from one person, once. That is a quarter of a million dollars. Three and a half times the average contributor in a country people hold up as a model economy. One percent puts an ordinary trade at the level the wider economy reserves for its top performers.

Why it is not the same screen for everybody.

That table is a reference. As an answer it would be dishonest. Trades are not interchangeable. A screen that hands a plumber and a physiotherapist the same number tells neither of them anything.

So two things weight it to you.

The first the app works out on its own. It already models how many people doing your job a population of five million needs. That is what the Workforce screen has always been for. Turn that around and you get how many people one of you serves when your trade is staffed normally. For some trades that is a few hundred people. For others it is tens of thousands. Then it tells you how far 1% is beyond that: forty times your trade's normal load, or five times, depending on what you do. That gap is not hidden, because the gap is what the five routes are for.

I want to be exact about the limit on that, because it is real. The skills map carries a weight for each sector and not yet for each job title inside one. So it separates trades in different sectors properly, and two jobs inside the same sector currently land on the same figure. The screen says so where it shows the number. It will sharpen when the map carries a weight per job rather than per sector.

The second weight the app cannot work out, so it asks. How often does one person need you in a year? A hairdresser and a roofer do not have the same answer and no table in my app knows either of them. You type what you charge for one job and how often one person needs you, and it computes your own row. Less than once a year is fine — once every two years, once a decade, whatever your trade actually is.

Nothing you type is saved. It is not sent anywhere, it is not stored, and it is gone when you reload. It is a calculator, not a form.

I am not forecasting anybody's income and the screen says so plainly. These are speculative figures on a stated share of a population estimate. What actually happens takes talent and work and luck in some combination, and no screen hands you those.

That is true, and it is the reason 1% sounds absurd the moment you hear it. A plumber can only be in one house. A hairdresser can only cut one head of hair. A mechanic has two hands.

It is a ceiling on one route. It is not a ceiling on the skill.

So the tab lists five of them, and each one links to the part of the app that carries it.

Do the work itself, in person or by traveling to it. One person at a time, and limited by where you can physically be. That is Foundation.

What a feed is.

A feed is a plain list of what has been published here — each post's title, its date, a few sentences saying what it is, and its address. It is written for software to read rather than for a person.

You point an app at it once. From then on the app checks the list for you and shows you what is new. You do not have to remember to come back, and nothing decides what you see first: posts arrive in the order they were written.

This is old technology and it is still the plainest arrangement anybody has come up with. It is also how every podcast in the world works. If you have ever subscribed to a podcast, you have used a feed and did not have to know it.

Why it is here.

Quora has erased my accounts forty-seven times.

What those erasures took was never the writing. Every post is in this repository and I can rewrite anything. What they took was people knowing where I am. Each time an account went, everybody following it lost the thread, and most of them never found their way back — not because they stopped caring, but because there was no route from where they were to where I went.

A feed cannot be taken away like that. There is no account to suspend, no address I hold, and no company standing between you and the list. It is a file on the blog, the same blog that carries the posts. Anybody who has it stays connected to this whether or not any platform still lets me exist on it.

What it will not do.

Most people do not use feed readers. They were common until Google closed the best one in 2013, and since then they have been a thing for people who already knew about them. If you have never installed one, you probably will not install one for a blog, and that is a reasonable decision.

It also does not reach you the way a message does. Nothing buzzes. You open your reader when you feel like it and the posts are sitting there, which some people much prefer and others will find useless.

So this is not the thing that solves being erased. It is one route that cannot be closed by anybody, sitting alongside the ones that can.

How to use it, if you want to.

You need a reader app — there are many, most are free, and they run on a phone. Any of them will do; nothing about this feed is particular.

In most of them you can paste this blog's address on its own and the app will find the feed by itself. If yours asks for the feed address specifically, it is the one at the top of this post. There is also a "Follow by RSS" link in the footer of every page here.

The feed carries the newest fifty posts across everything published here — the posts, the product updates, the guides, the archive. It is rebuilt every time this blog publishes, so it is never behind. When I bring in old material from the erased accounts it is filed under the date it was originally written, which means it lands where it belongs in the record instead of arriving in your reader as fifty new things.

Why not an email list.

Several people have asked, and the honest answer is that email is the thing most people use and also the thing most easily taken from me.

A mailing list lives or dies on whether the large mail providers think you are wanted. That judgment runs mostly on how many recipients mark a message as unwanted, and the threshold is low enough that a small number of people acting together can cross it for everybody. Once crossed, every subscriber stops getting mail, including the ones who asked for it. The service sending it removes the account and the list goes with it — which is the same shape as everything above, with a different company's name on it.

That is not a reason to never do it. It is a reason to build the thing that cannot be taken first, and to design the fragile one so that losing it costs me a send rather than the people.

If you use a reader already, tell me which one — I would rather recommend something a person here actually uses than something I read about. If you tried to follow me somewhere and lost me when the account went, that is worth writing down too.

How are you doing?

There is a thing I want to name, because once you can see it you cannot stop seeing it, and it explains a category of interaction that otherwise reads as confusing or even friendly.

Performed kindness. Kind words, said in a way that is not kind. The words are the cover and the delivery is the content.

What it looks like.

Somebody holds a door for me and then says something as I go through it. The door was never the point — walking close enough to say the thing was. If I had opened it myself there would have been no opening to speak into.

Somebody walks past a woman and tells her she should smile more. Men stand around with the same face on and are never told. It is said to women, it is framed as encouragement, and it is an instruction from a stranger about what her face should be doing.

In a public laundry room, somebody took issue with me for moving a stranger's washing out of a machine and not putting it in a dryer. Every washer was full. Nobody is supposed to leave clothes sitting in a public laundry for an extended period of time, and when they do, the clothes get moved — that is how the room works, and moving them is not taking them. I did not put the load in a dryer on purpose. You do not run a dryer on somebody else's clothes unless you know them and know how they do their washing. Heat shrinks things. Some of it should be on a lower setting and some of it should not go in at all.

So the complaint was that I had failed to do the thing that would have wrecked a stranger's clothes.

That is the shape of all of it. Saying something costs nothing. It buys the appearance of being a good person, by their definition, and it usually comes with somebody watching.

And then the question.

Which brings me to the one in the title.

Their raw material is your pain. Not your work, not your time, not anything you produce. What they are collecting is how badly you are doing, and they collect it by asking.

So when the question arrives from that direction, it is not concern. It is a meter reading. A bad answer is a good day for them.

How to run a one percent session.

In the last post I said what I can offer, beyond building the app: sitting with Targeted Individuals over their own figures.

There is an obvious problem with that offer. There is one of me.

So here is the whole method, written down. Run it on yourself. Run it for somebody else. You do not need me in the room and you do not need anything from the app to do it — the app makes one step faster, and that is all.

What it is.

Eight questions, in order. They turn a list of what you can do into a figure, and then turn the figure into the first thing to do about it.

It takes about an hour. Most of that is the last three questions; the first five go quicker than you expect.

Before you start.

If you are doing this for somebody else, read their listing or ask them what they can do, and nothing else. Do not go researching a person before you sit down with them. Whatever turns up shapes how you hear them and they did not offer it.

If you are doing it alone, write your answers down as you go. This does not work in your head. The figure in particular has to be seen.

Two things to say first, if it is for somebody else.

Both before anything else, because both change what a person feels able to say.

Nothing about their standing changes. Not because of what they say, not because they end it early, not because they say no to the whole thing. If you have any power over them at all — you run the group, you made the list, you know people — say this out loud rather than assuming it is understood.

Notes are not publication. If you are writing anything down, say so, and say that writing it down is not permission to repeat it. That is a separate question, asked separately, later, and never in the same conversation as arranging help.

The eight.

1. What do you need?

Not how are you doing. I have written about why — the short version is that one of those questions has an answer somebody can act on and the other collects something.

Take the answer as given. Do not fix it yet.

2. Is the list of what you can do right?

Read their skills back to them, or read your own. What is missing, what is wrong, what would come off.

Most people have never checked. If the list was built for somebody rather than by them, this is often the first time anybody has read it to them, and the corrections are usually substantial.

3. What would you charge one person, once?

Their rate. Not a market rate they read somewhere, not what they think they are allowed to ask, and not an aspiration.

What would they actually ask a real person for the thing they actually do. If they have never charged for it, ask what would make it worth the trouble.

This question stalls people. Let it. The stall is information.

4. Run the arithmetic in front of them.

One percent of five million survivors is fifty thousand people. Multiply.

Then say what the number is and what it is not. It is a target with their own rate underneath it. It is not a forecast, it is not income, and nobody is owed it.

The comparison worth giving: the app's Workforce screen splits a whole economy evenly and lands near $142,500 a person. No real outcome is an even split. Doing well comes out of three parts — talent, luck, and how much work goes in — and none of the three is evenly distributed. The average has all of it averaged away, which is why nobody lands on it.

If they have an account, the What's your 1%? tab does this multiplication for them off their listing. If they do not, a calculator does the same job.

5. Who is the first one?

Not the fifty thousandth. The first person who could pay that rate for that thing.

A name if they have one. A kind of person if they do not.

6. What stands between you and that one?

Let it be concrete. The answer is almost always material: no transport, no address, no tools, no room to work in, no way to be found by anybody looking.

Write the specific obstacle, not the category. "Transport" is a category. "No way to get to a client across town on a Tuesday morning" is an obstacle, and an obstacle has things you can do about it.

7. What can be arranged about that specific thing?

This is where a tool is a tool. The Directory makes somebody findable. Foundation is where an exchange gets offered or asked for. SkillsHunt finds a person with the trade that is missing. SkillUp teaches one or learns one.

If none of that touches the obstacle, say so plainly. Reaching for a tool that does not fit is worse than admitting the gap, and the gap is worth knowing.

8. One next action, with a name against it.

One. Not five.

Who does it, and roughly when. A session that ends in five good ideas ends in nothing, and everybody involved knows it by the following week.

Afterwards.

Write it down the same day if you are helping somebody. Memory rewrites a conversation inside twenty-four hours and the figures go first.

Then do the thing you said you would do. The conversation was not the work.

What this is not.

Not therapy. It is not a place to take a detailed account of what has been done to somebody. If that is what is needed, it is a different conversation with a different person, and this one should say so rather than absorbing it.

Not a sales call. Nobody has to join anything, use the app, or agree with a word I have written for the hour to be worth their time. Every question above works with a pen and a calculator.

Not a promise. The figure is arithmetic on an estimate. Saying that is part of the session, not a disclaimer at the end of it.

Why it is written down rather than offered.

Because one person cannot run five hundred thousand sessions, and a method that only exists inside somebody's head reaches exactly as far as that person's calendar.

It is also the honest answer to anybody who suspects this is a funnel. Here is the entire thing.

They cannot be ignored.

There is a well-known line about this shape. Martin Niemöller, a German pastor, said it after the Second World War, and the wording varies from telling to telling: first they came for one group, and I said nothing, because I was not one of them. Then they came for another. Then they came for me, and there was no one left to speak. I learned it at school when we studied the war, and it is the same thing.

Thirty-four years of ignoring them.

I am going to use myself as the example, on purpose. I do not want this to read as though ignoring is a failing that the person on Quora has and I do not.

I ignored them from age five to thirty-nine. That is a long stretch, and none of it changed anything.

Some people had COVID with no symptoms. They got to ignore it. It still had to be dealt with, because it was landing on everybody else, and the same is true here. It is my belief that as Targeted Individuals we do not, one at a time, have the tools to not ignore them properly. I did not. So I built the thing that gives those tools to all of us at once.

My threat model is physical safety. For me, living among like-minded people, with a gate, is coexistence — it is my way of not ignoring them. A lot of people hear a closed community and think isolation. It is not isolation. It is survival, and it is living with people who understand. I do not believe in integrating with their economy. It is counterintuitive, because a barrier looks like looking away. It is the opposite. It is full recognition of the problem, followed by something done about it.

The other threat is destitution. They take your employment. They have decided your labor is worth nothing to them, and the harsh reality is that your skills are extremely valuable. Two threats, two answers, and neither of them is ignoring anything.

So the honest answer to the first half is no. The second half has a better answer than it looks, once the question is asked about the right thing.

What they are after is not the harassment for its own sake. Your record. A dated log you own means your account of your life stops depending on memory under stress. ClickLog is that: date, a location if you choose one, fifteen seconds. Your day. The theatre is built to be studied, and every hour spent mapping it is an hour they took. I spent nine months on that and the turn is dated on the record. What replaces it is something that compounds, that is still there next month whatever they did this week.

The targeting is real. It is not the most interesting fact about you.

That is not an accident of the methods; it is the requirement they are built to meet. It is also why the reasonable next step — tell somebody — costs a survivor more than staying quiet, because the account of it sounds worse than the events.

The skits built from information only the target should know are the ones that make people think these people are reading minds. They are not — she says it in the same sentence, and says where it comes from: surveillance.

The sorting does not stop at a family either. The aim is that everybody ends up on one side or the other, a trafficker or a target, and that the traffickers outnumber the targets. That is how every trafficking ring and every psyop has been run.

Somebody who fits neither box is a problem for that arrangement. Two people I knew would not take part, and proved hard to turn into targets. They were killed.

It is not a remedy, and she was not offering one. No method I know of stops this, mine included.

What a description is good for is smaller and real. It tells a person who has been told they are imagining it that the thing has a shape, a name, and other people who recognize it on sight. Every survivor I have met remembers the first accurate description they read.

After that, the thing to do is not to study it further. It is to move what you can out of reach: a ride, a room, an hour of a trade, arranged with somebody who is not part of it. That is what this economy is, and why it is an economy rather than a support group.

No one to traffic.

I have written that nobody who joins owes me anything, and that I owe nobody either. That still stands, and this post is what follows from building the thing anyway.

What the network runs on.

It needs people who are destitute and alone.

Destitution is what makes somebody cheap to reach. No money means one route, one meal you can afford, one person who will still rent to you, and every one of those is a place to put somebody who is taking part. Isolation is what makes the reaching stick, because a person with four people left in their life can be predicted and can be cut off from all four.

Neither of those is a side effect. They are produced, on purpose, because they are the working conditions.

So an economy where survivors are not destitute and not alone is not a comfort. It is a supply problem for them.

The part that reaches past us.

I have written about the three places this road ends, and about what I expect if a fourth route exists, in Something to lose. I will not restate the argument; the short version is that people driven to those endings do not only harm themselves, and fewer of them is a benefit to everybody standing nearby, targeted or not.

I cannot prove that in advance, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. It is what I expect, and it is one of the reasons I am doing this rather than something easier.

Notice what it is not. It is not a favor to anybody, and nothing is being asked in return. A person who has never been targeted gets the benefit without joining, without agreeing with me, and without knowing this exists.

And the other side of it.

This is the part I have not written down before.

The people doing this are not all enjoying it. Some are. The rest are in because of what the structure already gave them — the job, the apartment, the promotion, the thing they took before they understood what it was for — and because walking away would cost them those things. That is the arrangement I described in the earlier post: the favors come first, and once you have them the terms are plain.

I want to be exact about what that is, because it is a reason and not a justification. Losing a home is traumatic to the person it happens to, and I am not going to tell anybody otherwise. But none of their lives are on the line. What is at stake for them is property and their own good opinion of themselves. What is at stake on the other side is somebody's life.

That difference is not incidental. Across these networks there is an understanding that a target can be robbed, injured or killed and nobody will answer for it, and that the people who did it will be rewarded instead. No such cover extends to them. Ordinary crime still applies to a person who is not a target, which is why what they risk is losing things and never losing their life.

Nobody in that position can simply walk away, because the debt is held over them and there is always another target to be assigned.

Unless there is not.

If this works, there is no one to traffic. Not fewer, not harder — none, because the material a person needs to be worth trafficking is what the economy takes away. A trafficker with no target has nothing to trade on and nothing being asked of them. The people who never wanted to do it stop having to.

What that is and is not.

It is not forgiveness, and it is not a deal. I said in the earlier post that following orders is not better than enjoying it, because the order is the same debt being collected one level up, and I have not changed my mind. Somebody who trafficked a person under pressure still did it.

What it is: the exit existing at all. Right now it does not. Every route out of that network runs back through the network, which is the same trap from the other side, and the people held there have as much reason as anybody to want this to succeed.

That is the argument, and it does not need anybody to be grateful for it. I am not owed. Neither is anybody else here. The work is worth doing on what it produces, which is fewer people with nothing left to lose, on both sides of the thing.

No.

That is the short answer, and the rest of this is about why it looks like something else, because the appearance is real and worth taking apart rather than waving off.

They watch, around the clock, in both places.

The surveillance runs all day and it runs in two places at once: on foot, and online. Neither half is exotic. A person on a street can be followed by people who take shifts. A person on a phone or a laptop can be read by anybody with access to the accounts, the network, or somebody who works where the data sits.

Do that for years and you do not need a sixth sense. You need notes. I have written about what that produces in Pizza is not my favorite food: they watched what I bought when I had almost no money, saw the same cheap hot meal over and over, and concluded it was my favorite food. They were wrong, and the way they were wrong is the proof. Somebody reading a mind would have known it was a budget. Somebody reading behavior guesses the reason and gets it backwards.

That post also has the other half of how the accuracy gets built: people work at it full time, the same operatives run several targets at once, patterns learned from one person get applied to the next, and they watch each other too.

They arrange the thing they appear to have foreseen.

The second reason is the one that gets missed, and it is the larger of the two.

A prediction is impressive when the person making it has no hand in the outcome. Take that condition away and it is not a prediction at all. A canceled ticket, a job that evaporates, a landlord who suddenly will not renew, a delay that makes you miss the thing you were going to — those are arranged, and somebody who arranges an event knows how it ends.

One bus trip of mine ran at least seven of those schemes in sequence, and one of them canceled my ticket while I was sitting on the bus it was for. Anybody with a hand in that could have told you in advance where I would be standing that night. It is not foresight. It is scheduling.

The plainest version of it is provocation. Somebody runs up and punches me in the face, and afterward they knew I would react. That is not a reading of me. It is an event built to produce the reaction it then claims to have foreseen.

And the reaction gets used twice, which is the part to watch for. Once to look prescient. Once as evidence for the story they were already telling about me: see, I told you she is violent, I told you she threatens people. The same staged moment does both jobs, and you cannot argue a smear down once it is running.

Then they tell you about it, before or after, because the telling is the point. They are not a secret society — they advertise themselves constantly, and an arranged event announced in advance is the cheapest way to look like they know everything.

Poverty narrows a life, and so does isolation.

The third reason is the one that compounds, and it is the one I would want a survivor to understand best.

Take away somebody's income and their choices go with it. One hot meal they can afford. One route they can walk. One person who will still rent to them. A life with the options stripped out is a life that can be called in advance by anybody looking, because there is only one thing left to do at each turn.

That is what the Forced Homecoming produces on purpose: a person destitute enough that the only door left leads back to relatives who can be reached, in a place where everybody around them is taking part. Surrounded by operators, with no money and nowhere else to be, a person becomes extremely easy to predict — and every scheme run on them afterward gets cheaper, because the operative can be told in advance where to stand.

Destitution is not the only way that narrowing happens. A self-identified Targeted Individual, in a comment under a different question on Quora, said in substance that they were fortunate: they had progressed far enough in their profession that holding a job, and being fulfilled in it, was achieved. That is my paraphrase rather than their words. The knock-on effect is mine to add rather than theirs — destitution at the traffickers' hands never arrived for them. Destitution can be dodged. Isolation is not dodged by anybody I have met or read about. It arrives whether or not the income holds, because turning the people around somebody is what the slander is for, and it is run on everybody.

So isolation does the same work by a different route. When the people around somebody have been turned, or were placed there to begin with, their week runs through a short list of known names — who drives them, who they eat with, who they tell things to. A person with an income and four people left in their life is not hard to call in advance either.

And it compounds with the staging, which is where the two halves meet. The chaos around a person is arranged. The reaction to an arranged event is the obvious one. The few people left to turn to afterward were selected. Predict the event, predict the reaction, predict who gets called — three guesses, and not one of them is a thought being read.

Isolation is the aim rather than a side effect, and being easy to predict is one of the things it buys them.

So the accuracy people take for mind reading is mostly this. Years of watching, plus events they staged themselves, plus a life narrowed by poverty or by isolation until it has one obvious next move.

Why the psychic story suits them.

It is worth asking who benefits from the other explanation.

If they are psychic then they are unbeatable, and there is nothing to do but endure it and try to interpret it. That is exactly the position all of this is designed to put a person in. The same holds for every version of the story that lifts them out of the human category — a bloodline, an ancient order, something with God behind it. Those are the most flattering accounts of themselves available, and they do not have to circulate them, because survivors do it for them.

What they actually have is money, technology, a network, and the willingness to commit crimes. Those are real advantages. None of them is insight, and calling them insight does part of their work for free.

What to do with this.

Two things, and the second is the one that changes anything.

Write down what happens, dated, as it happens. ClickLog takes about fifteen seconds an incident and your notes stay yours. A record is what lets you see the pattern afterward, including the times they got you wrong, which is the part that shows where the limit is.

And work on the options, because options are material rather than a mindset. Somewhere to cook. An hour of a trade you do not have. A ride from somebody who is not reporting on it. Money that does not come through anyone doing this to you. Every one of those is a move they cannot call in advance, and every one of them is what this economy is for.

That is not a defense against being watched. Nothing is. It is a way of being harder to predict, and worth less to watch.

Income. Not the career that was taken — that one usually does not come back the way it was, and people who wait for it lose years. What comes back is the ability to earn, which is not the same thing. Show up with your percent.

Most of a Targeted Individual's day is not theirs. It goes to the attacks and to what they force: moving again, watching the people around you, sleeping badly or not at all, and doing ordinary things that have been made slow on purpose. What is left at the end of it might be five percent. Some days it is nothing.

Five percent is not enough to reach a goal alone. A job in another state, a vehicle you can live in, a move that holds: each one is a chain of small steps, research and calls and follow-ups, done in order over weeks. The attacks land on the chain. A step gets missed, the order breaks, and it starts over. Anybody living this knows the shape of it, and knows that telling somebody in it to try harder is telling them to have a day they do not have.

The arithmetic changes when the five percent is not all one person's. Twenty members with five percent of a day each is a working day. Nobody in it had more time than they had. That is what the goal board runs on.

What it is.

PeerProgramming now opens on a Goals tab, and the tab is a board.

You post one goal with a finish line. You break it into cards: small tasks somebody else could do from a phone in under half an hour. Find three yards hiring near Dallas and add their numbers. Find three campers under six thousand dollars within two hundred miles of Tulsa. Ask one dealer whether they finance with no credit check.

The cards sit in three columns: Up for grabs, Doing, Done. You pick any one card, from anybody's goal. You do it, you post what you found, and it moves to Done with your name on it. The goal's owner reads the result and marks it "It helped", keeps it, or sends it back for somebody else. That is the entire loop.

There is no conversation on the board. A goal, its cards, and their results, and nothing else can be written there. The weekly sessions and the call are still in PeerProgramming.

Why it is shaped this way.

I have tried group chats for this three ways and none of them held. Who shows up on a day depends on how hard that day was, so the chat is a different room every time. It slides from doing into comparing notes, which is talk about the attacks, and the attacks already have most of the day. And on an open chat there is no filter: anybody put out can come back under a new name the same evening.

The board sits inside the app, behind Unlock, so taking part needs an approved account, and an approved account is not something you can rejoin under a new name. A card is one task with one result, so there is nothing to slide into. And a card asks for one small thing, once, so a bad day costs nothing: you take a card when you have the half hour, and you do not when you do not. Nobody is waiting on you to appear.

What counts.

When the goal's owner marks a card "It helped", it counts. It goes toward the day's 384, the count of members who delivered something to another member on one day, and toward the helper's Weavers of the Commons badge. Both are defined on the Dictionary. A card kept without that mark, or sent back, does not count, and nobody but the goal's owner and the helper sees which it was. A finished card is a finished card to the rest of the cohort.

In what I have seen, both: parents, and strangers.

Parents.

Where the parents take part, their children do too. I have not seen an exception. I have watched parents bring children into it as young as two.

A child that age is not choosing anything. They are doing what the adults around them do and are rewarded for, the way a child learns anything else at home.

Strangers.

It does not run the other way. Some children take part while one or both of their parents do not, and do not know. Those children are taught by strangers.

The recruiting runs on slander. Somebody is told a story about the target, is swayed by it, and is offered something for acting on it: sex, drugs, a job. My targeting started at five, and it was adults first. Then the children.

The targeting ran through school from there. What about school? is about that.

A teenager who knows a lot has usually been taking part for years. What looks like knowledge at sixteen is a habit that started before they could have understood it.

I do not know of a local office that takes a report of the entire pattern and acts on it. I would rather say that plainly than send anybody to a door that does not open.

That is not the end of the answer. It splits into four smaller ones.

What a report can carry.

A police report records an incident. A break-in, damage to a car, a threat, somebody following you onto private property: each of those is a crime with a name, and it is reported as what it is. I have called the police on individual incidents, and gone to a station over one.

What a single report was not built to hold is the pattern: many people, many days, each act small enough to explain away on its own. That is the part that makes this Specterati harassment rather than a run of bad luck, and it is also the part a report form has no box for.

None of this is legal advice, and it does not tell anybody what to do when a report goes nowhere. It is what a report is.

Where the pattern can be reported.

The United Nations collects reports of this, because it is a human rights violation and it happens in every country rather than one. The Special Rapporteur on Torture, who reports to the UN Human Rights Council, takes submissions from individuals. The Rapporteur's 2020 report to the Council (A/HRC/43/49) addressed coordinated harassment as a category and said the volume of allegations warrants investigation rather than dismissal.

A report there does not bring anybody to your door. It adds to the record that report was written from, and that record is also where my estimate of five million survivors worldwide begins.

Keep your own record first.

Whoever you end up telling, what makes it possible to tell anybody is a record: dated, written as it happens, not reconstructed a year later from memory.

ClickLog takes about fifteen seconds an incident. Your notes are not shown to anyone else here, and an incident you do not tag needs no location. If you choose to tag it, it joins a trend report that shows where things are happening across everybody who logs, which is a pattern no one person can see from inside their own week. ClickLog: your record, and the record says more about both uses.

A record is for you before it is for anybody else. It lets you see the pattern yourself without needing somebody else to believe it first.

The question underneath.

Often "who do I report this to" means "who can help me". That one has a better answer, and it is people.

Being believed is a start, and the Commons is a group chat of other Targeted Individuals. The help that changes a week is material: a ride from somebody who is not reporting on it, a room, an hour of a trade you do not have. The Directory lists people and what they can do, and Foundation is where members offer and exchange that help.

None of that stops it on its own. It does mean not carrying it alone.
